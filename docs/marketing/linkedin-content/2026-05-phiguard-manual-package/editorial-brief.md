# PHIGuard LinkedIn Editorial Brief

This package is prepared content only. Do not call Postiz, schedule posts, upload media, or touch any live queue.

## Audience

Write for practice administrators, office managers, privacy officers, and owners at small medical clinics with roughly 3-50 staff. They are busy, pragmatic, budget-aware, and often responsible for HIPAA operations without a full compliance department.

## Product Positioning

PHIGuard is HIPAA-native task management and compliance operations software for small clinics. The core contrast is not "more project management." It is clinic compliance work with BAA details checked against source material, PHI-aware task handling, audit history, vendor tracking, training records, incident work, policy review, risk work, and pricing details from PHIGuard source material.

Current traction context:

- PHIGuard does not have clients or customer proof yet.
- The product was built by an engineer/founder who saw a market gap: small clinics need HIPAA-aware compliance operations and task accountability, but many available options are generic project management tools or broader compliance/GRC systems that do not fit the small-clinic operating reality.
- Do not imply adoption, usage, revenue, testimonials, case studies, waitlist size, or customer outcomes.
- Use product-thesis language such as "built for," "designed to," and "positioned around" instead of customer-results language.

Allowed positioning:

- Built for small clinic HIPAA operations.
- Use the current BAA copy from `packages/knowledge/src/commercial.ts`.
- Flat per-clinic pricing, not per-seat expansion.
- Designed to help clinics assign, document, and retain compliance work.
- Strong fit for patient-adjacent compliance tasks that do not belong in generic tools.

Banned claims:

- Do not say PHIGuard makes a clinic "HIPAA compliant."
- Do not say "certified HIPAA compliant."
- Do not fabricate customer counts, testimonials, revenue, adoption, or results.
- Do not imply PHIGuard has clients, customers, case studies, production deployments, or market validation.
- Do not give legal advice.
- Do not state current competitor pricing/BAA availability unless the specific source file says it and the post phrases it cautiously.
- Do not imply a BAA alone makes any workflow safe.

## Voice

Practical, calm, precise, human. Use healthcare operations language: BAA, PHI, audit trail, covered entity, business associate, access review, training record, risk analysis, incident response. Avoid generic SaaS words like streamline, unlock, transform, optimize, seamless, game-changing, revolutionary, powerful platform, supercharge, and workflow magic.

The best posts should feel like a sharp operator explaining what actually goes wrong in clinics and how to make the work provable.

## Post Shape

Each post must stand alone on LinkedIn and should usually be 800-1,800 characters. Use paragraph breaks. No hashtags unless a writer has a strong reason. No emojis. Text-only.

Every post needs:

- A distinct hook.
- One clear argument.
- A useful operational takeaway.
- A traceable source path or PHIGuard URL.
- A CTA type: `discussion`, `checklist`, `soft_product`, `resource_link`, or `none`.
- Review status: `approved`, `needs_review`, or `rejected_rewritten`.

## Required Daily Mix

Each 15-post day should cover all seven pillars, with no pillar used fewer than two times:

- HIPAA basics
- PHI-safe task management
- BAA/vendor management
- Risk analysis and audit readiness
- Workforce training and access control
- Incident response
- Generic tool alternatives/comparisons

## Source Hierarchy

Prefer the source collections under `apps/marketing/src/content/`:

- `learn/` for educational HIPAA operations.
- `resources/` for templates and checklist posts.
- `alternatives/` and `comparisons/` for generic-tool positioning.
- `practice-types/` for specialty-specific clinic examples.
- `guides/` and `best/` for vendor/tool evaluation posts.

Use `CLAUDE.md`, `docs/marketing/seo-strategy.md`, and `apps/marketing/src/data/product.ts` for product positioning only.

## Quality Gate Checklist

Before a post is marked approved, confirm:

- The hook is not repeated from another post in the same day.
- The post does not read like a template.
- The reader learns something practical.
- The claim is traceable to the source.
- The language is not legal advice.
- The post contains no PHI or patient-specific invented scenario.
- The post avoids fake proof, exaggerated promises, and unsupported competitor claims.
- The CTA fits the argument.
