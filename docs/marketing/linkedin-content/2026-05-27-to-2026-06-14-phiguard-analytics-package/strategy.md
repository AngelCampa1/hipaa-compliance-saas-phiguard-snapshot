## Strategic Direction

This run keeps the prior package's manual, operator-led quality bar while shifting from broad coverage toward analytics-informed repetition control.

- Lead with operational usefulness, not product claims.
- Use PHIGuard as the named operating model when the source supports product positioning.
- Keep product CTAs soft and sparse.
- Emphasize the gap between generic work tracking and HIPAA operating records.
- Reuse high-quality source clusters, not exact hooks or phrasing from the prior package.
- Rotate concrete clinic roles: office manager, privacy officer, practice administrator, owner, security officer, billing lead, front desk manager, and multi-location operator.

## Recommended Source Cluster Mix

Initial mix for 285 posts:

- Resources and templates: heavy use for checklist, worksheet, tracker, and audit-readiness posts.
- Learn/compliance operations: heavy use for HIPAA basics, recurring tasks, policy review, annual review, access control, risk analysis, incident response, and workforce training.
- Alternatives and comparisons: steady use for generic tool alternatives, but avoid turning every comparison into a product pitch.
- Practice types: use for specificity and variety, especially dental, primary care, pediatrics, OB/GYN, dermatology, cardiology, orthopedics, mental health, chiropractic, and physical therapy.
- Guides and best-of pages: use for vendor/tool evaluation angles, especially when discussing BAAs, PHI handling, analytics, AI tools, cloud storage, forms, transcription, phone systems, ticketing, and HR software.
- State and city guides: use selectively for state-law awareness and geographic specificity only when the post can stay careful and non-legal.

## Pillar Strategy

HIPAA basics:

- Make basics practical: roles, records, retention, minimum necessary, notices, and recurring review habits.
- Avoid generic "HIPAA is hard" framing.

PHI-safe task management:

- Focus on where PHI leaks into task titles, comments, attachments, reminders, screenshots, exports, and generic tools.
- Tie the takeaway to operating records and ownership.

BAA/vendor management:

- Treat BAA work as a living vendor inventory, not a folder of signed PDFs.
- Keep the caveat clear: a BAA is necessary for certain vendor relationships, but it does not make every workflow safe.

Risk analysis and audit readiness:

- Center evidence, review cadence, asset inventory, remediation owners, and follow-through.
- Avoid implying a one-time checklist equals compliance.

Workforce training and access control:

- Connect training, role changes, offboarding, access review, sanctions logs, and acknowledgements.
- Keep examples generic and non-PHI.

Incident response:

- Focus on triage, documentation, ownership, escalation, decision trees, and tabletop exercises.
- Avoid breach-notification legal conclusions unless source wording is directly checked.

Generic tool alternatives/comparisons:

- Compare operating fit, not feature lists.
- Use cautious phrasing around vendor capability, BAA availability, and current pricing.
- The central contrast: generic tools can track work, but clinic compliance work also needs PHI-aware boundaries, evidence, audit history, and accountable records.

## CTA Strategy

Use CTA types with restraint:

- `discussion`: best for operational questions that invite comments without bait.
- `checklist`: best for posts that give a concrete review step.
- `resource_link`: best when the source is a template, tracker, worksheet, or guide.
- `soft_product`: use sparingly, only when the post naturally maps to PHIGuard's product behavior.
- `none`: use when the post is stronger as a standalone point.

Avoid hard selling across consecutive posts. Product mentions should feel like the operating consequence of the argument, not the point of the post.

## Cadence Strategy

Use 15 daily slots across early morning, business hours, afternoon, and evening Central time until analytics gives better guidance.

Each day should include:

- At least two practical checklist/resource posts.
- At least two generic-tool or comparison posts.
- At least two BAA/vendor posts.
- At least two risk/audit posts.
- At least two workforce/access posts.
- At least two incident-response or HIPAA basics posts.
- One or more specialty/practice-type angles when the source supports specificity.

## Claims And Safety Strategy

The package must preserve these constraints:

- No fake client/customer proof.
- No adoption, usage, outcome, testimonial, case study, revenue, or waitlist claims.
- No PHIGuard "HIPAA-compliant" or certification claims.
- No legal advice.
- No PHI-like invented details.
- No programmatically generated posts.
- No unsupported competitor claims.

Use "designed to," "built for," and "positioned around" for PHIGuard claims. Use "a clinic should evaluate," "the source frames," and "the operating question is" for compliance education.

## Performance-Informed Direction

Analytics source: `analytics/performance-summary.md`, `analytics/top-posts.csv`, and `analytics/daily-metrics.csv`.

The prior package was reach-positive but interaction-light. The export shows 7,046 organic page impressions, 9 clicks, 19 reactions, 4 comments, 0 reposts, and 32 total engagement actions across the reported page-metrics window. Treat engagement signals as directional only. Do not claim proof of audience preference, demand, traction, product adoption, or downstream conversion.

Top reach came from concrete clinic operations topics, especially:

- Patient rights complaints as process gaps.
- Offboarding as a PHI-risk handoff.
- End-of-day close routines.
- Incident closure before the policy question is answered.
- Generic project-tool limits.
- Access reviews, risk findings, and specialty vendor review.

Top engagement was also small, but the strongest themes were specific small-clinic friction:

- Shared logins as an unsafe shortcut.
- Generic tools such as Asana, ClickUp, Trello, Notion, Airtable, and Monday.com being useful but incomplete for HIPAA operating records.
- Cleaning crew or vendor access as a HIPAA operations question.
- End-of-day routines.
- Dermatology vendor review tied to photos and pathology.

Use these findings to shift the first half of the package toward sharper operational corrections. Posts should open by naming a common shortcut, missing review, or false sense of completion, then move quickly to the practical record a clinic should maintain. Avoid broad "HIPAA education" intros unless the post immediately turns into an operating task.

Performance-informed source and pillar weighting:

- Increase patient rights, offboarding, incident response, access review, end-of-day routine, shared login, vendor/subprocessor review, and generic-tool limitation posts.
- Keep all seven required pillars represented each day, but let the best-performing topic families carry more of the hooks and examples.
- Use resources, learn/compliance operations, alternatives/comparisons, and practice-type pages as the primary source clusters for days 1-3.
- Use state/city content sparingly and only when the post stays careful, operational, and non-legal.

Hook principles to reuse:

- Challenge a shortcut: "Shared logins are not a small-clinic shortcut."
- Reframe an ordinary routine as compliance work: "The end-of-day close is a HIPAA routine."
- Name an unfinished process: "An incident should not close before..."
- Turn a complaint or review into a process gap.
- Contrast useful generic tools with missing HIPAA evidence, ownership, access boundaries, or audit history.

CTA guidance:

- Use more `checklist`, `resource_link`, and `discussion` CTAs for concrete operating moves.
- Use `soft_product` sparingly and only where PHIGuard naturally follows from the problem, such as audit history, access review, vendor tracking, incident work, or PHI-aware task handling.
- Because total click volume was only 9, include some click/save-oriented posts but do not over-optimize the package around clicks.

Timing guidance:

- The strongest reported view-date days were May 17, May 14, May 16, May 13, and May 15. The export does not isolate posting-hour performance, so do not infer exact winning times.
- Keep the 15-slot daily cadence spread across early morning, business hours, afternoon, and evening Central time.
- Put the most concrete operational-correction posts in the stronger business-hour and late-afternoon slots, then compare results after the next export.

Safety constraints remain unchanged:

- No fake proof, testimonials, customer stories, adoption claims, outcome claims, or revenue claims.
- No PHIGuard compliance, certification, or "makes you HIPAA compliant" claims.
- No legal advice or breach-notification conclusions.
- No PHI-like invented patient, appointment, diagnosis, billing, lab, or date-of-service details.
- No unsupported competitor claims or unsupported current pricing claims.

Drafting direction for days 1-3:

- Day 1 should lead with the clearest operational corrections: patient rights complaints as process gaps, offboarding/access review, end-of-day routine, incident closure, and shared logins. Keep product mentions minimal.
- Day 2 should expand the generic-tool and vendor/subprocessor angles: where project tools are useful, where they stop being enough, what a vendor inventory needs to record, and why a BAA alone does not make a workflow safe.
- Day 3 should reinforce the same winners through different clinic roles and source clusters: practice administrator, office manager, privacy officer, front desk manager, security officer, billing lead, and owner. Avoid repeating exact hooks from days 1-2.
- Across all three days, include the scarce-engagement caveat in planning notes: the data supports topic direction, not hard proof. Draft for operational usefulness first, then review performance after the next export.
