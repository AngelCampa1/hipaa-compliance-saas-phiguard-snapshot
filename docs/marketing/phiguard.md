## Executive summary

**Current status note (2026-05-19):** the historical draft Notice of Privacy
Practices risk called out in this audit has been mitigated. The unpublished

The highest-confidence finding is that PHIGuard has a **content-governance problem before it has a content-volume problem**. Current canonical pricing is maintained from `packages/billing/src/plans.ts` and generated `apps/marketing/public/pricing.txt`. At the same time, multiple search-visible PHIGuard pages have historically shown **legacy navigation**, **legacy pricing**, and even **broken URLs**. Remaining recommendations should be treated as content-refresh and recrawl work, not as proof that the old defects are still live.

From an opportunity standpoint, PHIGuard is well positioned for SEO if it leans into a very specific wedge: **HIPAA-native operational workflow management for small clinics**. The strongest defensible product angles already visible on the site are: **BAA details from commercial knowledge**, **append-only / immutable audit history**, **incident tracking**, **recurring compliance tasks**, **evidence capture**, **vendor / BAA tracking**, **training / policy / risk workflows**, and **pricing model from commercial knowledge**. Those claims align with real buyer friction in HIPAA operations and with official federal guidance emphasizing **risk analysis**, **security incident procedures**, **administrative, physical, and technical safeguards**, and defensible documentation. citeturn30view0turn13view0turn13view1turn25search0turn25search3turn25search4turn26search1

## SEO gap map

### BOFU gaps

PHIGuard already has the beginnings of BOFU architecture through pricing, compare pages, specialty pages, and best-of lists, but the coverage is uneven and overly dependent on unstable or stale routes. The most profitable missing BOFU areas are: **vendor comparison pages for compliance vendors**, **vendor-intent “Is [tool] HIPAA compliant?” pages**, **alternative pages for generic work-management tools**, and **specialty / persona software pages** tuned to small-clinic operators rather than health-system buyers. ### MOFU gaps

The biggest MOFU opportunity is to become the operating manual for small-clinic HIPAA work. Official federal guidance repeatedly emphasizes documented **risk analysis**, **incident procedures**, and the practical work of protecting ePHI; the current site has hubs around those topics, but not enough workflow-level content for the actual admin and compliance tasks buyers must complete. citeturn25search0turn25search3turn25search4turn26search1

The best missing MOFU topics are operational, not abstract: **access reviews, annual review workflows, BAA registers, training completion records, offboarding, sanctions logs, tabletop exercises, evidence binders, vendor review workflows, incident triage, OCR inquiry prep, risk-analysis scoping, asset inventory, and multi-location rollouts**. These pages are where PHIGuard can naturally connect federal requirements to the product’s task ownership, append-only history, recurring workflows, evidence capture, and vendor tracking. citeturn30view0turn13view0turn13view1turn25search3

- multiple “best HIPAA software / best HIPAA project management / best HIPAA task management” pages with overlapping buyer intent
- specialty pages that overlap with use-case pages
- comparison pages that overlap with alternative pages
- generic learn pages that compete with commercial pages if they use the same primary keyword.

The simplest fix is a **keyword-role model**:

- **BOFU:** vendor comparison, alternative, software-for-[persona/use case], pricing pages
- **MOFU:** how-to, workflow, checklist, documentation, implementation
- **TOFU:** definition, examples, edge cases, rule explainers
- **Lead magnet:** landing pages optimized for conversion, not ranking.

## Open questions and limitations

A few items remain open and should be validated directly in a technical crawl or CMS export.

The first limitation is that this session could not directly verify **robots.txt**, **sitemap.xml**, or page-level canonical tags via parsed source, so canonical recommendations above are based on URL behavior and duplicate renders, not on direct view-source confirmation.

The second limitation is that several hub-discovered or search-visible URLs returned **cache misses or fetch issues** rather than a stable page render. That likely indicates one of three things: pre-render instability, stale indexed URLs, or route-level issues. Those URLs need a direct crawl from a crawler such as Screaming Frog and a matching Search Console export.

The third limitation is SERP instrumentation. I was able to capture current ranking patterns and search-visible results, but not a full Ahrefs / SEMrush / Search Console data export. That means the roadmap is prioritized by **buyer-value and sourceable commercial fit**, not by third-party search-volume estimates.

The fourth limitation is vendor mutability. Every vendor-intent page that references pricing, BAA availability, eligible plans, AI data-use rules, integrations, or retention should be refreshed **immediately before publication** against the official source.
