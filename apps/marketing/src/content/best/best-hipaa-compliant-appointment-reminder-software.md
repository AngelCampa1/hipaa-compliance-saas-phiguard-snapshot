## How we evaluated appointment reminder software

Appointment reminders look harmless until you read what is in them. "Hi Sarah, see you at 9am tomorrow with Dr. Patel at Riverside Endocrinology" is PHI under HIPAA. That means the reminder tool, the SMS carrier, the voice provider, and any storage layer along the way are business associates and need a BAA chain.

We evaluated five reminder and patient communication tools plus PHIGuard. The reminder tools handle the operational side. PHIGuard handles the compliance program around them: BAAs, policies, training, audit trails.

## 1. PHIGuard — compliance and task layer for patient communications

PHIGuard is the compliance and task management layer for clinics that send patient communications. It does not send messages. It does track every vendor in the messaging stack — the reminder platform, the SMS carrier, the voice gateway — and stores executed BAAs with breach notification terms. It also stores the written policy on what content is allowed in reminders, training records showing the front desk has read it, and an append-only audit log.


## 2. Klara

Klara is a secure patient messaging and communications platform that includes appointment reminders, two-way messaging, intake forms, and EHR integrations. Klara signs a BAA with covered entities.

Pricing is quote-based and typically scales by provider count. Klara is a strong operational fit for clinics that want a unified patient communication tool. The compliance program around it — BAAs with carriers, internal policies, training — still needs a system of record.

## 3. Phreesia

Phreesia is a patient intake and engagement platform with reminders, registration, payments, and clinical screenings. Phreesia signs a BAA. Pricing is quote-based and tied to volume.

Phreesia is heavier than a reminder-only tool and is most often deployed by mid-size groups that want intake and payments alongside reminders.

## 4. Mend

Mend is a patient engagement and telehealth platform with appointment reminders, scheduling, and intake forms. It signs a BAA and is offered as a tiered subscription.

Mend is a reasonable single-vendor option for small practices that want reminders and telehealth in the same tool.

## 5. Solutionreach

Solutionreach is a patient relationship management platform with reminders, recall campaigns, surveys, and reputation management. It signs a BAA. Pricing is subscription-based.

Solutionreach overlaps significantly with marketing tools, so when evaluating it, confirm which features are inside the BAA scope and which are not.

## Pricing models compared

Reminder pricing models include:

- **Per provider per month.** Klara typically. Scales with hiring.
- **Quote-based by volume.** Phreesia. Tied to patient throughput.
- **Tiered subscription.** Mend, Solutionreach.
- **Published plan details for compliance.** PHIGuard. Independent of message volume.

A solo provider sending 200 reminders per month and a 5-provider group sending 4,000 have very different bills under volume-based pricing. The compliance program is roughly the same size for both.

## Buying decision framework — 5 questions

1. Does the reminder vendor sign a BAA, and is the SMS/voice carrier covered separately
2. What is the actual content of the reminder, and is it on the minimum necessary side of HIPAA
3. How does pricing change with patient volume or provider count
4. Where is your written reminder content policy stored, and who has acknowledged it
5. If a reminder is misdirected, is there a documented incident workflow

## FAQ

**Are appointment reminders considered PHI** If the reminder includes patient name, appointment time, location, or provider, it is PHI.

## Source Posture and Buying Criteria

Best HIPAA-Compliant Appointment Reminder Software should be evaluated with a conservative source posture: prefer official vendor documentation for BAA availability, HHS or eCFR pages for HIPAA obligations, and the clinic's own contract files for final proof. The source set for this page is HHS: HIPAA for Professionals. Treat those pages as starting points, then confirm plan names, BAA eligibility, and support commitments in the current contract before relying on the tool for PHI. Do not treat marketplace badges, sales copy, or generic security language as proof that a specific plan can receive PHI.

For small clinics, the best option is usually the product that reduces coordination burden while preserving evidence. Any reminder system that sends patient name, time, or provider is handling PHI. SMS and voice carriers used by reminder tools must be covered by a BAA chain. Per-message and per-provider pricing scales fast as a practice grows. Buyers should compare how each tool handles ownership, reminders, user access, audit history, exports, and offboarding rather than ranking products only by feature count.

A practical decision record should name the chosen tool, the approved PHI workflows, the signed BAA location, the owner for admin settings, and the review cadence. If a product is useful but not covered for PHI, document the permitted non-PHI use cases and train staff not to put patient names, appointment details, diagnoses, payment notes, or attachments into that system.
