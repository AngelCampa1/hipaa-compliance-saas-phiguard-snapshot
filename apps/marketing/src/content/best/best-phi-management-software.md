## Decision summary

PHI management software should keep patient-linked work from spreading across too many places. The best fit is not always the biggest compliance platform or the most flexible project management tool.

That distinction matters. PHI does not only live in the EHR. It appears in intake packets, fax follow-up, billing notes, referral coordination, vendor tickets, file shares, spreadsheet exports, patient messages, and internal tasks. A clinic can have a compliant EHR and still lose control of PHI in the tools around it.

Use this shortlist to answer one practical question: where does PHI-bearing work actually happen after it leaves the system of record

## What good PHI management software needs to control

| PHI workflow | What the software should make easier | Common failure mode |
|---|---|---|
| Task follow-up | Assign owners, due dates, status, evidence, and escalation | PHI appears in chat or task comments with no durable record |
| Document sharing | Limit access, track sharing, preserve retention, and reduce uncontrolled exports | Files move through personal drives, email attachments, or copied links |
| Vendor oversight | Track vendors, BAA status, review dates, and follow-up work | Vendor lists become stale and BAAs are hard to find |
| Intake and forms | Keep submitted data tied to a secure workflow and downstream owner | Forms collect PHI but handoff happens in inboxes |
| Incident handling | Preserve intake, triage, decisions, notification work, and closeout | Incident history is reconstructed from email after the fact |

The stronger product is usually the one that reduces handoffs. If a document tool stores files but every follow-up task happens somewhere else, the clinic still has a PHI management problem. If a task tool assigns work but cannot preserve evidence or control sensitive details, the clinic has the opposite problem.

## How to choose without buying another disconnected tool

Start with a workflow audit, not a software demo. Use the [PHI workflow audit worksheet](/resources/phi-workflow-audit-worksheet) to list where PHI appears today: email, fax, forms, spreadsheets, file shares, chat, task tools, vendor portals, and exports.

Then score each candidate on four questions:

- Can staff complete the workflow without copying PHI into an unmanaged place
- Can a manager see the owner, status, evidence, and next step
- Can the clinic restrict access to the people who need it
- Can the clinic explain later what happened and why

If the answer is no, the product may still be useful, but it is not enough by itself.

## Where PHIGuard fits

PHIGuard fits clinics that have outgrown shared drives, spreadsheets, and informal reminders. The product is strongest when the clinic needs to keep the operational record together: vendor follow-up, risk remediation, incident response, recurring reviews, and evidence attached to the work.

That is different from a pure file-sharing tool. It is also different from a broad productivity suite. PHIGuard is the layer for accountable PHI-related work that should not disappear into a chat thread or someone else's inbox.

## The practical recommendation

Do not buy PHI management software because it says "HIPAA" on the page. Buy it because it controls the exact places where your clinic loses visibility today.

For one clinic, that may be file sharing. For another, it may be vendor BAAs. For another, it may be incident follow-up or task comments. The best software is the one that makes the risky workaround unnecessary.
