## Decision summary

HIPAA-compliant transcription is not one market anymore.

Clinics now compare at least four different categories:

- medical dictation tools that turn clinician speech into EHR text
- ambient AI scribes that listen to encounters and draft structured notes
- meeting transcription tools for care coordination, compliance meetings, or staff communication
- general transcription or captioning services that process uploaded audio and video

Those categories have different risks. A dictation tool may never store a full encounter recording. An ambient AI scribe may capture a complete patient visit. A meeting agent may write searchable summaries and action items. A human transcription service may involve staff or subcontractors reviewing audio.

Before choosing a vendor, define the workflow. The right tool for provider dictation may be the wrong tool for compliance meeting transcripts.

## AI scribe vs transcription service vs dictation software

| Category | What it does | Main HIPAA review question |
|---|---|---|
| Dictation software | Turns a clinician's spoken words into text in an EHR or document field | Does the contract and deployment cover clinical speech data, adaptation data, and retention? |
| Ambient AI scribe | Records or listens to encounters and drafts structured clinical notes | What happens to encounter audio, transcripts, draft notes, patient consent, model improvement, and EHR handoff? |
| Meeting transcription | Records staff, care coordination, or compliance meetings | Does the Enterprise plan and BAA cover recordings, transcripts, summaries, action items, memories, and sharing? |
| General transcription service | Processes uploaded audio or video into transcripts or captions | Is the HIPAA subscription active, and are human services, public sharing, email delivery, and URL imports modified or removed? |

## What to verify before any PHI enters the tool

| Requirement | What to ask |
|---|---|
| BAA scope | Does the BAA cover the exact product, workspace, account, AI features, mobile app, and integration? |
| Raw-audio retention | Is audio stored, for how long, where, and can the clinic delete it? |
| Model training | Is PHI ever used to train, fine-tune, evaluate, or improve AI models? |
| Subprocessors | Which transcription, storage, AI, support, and analytics vendors receive data? |
| Human review | Are humans involved in transcription, quality review, support, or annotation? |
| Patient notice and consent | Does the clinic need consent or notice under state law, facility policy, or specialty rules? |
| Accuracy workflow | Does a clinician review and approve the note before it becomes part of the medical record? |
| EHR handoff | Does the transcript or note move through a covered integration, copy-paste, API, or manual upload? |
| Audit evidence | Can the clinic prove who approved the vendor and when the tool was used? |

The BAA matters, but it is not the whole review. A clinic also needs evidence that the tool's configuration matches the PHI workflow.

## Model training and de-identified data

Do not treat all "training" language the same. A vendor using identifiable PHI to train a general model is a different risk from a vendor using HIPAA de-identified or anonymized data for product improvement under contract controls.

The clinic should still ask for exact language. Confirm whether audio, transcript text, draft notes, prompts, corrections, feedback, or metadata are used for training, evaluation, safety review, or product improvement. Also confirm whether the vendor's definition of de-identification matches HIPAA de-identification standards or is only an internal anonymization process.

## Patient consent and recording policy

HIPAA is not the only rule that matters when a tool records clinical encounters. State recording laws, behavioral health rules, organization policy, payer requirements, and patient trust can all require more explicit notice or consent than the vendor's BAA covers.

Before enabling ambient AI or meeting transcription, write a short policy:

- which encounters may be recorded
- what patients are told before recording starts
- who can pause or stop recording
- how corrections are handled before the note enters the record
- how long raw audio and drafts are retained
- what staff should do if the wrong person is recorded

## What not to use

Avoid:

- consumer transcription apps with no signed BAA
- default meeting bots in non-enterprise workspaces
- non-Enterprise Otter for PHI
- ordinary Rev plans for PHI instead of the HIPAA subscription
- video-call transcription features unless the video platform BAA explicitly covers transcription, summaries, and recordings
- browser extensions or phone recorders that staff install outside the approved vendor process

## AI transcription risks clinics underestimate

**The raw audio may be more sensitive than the transcript.** It can contain names, diagnoses, medications, family details, behavioral health context, incidental staff comments, and third-party identifiers.

**The summary can create new risk.** AI summaries may omit qualifiers, overstate certainty, or transform patient language into clinical assertions. A clinician still needs to review the note before it enters the chart.

**Meeting tools are not medical scribes.** A meeting transcript of a compliance meeting can contain PHI, incident details, employee names, vendor issues, and patient complaints. Treat meeting agents as PHI processors when patient-specific content may be discussed.

**Human review changes the subcontractor question.** If a vendor uses human transcriptionists, QA reviewers, support staff, or offshore teams, the BAA and subcontractor controls need to account for that access.

**Consumer AI is the shadow-IT risk.** Staff may use a free recorder, phone app, browser extension, or generic AI note taker because the approved workflow is slow. That is a training and governance issue, not just a software issue.

## Demo questions

Ask each vendor:

- Will you sign a BAA for the exact product and plan we will use?
- Is HIPAA support self-serve, enterprise-only, annual-only, or sales-assisted?
- Do you store raw audio, and can the clinic set retention?
- Is PHI used for model training, fine-tuning, evaluation, or prompt improvement?
- Are humans or offshore teams involved in transcription, support, QA, or annotation?
- Which subprocessors handle audio, transcripts, summaries, or metadata?
- How does the clinician review and correct the final note?
- Does the tool create action items, summaries, or meeting memories outside the transcript?
- What happens if a recording includes the wrong patient or a third party?
- Can the clinic export audit evidence without exposing unnecessary PHI?

The best HIPAA-compliant transcription service is the one that gives the clinic a clear workflow from approval to capture to review to retention.
