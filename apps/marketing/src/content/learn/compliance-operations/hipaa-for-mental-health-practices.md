## Why mental health has unique HIPAA exposure

Mental health practices operate under HIPAA the way every covered entity does, but with additional layers most specialties never encounter. Psychotherapy notes, defined narrowly under the Privacy Rule, are kept separate from the rest of the designated record set and are subject to stricter authorization rules. The patient's right of access, broad in most contexts, is limited for these notes under 45 CFR § 164.524(a)(2)(i), and most disclosures require a separate authorization under § 164.508(a)(2).

Practices that provide substance use disorder treatment may also fall under 42 CFR Part 2, which is stricter than HIPAA on consent, redisclosure, and what counts as patient identifying information. Part 2 is not HIPAA, and a HIPAA-compliant disclosure may still violate Part 2.

The clinical setting itself creates exposures other specialties do not face. Group therapy, family sessions, and waiting-room dynamics all create incidental disclosure paths that must be controlled by reasonable safeguards rather than eliminated.

## Top HIPAA risks for mental health clinics

**1. Mishandled psychotherapy notes.** Practices that store psychotherapy notes inside the general EHR record, without separation, lose the heightened protection the Privacy Rule grants when notes are kept separate from the designated record set.

**2. Telehealth platforms without BAAs.** Consumer video tools and unsecured email used for therapy are recurring exposures. The platform must be a business associate.

**3. Part 2 missteps.** Practices that treat both mental health and substance use cases sometimes apply HIPAA-only consent forms to Part 2 records, which is not sufficient.

**4. Group therapy disclosure.** Sign-in sheets, intake conversations, and shared waiting rooms can disclose participation. Policies and training must address these.

## Vendor and BAA checklist for mental health

Confirm a signed BAA is on file for each of:

- Mental health EHR or general EHR used for behavioral records
- Telehealth and video session platforms
- Secure messaging and patient portal vendors
- Outcome measure and assessment tools
- Billing and clearinghouse vendors
- Transcription and dictation services, if used
- Cloud backup and managed IT vendors
- Group practice scheduling platforms

If a vendor will not sign a BAA, it cannot receive PHI. There is no consumer-grade exception for therapy.

## State law overlays affecting mental health

HIPAA is the federal floor. Many states impose stricter rules on mental health records, including separate consent for disclosure to family members, heightened protection for minors' records, and additional protections for reproductive health-related counseling and immigration-related records. Some states also have stricter rules on disclosures for criminal proceedings and on the involuntary commitment record trail. Confirm with counsel which overlays apply, and align authorization forms and disclosure logs to the stricter standard.

## HIPAA compliance checklist for mental health practices

1. Determine whether 42 CFR Part 2 applies to any service line, and if so, document the boundary clearly.
2. Store psychotherapy notes separately from the designated record set, with access limited to the treating clinician.
3. Use a separate, specific authorization form for psychotherapy note disclosures under § 164.508(a)(2).
4. Confirm a signed BAA is on file for every telehealth, EHR, billing, and messaging vendor.
5. Train clinicians on the limited right of access to psychotherapy notes under § 164.524(a)(2)(i).
6. Establish a written policy for group therapy intake, sign-in, and waiting-room handling that minimizes incidental disclosure.
7. Use a HIPAA-aware consent form that addresses state law overlays for minors, reproductive, and immigration-related records.
8. Configure role-based access so administrative staff cannot read clinical notes beyond minimum necessary.
9. Run an annual risk analysis that covers telehealth, Part 2 boundaries, and state overlays.
10. Maintain an incident response runbook that addresses both HIPAA and Part 2 breach notification, where applicable.
