Addiction medicine sits under a confidentiality regime that is materially stricter than HIPAA in several ways. If your practice diagnoses or treats substance use disorder and receives federal assistance, you operate under both HIPAA and 42 CFR Part 2. The 2024 Part 2 final rule narrowed the gap between the two but did not eliminate it, and a HIPAA-only compliance program will leave you exposed.

This guide is for practice administrators at office-based opioid treatment practices, methadone clinics, residential and outpatient SUD programs, and primary care practices that incorporate MAT.

## Why addiction medicine has unique HIPAA exposure

Substance use disorder records have been treated as a special category of health information since 1972 because Congress recognized that fear of disclosure deters people from seeking treatment. Part 2 enforces that policy with three structural rules that HIPAA does not impose:

1. Most disclosures require written patient consent that names the recipient, the purpose, and an expiration.
2. Recipients of Part 2 records cannot re-disclose them without further patient consent or another Part 2 basis.
3. Part 2 records are protected against use in civil, criminal, administrative, or legislative proceedings absent a court order that meets Part 2 standards.

The 2024 final rule allowed a single consent to authorize treatment, payment, and operations disclosures going forward, and aligned Part 2 breach notification with HIPAA. It did not collapse Part 2 into HIPAA.

## Top HIPAA risks for addiction medicine practices

The risks below appear repeatedly in OCR and SAMHSA findings.

- Sharing SUD treatment notes with a referring primary care provider under HIPAA treatment rules without obtaining Part 2 consent.
- Faxing or emailing methadone dosing records without confirming the recipient is authorized under the consent on file.
- Releasing records in response to a subpoena that does not meet Part 2's court-order requirements.
- Failing to include the required Part 2 notice prohibiting re-disclosure on every disclosure of SUD records.
- PDMP query data being co-mingled with Part 2 records in the EHR without separation controls.
- Telehealth platforms used for SUD counseling without a BAA and without Part 2-compliant features.
- Care coordination tools that aggregate records from Part 2 programs alongside non-Part 2 records.
- Staff treating Part 2 consent as interchangeable with a HIPAA authorization.

## Vendor and BAA checklist for addiction medicine

Addiction medicine practices need BAAs that explicitly address Part 2 obligations where applicable. Confirm coverage for each:

- EHR vendor with Part 2 segmentation features.
- Telehealth platform used for counseling and MAT visits.
- Lab vendor handling toxicology results.
- PDMP integration vendor.
- Pharmacy or dispensing system for methadone and buprenorphine.
- Care coordination platform if records flow to or from non-Part 2 providers.
- Billing and revenue cycle vendor.
- Secure messaging platform for clinical communication.
- Records release fulfillment service.
- IT managed service provider with EHR access.

For Part 2 records, the vendor must agree to be bound by Part 2 to the extent it would be applicable if the vendor were a Part 2 program.

## State law overlays affecting addiction medicine

State law adds another layer. Several states have their own SUD confidentiality statutes that are stricter than Part 2 in narrow areas, particularly around minors and around law enforcement access. PDMP rules are entirely a creature of state law: who must query, who may query, retention of query records, and patient access vary widely. Some states require separate written consent for HIV-related information that may appear in SUD records. Buprenorphine and methadone prescribing rules under state medical and pharmacy boards add additional documentation requirements that intersect with confidentiality.

If your practice operates across state lines via telehealth, you are subject to the patient-state confidentiality rules.

## HIPAA compliance checklist for addiction medicine

1. Determine in writing whether your practice is a 42 CFR Part 2 program and document the analysis.
2. Adopt a Part 2-compliant consent form that meets the elements in § 2.31, including the new TPO option from the 2024 rule if you intend to rely on it.
3. Apply the required Part 2 notice prohibiting re-disclosure to every disclosure of SUD records.
4. Train every staff member on the difference between HIPAA authorization and Part 2 consent.
5. Configure your EHR to segment Part 2 records and to require consent verification before disclosure.
6. Maintain a log of all Part 2 disclosures, including the consent on file that authorized each one.
7. Establish a procedure for handling subpoenas and other legal process that recognizes Part 2's court-order standard.
8. Sign Part 2-compliant BAAs (or qualified service organization agreements) with every vendor that handles SUD records.
9. Document PDMP queries and configure access so PDMP data is treated according to your state's rules.
10. Update your breach response runbook to address both HIPAA and the 2024 Part 2 breach notification requirements.
