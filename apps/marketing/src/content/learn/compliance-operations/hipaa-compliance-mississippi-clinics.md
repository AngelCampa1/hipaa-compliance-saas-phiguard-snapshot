Mississippi medical clinics operate under HIPAA as the primary governing framework for health information privacy and security. Unlike states with comprehensive state health privacy laws that exceed HIPAA in multiple areas, Mississippi's state-specific requirements concentrate in two areas: a 45-day breach notification deadline under Mississippi Code Ann. Section 75-24-29 and a 10-year medical records retention period for hospitals under MS Code Section 41-9-107. For most Mississippi clinics, a well-implemented HIPAA compliance program that accounts for these two state-law differences will satisfy both federal and state obligations.

## HIPAA Baseline Requirements

Every Mississippi clinic that transmits health information electronically in connection with covered transactions is a HIPAA-covered entity subject to the full federal compliance framework:

- A documented risk analysis and risk management program under 45 CFR Section  164.308(a)(1)
- Administrative, physical, and technical safeguards under 45 CFR Sections  164.308-164.316
- Business associate agreements with every vendor or contractor handling PHI, per 45 CFR Section  164.502(e)
- A Notice of Privacy Practices provided to patients at their first service encounter
- Workforce training on privacy and security policies under 45 CFR Section  164.530(b)
- Breach notification procedures under the Breach Notification Rule at 45 CFR Part 164, Subpart D

HIPAA gives covered entities up to 60 days from discovery to notify affected individuals of a breach. Mississippi law tightens that window.

## Mississippi Health Privacy Law Overview

Mississippi's primary health privacy statutes that affect clinic operations are focused on breach notification and records retention.

### Mississippi Code Ann. Section 75-24-29 - Breach Notification

Mississippi's security breach notification law requires any person conducting business in Mississippi who owns or licenses computerized data containing personal information of Mississippi residents to notify those residents of a breach within 45 days of discovery. This is 15 days shorter than HIPAA's 60-day notification ceiling.

Mississippi defines personal information as a first name or initial plus last name combined with one of the following unencrypted data elements:

- Social Security number
- Driver's license or state identification number
- Account, credit card, or debit card number plus any required security code

This definition covers a narrower set of data elements than HIPAA's PHI definition, which encompasses 18 identifiers. A given security incident may trigger Mississippi's breach notification law, HIPAA's Breach Notification Rule, both, or neither. Each analysis must be conducted independently.

If notification is made to more than 1,000 Mississippi residents at one time, the breaching entity must also notify all consumer reporting agencies. Mississippi does not require formal notification to the AG for most breaches, but the AG's Consumer Protection Division may investigate complaints from affected residents.

### MS Code Section 41-9-107 - Hospital Records Retention

MS Code Section 41-9-107 requires Mississippi hospitals to retain medical records for a minimum of 10 years following the patient's discharge or last treatment at the facility. This is more demanding than HIPAA's administrative record retention requirement of 6 years from creation or last effective date.

The 10-year retention requirement under Mississippi law applies specifically to hospital records. Clinics operating within hospital systems, including outpatient departments of hospitals, must apply the 10-year standard. Freestanding outpatient clinics should determine their applicable retention period under Mississippi law based on their facility type and licensing.

For any Mississippi clinic, the HIPAA standard is that medical record retention follows applicable state law. Where state law requires a longer retention period than HIPAA's administrative documentation minimum, state law controls. Mississippi clinics should document their retention policy based on the longer of the applicable state-law period or the HIPAA minimum.

### Mississippi Consumer Protection Framework

Mississippi's general consumer protection statute (Mississippi Code Ann. Section 75-24-1 et seq.) provides an additional enforcement mechanism for data practices that harm Mississippi consumers. While this statute is not health-specific, the AG's Consumer Protection Division applies it to business practices including inadequate data security.

## Key Differences: Mississippi Law vs. HIPAA

| Area | HIPAA | Mississippi Law |
|------|-------|----------------|
| Breach notification deadline | 60 days from discovery | 45 days from discovery (Section 75-24-29) |
| Large-breach reporting | Media notice + HHS if 500+ in a state | Consumer reporting agency notice if 1,000+ affected |
| Medical records retention | Defers to state law for clinical records; 6 years for administrative documentation | 10 years for hospital records (Section 41-9-107) |
| State health privacy law | HIPAA governs | No Mississippi health privacy law exceeds HIPAA in most areas |
| Enforcement | OCR civil monetary penalties | Mississippi AG Consumer Protection Division; also OCR |

## AG Enforcement in Mississippi

The Mississippi Attorney General's Consumer Protection Division enforces the state's breach notification law and has concurrent authority to pursue HIPAA violations affecting Mississippi residents under the HITECH Act.

The Mississippi AG may bring civil actions against covered entities for:

- Failure to notify affected residents within 45 days under Section 75-24-29
- HIPAA violations that harm Mississippi residents
- Consumer protection violations arising from inadequate data security practices

Mississippi enforcement posture - like that of most state AGs - is complaint-driven. A breach notification sent to patients is a direct trigger for patient complaints to the AG's office. The AG may open an investigation based on complaint volume, severity of the breach, or apparent inadequacy of the clinic's security program.

The Mississippi AG enforcement track is separate from OCR enforcement. A single PHI breach can result in simultaneous investigations by both OCR and the Mississippi AG.

## 5 Action Items for Mississippi Clinics

**1. Align your breach response timeline to 45 days.** Update your incident response plan to treat Mississippi's 45-day notification deadline as the controlling timeline for incidents affecting Mississippi residents. Build a checklist that runs both the Mississippi Section 75-24-29 analysis and the HIPAA risk assessment in parallel from the moment an incident is discovered. Both analyses begin at discovery - do not wait to complete the HIPAA risk assessment before starting the Mississippi analysis.

**2. Confirm your records retention policy.** Determine your clinic's applicable Mississippi records retention standard based on your facility type and licensing. If your clinic operates within a hospital system or as an outpatient department of a hospital, apply the 10-year retention period under MS Code Section 41-9-107. Document your retention policy and ensure your EHR or records management system is configured to reflect it. The retention policy should cover both clinical records and HIPAA administrative documentation.

**3. Execute and maintain current business associate agreements.** For Mississippi clinics, where HIPAA is the primary governing framework, BA agreement gaps represent the single most common compliance exposure. Every contractor with access to PHI - including EHR vendors, billing companies, transcription services, and IT support providers - requires a written BA agreement. Review your BA inventory at least annually.

**4. Build and maintain your risk analysis documentation.** OCR's first document request in any enforcement investigation is the covered entity's most recent risk analysis. Mississippi clinics without a current, documented risk analysis are immediately exposed in any investigation. Use the [HIPAA compliance self-assessment](/resources/hipaa-compliance-self-assessment) as a starting framework. The analysis must address risks to the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of all forms of electronic PHI that your clinic creates, receives, maintains, or transmits.

**5. Document workforce training.** HIPAA's workforce training requirement under 45 CFR Section  164.530(b) requires that all workforce members be trained on your privacy and security policies as necessary and appropriate for them to carry out their duties. Documentation of who was trained, when, and on what topic is required. Training records must be retained for 6 years. For Mississippi clinics without a dedicated compliance officer, the practice manager typically owns this documentation function.
