West Virginia medical clinics operate under HIPAA's federal compliance framework and three state-law provisions that create distinct obligations: the West Virginia Personal Information and Privacy Protection Act, which establishes a 45-day breach notification deadline; the West Virginia Patient Bill of Rights, which creates statutory patient rights independently enforceable under state law; and W. Va. Code Section 55-2-15, which establishes the records retention framework. The most significant operational impact for most West Virginia clinics is the 45-day breach notification deadline and the broad definition of personal information that includes medical data.

## HIPAA Baseline Requirements

Every West Virginia clinic that transmits health information electronically in connection with covered transactions is a HIPAA-covered entity subject to the full federal compliance framework:

- A documented risk analysis and risk management program under 45 CFR Section  164.308(a)(1)
- Administrative, physical, and technical safeguards under 45 CFR Sections  164.308-164.316
- Business associate agreements with every vendor or contractor handling PHI, per 45 CFR Section  164.502(e)
- A Notice of Privacy Practices provided to patients at their first service encounter
- Workforce training on privacy and security policies under 45 CFR Section  164.530(b)
- Breach notification procedures under the Breach Notification Rule at 45 CFR Part 164, Subpart D

HIPAA gives covered entities up to 60 days from discovery of a breach to notify affected individuals. West Virginia law requires notification in 45 days.

## West Virginia Health Privacy Law Overview

West Virginia's three primary statutes affecting clinic compliance operations each address a distinct dimension: breach response, patient rights, and records retention.

### West Virginia Personal Information and Privacy Protection Act (W. Va. Code Section 46A-2A-101 et seq.)

The West Virginia Personal Information and Privacy Protection Act requires breach notification within 45 days of discovery. The statute applies to any person that owns, licenses, or maintains computerized data containing personal information of West Virginia residents.

West Virginia's definition of personal information is notably broader than many state breach notification laws. It includes:

- First name or initial plus last name combined with Social Security number
- First name or initial plus last name combined with driver's license or state ID number
- First name or initial plus last name combined with financial account information and access codes
- First name or initial plus last name combined with **medical information**

The explicit inclusion of medical information in West Virginia's personal information definition is significant for clinics. It means that a breach of clinical records - even records that do not include Social Security numbers, financial data, or government-issued identifiers - may trigger West Virginia's breach notification obligation. Clinics must analyze each incident against this definition alongside their HIPAA breach risk assessment.

If a breach affects more than 1,000 West Virginia residents simultaneously, the breaching entity must notify the major credit reporting agencies. The West Virginia AG must be notified promptly when notification is being provided to more than 1,000 residents.

### West Virginia Patient Bill of Rights (W. Va. Code Section 16-29H-1 et seq.)

The West Virginia Patient Bill of Rights establishes statutory rights for patients receiving healthcare services in West Virginia healthcare facilities. Key provisions include:

- The right to privacy during examination and treatment
- The right to access one's own medical records and to have information explained
- The right to receive care without unnecessary exposure to other patients or visitors
- The right to be informed of the identity and professional status of those providing care
- The right to receive information necessary to give informed consent for procedures

These rights are independently enforceable under West Virginia law. HIPAA provides overlapping protections in many areas, and HIPAA's access and privacy requirements generally meet or exceed the Bill of Rights provisions. However, the Bill of Rights creates a distinct state-law cause of action that does not depend on HIPAA. Patients can assert Bill of Rights violations through the healthcare facility's patient advocate process and, where facilities fail to resolve complaints, through state regulatory channels.

West Virginia clinics should ensure their Notice of Privacy Practices and patient rights procedures address both HIPAA requirements and the Patient Bill of Rights.

### W. Va. Code Section 55-2-15 - Records Retention

W. Va. Code Section 55-2-15 addresses the statute of limitations framework for medical malpractice actions in West Virginia and establishes the records retention period necessary to support potential malpractice defense. West Virginia clinics typically apply a retention period of 10 years for adult patient records under this framework, though the applicable period should be confirmed with West Virginia healthcare counsel.

HIPAA's administrative record retention minimum (6 years for policies, procedures, and compliance documentation) applies separately from clinical records retention. West Virginia clinics must retain clinical records for the period required under West Virginia law - typically longer than HIPAA's administrative minimum - and retain HIPAA compliance documentation for the 6-year HIPAA minimum.

## Key Differences: West Virginia Law vs. HIPAA

| Area | HIPAA | West Virginia Law |
|------|-------|------------------|
| Breach notification deadline | 60 days from discovery | 45 days from discovery (Section 46A-2A-102) |
| Breach definition | PHI involved in unauthorized access, use, or disclosure | Personal information (including medical information) |
| Medical records retention | Defers to state law for clinical records | ~10 years under Section 55-2-15 framework |
| Patient rights | Privacy Rule access rights | Patient Bill of Rights (Section 16-29H-1 et seq.) |
| Enforcement | OCR civil monetary penalties | WV AG; also OCR |

The medical information inclusion in West Virginia's personal information definition is the most distinctive compliance challenge. It means virtually every PHI breach will require analysis under West Virginia's breach notification law, and many incidents that would not trigger HIPAA breach notification may still trigger West Virginia notification.

## AG Enforcement in West Virginia

The West Virginia Attorney General enforces the Personal Information and Privacy Protection Act and has concurrent HIPAA enforcement authority under the HITECH Act for violations affecting West Virginia residents.

The WV AG's enforcement powers include:

- Civil actions against entities that fail to provide timely breach notification
- Injunctive relief requiring remediation of security deficiencies
- Pursuit of damages on behalf of West Virginia residents
- Civil penalties under West Virginia consumer protection law

West Virginia AG enforcement operates alongside OCR enforcement - a single incident can trigger both tracks. The AG's office accepts consumer complaints directly, meaning breach notification recipients can immediately trigger an AG investigation by filing a complaint.

The West Virginia Board of Medicine and the West Virginia Department of Health and Human Resources also have oversight roles that can be activated by serious privacy or security incidents at clinical facilities.

## 5 Action Items for West Virginia Clinics

**1. Expand your breach trigger analysis to include medical information.** Because West Virginia's personal information definition includes medical information, your incident response procedures must evaluate every PHI incident against West Virginia's 45-day notification requirement - not just incidents involving financial or government-identifier data. Update your incident classification checklist to include a specific West Virginia trigger analysis step for any incident involving medical records, clinical notes, or patient data.

**2. Set a 45-day notification deadline as your standard.** Remove HIPAA's 60-day window as the primary deadline reference in your incident response plan for West Virginia patients. West Virginia's 45 days controls. For incidents affecting large numbers of residents (1,000+), build in the West Virginia AG notification requirement as a separate action item.

**3. Align patient rights procedures with both HIPAA and the Patient Bill of Rights.** Review your Notice of Privacy Practices and patient rights acknowledgment forms to confirm they address the West Virginia Patient Bill of Rights provisions alongside HIPAA requirements. Your patient advocate or complaint-handling process should be capable of receiving and resolving Patient Bill of Rights complaints within the timelines required by West Virginia healthcare facility regulations.

**4. Confirm your records retention policy and configure systems accordingly.** Determine the applicable retention period for your clinic's clinical records under West Virginia law - typically 10 years for adult patients under the Section 55-2-15 framework - and document that period in your written retention policy. Verify that your EHR or records management system enforces the correct retention period and does not permit premature deletion of records.

**5. Conduct and document an annual risk analysis.** A current, written risk analysis is the foundation of Security Rule compliance and the first document OCR requests in any investigation. West Virginia clinics that have not conducted a formal risk analysis since implementing their current EHR or expanding their physical locations have an immediate gap to close.
