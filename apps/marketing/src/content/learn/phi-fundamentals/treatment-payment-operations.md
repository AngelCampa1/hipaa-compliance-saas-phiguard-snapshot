Treatment, payment, and healthcare operations (TPO) are the three categories under 45 CFR §§ 164.502(a)(1)(ii) and 164.506 for which your clinic may use and disclose PHI without patient authorization. TPO is the operational foundation of HIPAA's privacy regime: it allows routine clinical and administrative activities to function without requiring signed authorization for every PHI use. But the boundaries of TPO are specific, and activities commonly assumed to fall within TPO sometimes do not.

**Small-clinic example:** A 6-provider internal medicine practice's billing coordinator shares a patient's encounter record with the patient's insurer for a utilization review — that's payment, no authorization needed. The same coordinator shares the same patient's records with a pharmaceutical company conducting market research — that is not TPO, and authorization was required. Both situations involve disclosing PHI to a third party. Only one is covered by the TPO permission.

## The Regulatory Basis

Under 45 CFR § 164.502(a)(1)(ii), your clinic may use or disclose PHI for its own treatment, payment, or healthcare operations activities. Under 45 CFR § 164.506(c), additional permissions allow disclosures between covered entities for treatment, between a covered entity and a business associate for payment or healthcare operations, and between covered entities in organized healthcare arrangements.

The definitions of treatment, payment, and healthcare operations appear in 45 CFR § 164.501.

## Treatment Defined

**Treatment** means the provision, coordination, or management of health care and related services by one or more health care providers, including coordination or management of health care with a third party, consultation between providers relating to a patient, and referral of a patient for health care from one provider to another.

**What treatment includes:**
- A physician's clinical examination, diagnosis, and treatment of a patient
- Sharing records with a specialist to whom a patient is being referred
- A cardiologist consulting with a primary care physician about a patient's medication management
- A care coordinator communicating with a home health agency about a patient's discharge plan
- Sharing summary records with an emergency department when a patient presents for emergency care

**What treatment does not include:**
- Communications with providers who are not treating the patient
- Disclosures to employers for occupational health purposes (unless a treatment relationship exists)
- Sharing records for research purposes, even if the research may benefit patients generally

**Minimum necessary exception for treatment.** The minimum necessary standard — which requires your clinic to use, disclose, and request only the minimum PHI necessary for the intended purpose — does **not** apply to treatment disclosures between treating health care providers (45 CFR § 164.502(b)(2)(i)). When physicians communicate for treatment purposes, they are not required to limit themselves to the minimum necessary portion of the record.

## Payment Defined

**Payment** means activities undertaken by a covered entity to obtain or provide reimbursement for health care, including: billing and collection activities; determinations of eligibility or coverage; claims processing, adjudication, and subrogation; medical necessity determinations; utilization review (prospective, concurrent, and retrospective); coordination of benefits; and risk adjusting amounts owed based on beneficiary health status.

**What payment includes:**
- Submitting a claim to Medicare, Medicaid, or a commercial insurer
- Verifying a patient's insurance eligibility before an appointment
- Following up on a denied claim with medical records supporting the denial appeal
- Responding to an insurer's request for records for a utilization review
- Coordinating benefits when a patient has multiple insurers

**What payment does not include:**
- Disclosures made primarily for marketing the covered entity's services, even if framed as financial
- Disclosures to collection agencies beyond what legitimate debt collection requires
- Selling PHI to data brokers or analytics companies

The **minimum necessary standard applies to payment disclosures**. When submitting claims, your clinic should include only the PHI necessary for the claim — not the complete medical record unless the payer's adjudication process specifically requires it.

## Healthcare Operations Defined

Healthcare operations is the broadest and most complex category. Under 45 CFR § 164.501, it includes:

**(1) Quality assessment and improvement activities**, including outcomes evaluation, development of clinical guidelines, patient safety activities, and population-based activities relating to improving health or reducing health care costs.

**(2) Reviewing the competence or qualifications of health care professionals**, credentialing, peer review, and training programs in which students, trainees, or practitioners learn under supervision.

**(3) Conducting or arranging for medical review, legal services, and auditing functions**, including fraud and abuse detection and compliance programs.

**(4) Business planning and development**, including cost-management and planning-related analyses.

**(5) Business management and general administrative activities**, including customer service, resolution of internal grievances, and creating de-identified data.

**(6) Due diligence in connection with a proposed merger or acquisition.**

**(7) Fundraising activities**, subject to specific limitations on what PHI may be used and patient opt-out rights.

**Activities clearly within healthcare operations:**
- Staff training programs using case examples (with minimum necessary limits)
- Internal quality audits reviewing claim denials or readmission rates
- Preparing statistical reports on patient outcomes for the clinic's planning purposes
- Attorney review of records as part of defending a malpractice claim
- Practice administrator reviewing billing records for compliance with billing policies

**Activities that are NOT healthcare operations:**
- Using PHI to market products or services to patients, unless specifically permitted
- Selling PHI to employers, insurers, or data companies for underwriting or risk assessment
- Sharing clinical data with pharmaceutical companies for drug research without a research authorization or IRB waiver

## Where TPO Ends and Authorization Begins

The most common compliance errors involve treating non-TPO activities as if they were covered by the TPO permissions.

**Marketing.** The Privacy Rule defines marketing broadly: a communication about a product or service that encourages the recipient to purchase or use it. Even communications about health-related products require authorization if they financially benefit the covered entity, with limited exceptions for face-to-face communications and promotional gifts of nominal value. A clinic that sends patients a newsletter promoting a supplement it sells, or that shares a patient list with a medical equipment company in exchange for a referral arrangement, is using PHI for marketing — not healthcare operations. Authorization is required.

**Employment purposes.** A covered entity may use PHI about its employees for its own healthcare operations, but disclosing patient PHI to an employer for occupational health purposes without the patient's authorization is outside TPO for most purposes.

**Research.** Clinical research that uses PHI requires patient authorization, an IRB-approved waiver of authorization, or another specific Privacy Rule permission. Treating research as healthcare operations because the research might benefit patients is a compliance error.

**Fundraising.** Fundraising is technically a healthcare operations activity, but with specific constraints. Your clinic may use certain PHI — demographic information and dates of health care — without patient authorization for its own fundraising, but must provide patients with an opportunity to opt out. Broader PHI use for fundraising requires authorization.

**What not to do:** Your clinic receives a request from a local hospital system asking for a list of patients with a specific chronic condition for a population health initiative. This may seem like treatment or healthcare operations, but if your clinic does not have an existing care coordination relationship with that hospital system covering those patients, the disclosure is not automatically covered by TPO. Evaluate the relationship and the purpose before disclosing.

## The Minimum Necessary Standard and TPO

The minimum necessary standard requires your clinic to make reasonable efforts to limit PHI to the minimum necessary to accomplish the intended purpose (45 CFR § 164.514(d)).

| Activity | Minimum Necessary Standard Applies? |
|----------|-------------------------------------|
| Treatment disclosures between treating providers | No (explicitly exempt) |
| Payment activities | Yes |
| Healthcare operations activities | Yes |
| All other disclosures outside TPO | Yes |

For payment and operations, this means: claims should include only the PHI the payer needs for adjudication; internal quality reviews should access only the records relevant to the quality measure being reviewed; and training programs should use the minimum patient information needed to illustrate the educational point.
