## Who Needs This Template

Every covered health care provider that has a direct treatment relationship with patients must provide a Notice of Privacy Practices. That includes primary care clinics, specialty practices, behavioral health providers, physical therapists, chiropractors, and any other provider who delivers care directly to patients and transmits any health information electronically.

The NPP is not optional and not interchangeable with a general privacy policy. The HIPAA Privacy Rule specifies exactly what it must say, how it must be formatted, and when it must be delivered. Using a generic disclaimer or a template that has not been verified against 45 CFR §164.520 is a compliance gap that OCR has cited in enforcement actions against small practices.

## What HIPAA Rule It Satisfies

45 CFR §164.520 governs the content, distribution, and acknowledgment of the Notice of Privacy Practices. The rule requires covered entities to produce an NPP, make it available on request, post it in the facility, and for providers with direct patient relationships, offer it at the first service encounter.

The NPP is your clinic's formal statement of patient rights and your legal basis for how you use PHI. It covers what you are permitted to do with patient information without explicit authorization, what rights patients have, and how they can exercise them. An NPP that omits a required element leaves your clinic without a valid legal basis for the uses it describes.

## What Is Inside This Template

**Header and effective date.** The NPP must include the effective date and a header stating "THIS NOTICE DESCRIBES HOW MEDICAL INFORMATION ABOUT YOU MAY BE USED AND DISCLOSED AND HOW YOU CAN GET ACCESS TO THIS INFORMATION."

**Permitted uses and disclosures.** The template covers the three categories allowed without authorization — treatment, payment, and health care operations — with plain-language examples relevant to a small clinic. It includes language for the required and permitted disclosures such as public health activities, law enforcement, and court orders.

**Patient rights section.** The NPP must describe each right:

- Right to request restrictions on use and disclosure
- Right to request confidential communications
- Right to access and copy PHI in the designated record set (45 CFR §164.524)
- Right to request amendments (§164.526)
- Right to an accounting of disclosures (§164.528)
- Right to receive a paper copy of the NPP on request
- Right to file a complaint with the clinic and with HHS OCR

**Clinic duties section.** The NPP must state that the clinic is required to maintain the privacy of PHI, to provide this notice, and to abide by the terms currently in effect.

**Complaint procedures.** Name and contact information for your Privacy Officer, plus the HHS OCR complaint filing address.

**Customization fields.** Bracketed placeholders for clinic name, state, Privacy Officer name, effective date, and any state-specific disclosures your state requires beyond federal minimums.

**Acknowledgment receipt.** A detachable receipt for patients to sign, with a field for the staff member to note when a patient declined to sign.

## How to Fill It Out

**Step 1.** Replace every bracketed field with your clinic's specific information. Pay particular attention to the Privacy Officer name and contact — patients must have a real person to contact.

**Step 2.** Review the state-specific disclosures section. Several states impose stricter requirements on disclosures related to mental health, HIV status, substance use, and reproductive health. If your practice touches any of these areas, consult your state's health privacy statutes before finalizing.

**Step 3.** Have your Privacy Officer or legal counsel review the completed NPP before distribution.

**Step 4.** Post in a clear location in the clinic lobby. Publish on your website. Load into your patient portal.

**Step 5.** Train front-desk staff on the distribution requirement. Every new patient receives the NPP before or at the first visit. Staff obtain and document the acknowledgment, or document the attempt when patients decline.

## Who Fills It Out, Where to Post It, and When to Update It

**Who fills it out:** Your Privacy Officer or practice administrator completes the bracketed customization fields and coordinates legal or compliance review before distribution. Front-desk staff distribute it at first patient contact and document acknowledgment.

**Where to store and post it:** The NPP must be posted in a clear and prominent location in the facility where patients receive service. If the clinic maintains a website, the current NPP must be posted there as well (45 CFR §164.520(c)(3)). Retain acknowledged copies — or documentation of a patient's refusal to sign — in the patient's record for the duration of the relationship and for six years thereafter.

**How often it is updated:** Review the NPP annually. A new version with an updated effective date is required whenever there is a material change to your privacy practices.

**What triggers a required update:** Any material change to your uses or disclosures, your individual rights procedures, your legal duties, or your complaint process requires a prompt NPP revision under 45 CFR §164.520(b)(3). Specific triggers include: adding a new service category that changes how PHI is used; a change to state health privacy law; a change in your Privacy Officer; or any new HIPAA regulatory requirement affecting patient rights.
