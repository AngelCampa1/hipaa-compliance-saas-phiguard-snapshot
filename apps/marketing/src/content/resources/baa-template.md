## How to Use This Pack

This document is three things in one. First, a pre-flight checklist to confirm a Business Associate Agreement is actually required for the vendor relationship in front of you. Second, a full BAA template that tracks the required provisions of 45 CFR §164.504(e)(2). Third, a tracking template for the signed agreements you already hold, because a BAA you cannot find is effectively a BAA you do not have.

A practice administrator with no legal background can work through this in an afternoon. Copy the template into a Word document, fill in the bracketed fields, have your vendor countersign, and file both copies in your compliance program records. Keep legal counsel in the loop for unusual relationships, indemnification negotiations, or any vendor that pushes back on the standard terms.

## Part 1 - When Is a BAA Required?

A covered entity must execute a BAA with any person or organization that creates, receives, maintains, or transmits Protected Health Information on the covered entity's behalf. The test is function, not job title. If the vendor touches PHI in the course of providing a service to you, a BAA is required before that access begins.

### The pre-flight checklist

Go through this list for every vendor relationship, active and planned. Any "yes" in the left column means you need a signed BAA on file.

1. Does the vendor have access to your EHR, practice management, or billing system?
2. Does the vendor send, receive, or store email on your behalf where that email may contain PHI?
3. Does the vendor host your data in a cloud environment - including backups, file storage, or document repositories?
4. Does the vendor perform transcription, coding, claims submission, or any revenue cycle work on your patient records?
5. Does the vendor provide IT support that includes remote access to systems holding PHI?
6. Does the vendor host your patient portal, appointment reminders, or two-way patient messaging?
7. Does the vendor shred, destroy, or dispose of documents or devices that held PHI?
8. Does the vendor offer analytics, population health, or reporting services that consume patient data?
9. Does the vendor administer your telehealth platform?
10. Does the vendor provide answering, scheduling, or after-hours call services that handle patient information?

### When a BAA is not required

Some relationships that feel like they need a BAA do not. The conduit exception covers entities that only transmit PHI and do not store it in any meaningful way: the US Postal Service, common carriers, and internet service providers that carry traffic but do not access its content. Janitorial services that may incidentally encounter paper records during cleaning are generally not business associates, though they should be covered by your workforce training and physical safeguards.

Staffing agencies that place workforce members who then act as your workforce are also not business associates for that placement function. Those workers are part of your workforce and must be trained and credentialed as such.

When in doubt, treat the relationship as requiring a BAA. The cost of an extra signed agreement is zero. The cost of a missing one during an OCR investigation is not.

## Part 2 - The BAA Template

The text below is designed to be copied into a Word document and filled in. Bracketed fields are variable; everything else tracks the required elements of §164.504(e)(2). This template is drafted from the covered entity's perspective - you are the CE, the vendor is the BA.

---

**BUSINESS ASSOCIATE AGREEMENT**

This Business Associate Agreement ("Agreement") is entered into as of **[EFFECTIVE DATE]** (the "Effective Date") by and between **[COVERED ENTITY LEGAL NAME]**, a **[STATE]** **[ENTITY TYPE]** ("Covered Entity"), and **[BUSINESS ASSOCIATE LEGAL NAME]**, a **[STATE]** **[ENTITY TYPE]** ("Business Associate"). Covered Entity and Business Associate are each a "Party" and collectively the "Parties."

**Recitals.** Covered Entity is a covered entity as defined at 45 CFR §160.103. Business Associate provides services to Covered Entity that involve the creation, receipt, maintenance, or transmission of Protected Health Information. The Parties enter into this Agreement to comply with the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act of 1996 and its implementing regulations at 45 CFR Parts 160 and 164 ("HIPAA"), as amended by the HITECH Act and the Omnibus Rule.

### 1. Definitions

Capitalized terms used but not defined in this Agreement have the meanings given at 45 CFR §§160.103 and 164.501. For clarity:

- "PHI" means Protected Health Information, limited to information created, received, maintained, or transmitted by Business Associate on behalf of Covered Entity.
- "ePHI" means PHI transmitted or maintained in electronic media.
- "Security Incident" has the meaning at 45 CFR §164.304.
- "Breach" has the meaning at 45 CFR §164.402.
- "Required By Law" has the meaning at 45 CFR §164.103.

### 2. Permitted Uses and Disclosures

Business Associate may use and disclose PHI only as follows:

(a) To perform the services described in the underlying services agreement between the Parties (the "Services Agreement") and for no other purpose.

(b) For the proper management and administration of Business Associate, provided that any disclosure outside Business Associate is Required By Law or is made under a written agreement that the recipient will hold the information confidentially and notify Business Associate of any breach.

(c) To provide data aggregation services relating to the health care operations of Covered Entity, if and only if the Services Agreement expressly contemplates such services.

(d) To report violations of law to appropriate federal and state authorities, consistent with 45 CFR §164.502(j)(1).

Business Associate will not use or disclose PHI in a manner that would violate Subpart E of 45 CFR Part 164 if done by Covered Entity, except as specifically permitted above.

### 3. Minimum Necessary

Business Associate will limit use, disclosure, and requests for PHI to the minimum necessary to accomplish the intended purpose, consistent with 45 CFR §164.502(b) and guidance issued by HHS.

### 4. Safeguards

Business Associate will implement administrative, physical, and technical safeguards, and maintain policies and procedures, that reasonably and appropriately protect the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of ePHI as required by 45 CFR §§164.308, 164.310, 164.312, and 164.316.

On written request, Business Associate will provide Covered Entity with a summary of its current safeguards, including encryption standards applied to ePHI at rest and in transit. Encryption at rest and in transit meeting NIST FIPS 140-2 or a successor standard is required for any ePHI maintained or transmitted by Business Associate.

### 5. Reporting

(a) **Security Incidents.** Business Associate will report to Covered Entity any Security Incident of which it becomes aware. Unsuccessful Security Incidents such as pings and port scans that do not result in unauthorized access may be reported in aggregate on a quarterly basis.

(b) **Breaches of Unsecured PHI.** Business Associate will notify Covered Entity of any Breach of Unsecured PHI without unreasonable delay and in no case later than **twenty-four (24) hours** after Discovery. Notice will include, to the extent then known: the identification of each affected individual, the nature of the PHI involved, the date of the Breach, the date of Discovery, and a description of the circumstances.

(c) **Uses or Disclosures in Violation of this Agreement.** Business Associate will report to Covered Entity any use or disclosure of PHI not permitted by this Agreement within five (5) business days of Discovery.

### 6. Subcontractors

Business Associate will not disclose PHI to any subcontractor that creates, receives, maintains, or transmits PHI on Business Associate's behalf without first obtaining satisfactory written assurances, in a form substantially equivalent to this Agreement, that the subcontractor will appropriately safeguard the PHI. Business Associate will provide Covered Entity with a current list of such subcontractors on written request.

### 7. Individual Rights

Business Associate will:

(a) Make PHI in a Designated Record Set available to Covered Entity, or to the Individual as directed by Covered Entity, within ten (10) business days of request, to enable Covered Entity to meet its obligations under 45 CFR §164.524.

(b) Make amendments to PHI in a Designated Record Set as directed by Covered Entity under 45 CFR §164.526, within thirty (30) days of receipt of the direction.

(c) Document and make available the information required for an accounting of disclosures under 45 CFR §164.528, within thirty (30) days of receipt of a request from Covered Entity.

### 8. Access by HHS

Business Associate will make its internal practices, books, and records relating to the use and disclosure of PHI available to the Secretary of HHS for purposes of determining compliance with HIPAA.

### 9. Mitigation

Business Associate will mitigate, to the extent practicable, any harmful effect known to it of a use or disclosure of PHI in violation of this Agreement.

### 10. Term and Termination

(a) **Term.** This Agreement takes effect on the Effective Date and remains in effect until the Services Agreement terminates or until either Party terminates this Agreement under subsection (b).

(b) **Termination for Breach.** Covered Entity may terminate this Agreement and the Services Agreement immediately on written notice if it determines that Business Associate has materially violated this Agreement and has not cured the violation within thirty (30) days of written notice, or if cure is not feasible.

(c) **Return or Destruction of PHI.** On termination, Business Associate will return or destroy all PHI received from, or created or received on behalf of, Covered Entity, and will retain no copies. If return or destruction is not feasible, Business Associate will extend the protections of this Agreement to the retained PHI and limit further uses and disclosures to those purposes that make return or destruction infeasible. Business Associate will provide Covered Entity with a written certification of return or destruction within thirty (30) days of termination.

### 11. Indemnification

Business Associate will indemnify and hold harmless Covered Entity from any third-party claim, civil monetary penalty, or regulatory assessment arising from Business Associate's breach of this Agreement or violation of HIPAA. This section survives termination.

### 12. Miscellaneous

(a) **Amendment.** The Parties will amend this Agreement as necessary to comply with changes in HIPAA. Neither Party will unreasonably withhold consent to such an amendment.

(b) **Interpretation.** Any ambiguity is resolved in favor of a meaning that permits compliance with HIPAA.

(c) **No Third-Party Beneficiaries.** Nothing in this Agreement confers any rights on any person other than the Parties.

(d) **Governing Law.** This Agreement is governed by the laws of the State of **[STATE]**, without regard to conflict-of-law principles, except to the extent HIPAA preempts state law.

## Part 3 - Common Mistakes That Void BAA Coverage

The five issues below account for the majority of BAA findings in OCR resolution agreements involving small practices.

**No BAA at all.** The most frequent finding. A vendor has been handling PHI for years, no written agreement was ever executed, and nobody at the clinic thought to ask. Start with the pre-flight checklist above and work through every active vendor. If any row lacks a signed BAA, you have an open compliance gap.

**Accepting the vendor's standard terms without a HIPAA addendum.** General SaaS terms of service do not satisfy §164.504(e). Some vendors will offer a HIPAA addendum on request but do not include it by default. Ask explicitly: "Do you sign a BAA as part of standard onboarding, and if so can I see it before I pay?"

**Unsigned or undated agreements.** A BAA in a drawer with no countersignature and no date has no legal effect. When you receive a signed copy back, confirm that the signature block is complete and that the date matches the vendor's countersignature date. Store the final executed version, not the draft you sent.

**Failure to reissue on subcontractor changes.** If your EHR vendor migrates from one cloud provider to another, or your billing service adds a new offshore coder, the subcontractor chain has changed. Ask the vendor in writing whether its subcontractors are current under the §164.502(e)(2) flow-down terms of your BAA. Document the response.

**Missing breach notification timelines.** §164.410 gives business associates up to 60 days to notify covered entities, but OCR has found that 60 days is almost always too slow in practice. Negotiate a shorter window. The template above uses 24 hours. If a vendor pushes back, ask why, and document the rationale.

## Part 4 - Signed BAA Tracking Template

A signed BAA you cannot locate in a hurry is a compliance gap. Maintain a single inventory document with one row per vendor. At minimum capture:

- Vendor legal name
- Services provided
- Whether PHI is accessed, and the category of PHI
- Date BAA executed
- Date BAA last reviewed
- Contact for BAA renegotiation at the vendor
- Location of the signed PDF in your records
- Renewal or review date
- Status: Active, In Negotiation, Missing, Terminated

Review the tracker at least annually, and any time you onboard a new vendor, change platforms, or terminate a relationship. The Vendor BAA Tracker resource on this site includes a more detailed version of this inventory as a downloadable spreadsheet.
