## What an Annual Compliance Audit Actually Requires

The HIPAA Security Rule requires covered entities to perform periodic technical and non-technical evaluations in response to environmental or operational changes — and at least annually regardless of changes (45 CFR § 164.308(a)(8)). The Privacy Rule imposes parallel administrative requirements covering policies, training, sanctions, and the Notice of Privacy Practices.

For a small medical clinic, "periodic evaluation" translates in practice to an annual structured review of the entire compliance program. Not because OCR will show up every year — OCR's audit program is targeted and reactive — but because a compliance program that is not actively maintained decays. Policies go stale. Vendors are added without BAAs. Training is skipped for one staff member and then another. Access permissions accumulate for employees whose roles changed months ago. The annual audit is the mechanism that prevents silent decay.

This downloadable PDF structures the annual review as a single afternoon's work for a clinic with an established compliance program. If your program is starting from scratch, the same checklist tells you exactly what needs to be built. Each of the eight elements has specific action items, the responsible role, the regulatory citation, and the evidence to file.

## The Eight Elements

### Element 1 — Risk Analysis Updated or Reviewed

**Regulatory basis:** 45 CFR § 164.308(a)(1)(ii)(A)

The risk analysis is the foundation of the Security Rule compliance program. The annual review for risk analysis asks:

- Has the risk analysis been updated in the past 12 months, or if no material changes occurred, has it been reviewed and documented as current? (A signed notation on the existing analysis that it was reviewed and found to be current is acceptable if the environment has not changed materially.)
- Are there any new assets (new software, new vendor, new location) that should be added to the analysis?
- Are there any threats that have emerged since the last analysis (new malware types, a breach at a similar clinic that surfaced a vulnerability) that should be reflected?

**Responsible role:** Security Officer

**Evidence to file:** Updated risk analysis with current date, or signed review notation on existing analysis; list of material changes reviewed.

### Element 2 — Risk Management Plan Reviewed and Mitigations Completed

**Regulatory basis:** 45 CFR § 164.308(a)(1)(ii)(B)

The risk management plan documents how identified risks will be addressed. The annual review asks:

- Have all high-priority remediations from the risk analysis been completed, or do they have documented status?
- Are open remediations still tracking to their target dates?
- Have any risks been formally accepted (not remediated) with a documented rationale and Security Officer signature?

**Responsible role:** Security Officer, Practice Administrator

**Evidence to file:** Updated risk register with remediation status; closed risk documentation with evidence; formal risk acceptance documentation where applicable.

### Element 3 — Policies and Procedures Reviewed and Updated

**Regulatory basis:** 45 CFR § 164.530(i), 45 CFR § 164.316

Privacy and security policies must be reviewed and updated as needed in response to material changes. The annual review asks:

- Have all HIPAA policies been reviewed in the past 12 months?
- Have any policies been updated to reflect operational changes, new technologies, or regulatory guidance updates?
- Are all policies dated and version-controlled?

**Responsible role:** Privacy Officer, Security Officer

**Evidence to file:** Policy version log with last review date; updated policies with effective dates; change log if policies were revised.

### Element 4 — Workforce Training Completed and Documented

**Regulatory basis:** 45 CFR § 164.530(b), 45 CFR § 164.308(a)(5)

Training must be provided to all workforce members and must be effective. The annual review asks:

- Have all active workforce members completed annual HIPAA training in the current period?
- Are training records signed and filed for every workforce member?
- Have any new workforce members completed initial training before accessing PHI?
- Were any staff members unable to pass the knowledge assessment? If so, is remediation documented?

**Responsible role:** Privacy Officer

**Evidence to file:** Training log with completion dates and signatures; quiz scores or knowledge assessment results; remediation records for any staff who did not pass.

### Element 5 — BAA Register Reviewed and Renewals Processed

**Regulatory basis:** 45 CFR § 164.308(b), 45 CFR § 164.504(e)

Every vendor, contractor, and service provider that creates, receives, maintains, or transmits PHI on your behalf is a business associate and requires a signed BAA. The annual review asks:

- Is your BAA register current? Are all active business associates listed?
- Are there any vendors currently using PHI without a signed BAA?
- Have any BAAs expired or are any approaching expiration? (Some BAAs have term limits; others are perpetual but should be reviewed when vendor terms of service change.)
- For vendors with BAAs executed before the 2013 Omnibus Rule, have the BAAs been updated to comply with current requirements?

**Responsible role:** Privacy Officer

**Evidence to file:** Updated BAA register with vendor name, BAA execution date, and renewal date; copies of all executed BAAs; documentation of any vendor reviews conducted.

### Element 6 — Access Review Completed

**Regulatory basis:** 45 CFR § 164.308(a)(4)

User access to systems containing PHI should be reviewed regularly to ensure that access reflects current roles and that former employees do not retain access. The annual review asks:

- Has a user access review been completed for the EHR and all other systems containing PHI?
- Are there any active accounts for employees who have been terminated?
- Are there any active accounts with access levels that do not match the employee's current role?
- Are any accounts shared between users? (Shared accounts violate the unique user identifier requirement at 45 CFR § 164.312(a)(2)(i).)

**Responsible role:** Security Officer, IT

**Evidence to file:** Access review report with date, list of accounts reviewed, changes made, and name of person who conducted the review.

### Element 7 — Incident Log Reviewed

**Regulatory basis:** 45 CFR § 164.308(a)(6), 45 CFR § 164.530(j)

The annual review of the incident log confirms that all incidents from the prior year were properly classified, investigated, and resolved. It also triggers the end-of-year small breach reporting obligation. The review asks:

- Is every reported incident from the prior 12 months in the log with a documented outcome?
- Were there any incidents that resulted in breach notification? If so, were notifications sent and are they documented?
- Were there breaches affecting fewer than 500 individuals? If so, the end-of-year notification to HHS via the online portal is due by March 1 of the following year.
- Were there any incidents that, in retrospect, were classified differently than they should have been? This review is an opportunity to correct the record before an audit.

**Responsible role:** Privacy Officer

**Evidence to file:** Incident log with all entries; breach notification records; HHS portal submission confirmation if applicable.

### Element 8 — NPP Reviewed and Current

**Regulatory basis:** 45 CFR § 164.520

The Notice of Privacy Practices must be provided to patients and must accurately describe the covered entity's privacy practices. The annual review asks:

- Has the NPP been reviewed in the past 12 months?
- Does the NPP accurately reflect current privacy practices? If any practices have changed — new uses or disclosures, new contact information, changed patient rights procedures — the NPP must be updated.
- Is the NPP posted prominently at service delivery locations and on the covered entity's website (if the entity has one)?
- Is the NPP being distributed to new patients at first service?

**Responsible role:** Privacy Officer

**Evidence to file:** Current NPP with effective date; documentation confirming posting and distribution procedures are in place.

## The Evidence Binder

The evidence binder section of the PDF lists every document category that your annual audit should produce and file. The binder is organized to match the eight elements above. For each element, it lists: the specific documents, the required retention period under 45 CFR § 164.530(j), and the recommended file format.

If your program is audited by OCR, the evidence binder is what you will produce. A binder assembled in advance — not during an audit response — demonstrates that the compliance program is active and maintained, not assembled retroactively.

## Who Should Use This Resource

This checklist is designed for:

- **Privacy Officers** at small and mid-size clinics who own the annual compliance review process.
- **Practice Administrators** who manage the compliance program without a dedicated compliance team.
- **Clinics preparing for a payer audit, a new contract requiring HIPAA compliance attestation, or an OCR investigation** who need to assess their program quickly and produce organized evidence.

## Regulatory Basis

The Security Rule's evaluation requirement at 45 CFR § 164.308(a)(8) requires periodic technical and non-technical evaluations based on the standards under the Security Rule and in response to environmental or operational changes. The Privacy Rule administrative requirements at 45 CFR § 164.530 cover training, sanctions, mitigation, complaint procedures, retaliation prohibition, waiver prohibition, policies and procedures, and documentation — all addressed in this checklist.
