## What the Evidence Binder Is — and What It Is Not

The HIPAA evidence binder is the organized collection of documentation that demonstrates your clinic's compliance program existed and functioned as required. It answers the question every OCR investigator asks first: "Show me your compliance documentation."

The evidence binder is not a guarantee of compliance. A clinic can have a well-organized binder full of documents and still have compliance gaps. A clinic without organized documentation cannot demonstrate compliance even if its practices were sound. The absence of documentation is treated as evidence of absent practice.

Under 45 CFR §164.530(j), covered entities must retain required HIPAA documentation for six years from the date of creation or the date the document was last in effect, whichever is later. "Required documentation" includes policies, training records, risk analyses, BAAs, incident records, and other artifacts generated by compliance activities.

Most small clinics have most of this documentation. It lives in a practice administrator's email inbox, a shared drive with no consistent structure, a filing cabinet in the back office, and a folder on someone's desktop. The evidence binder consolidates it into a structure that allows rapid production under pressure.

## The 2-Hour Initial Setup Protocol

Block two uninterrupted hours for the initial binder setup. This is a single focused session, not a multi-week project. Set up the folder structure first, then populate it with what you already have.

**First 30 minutes: set up the folder structure**

Create a top-level folder for the binder. Inside it, create seven subfolders using the names in the section structure below. For a physical binder, create seven tabbed sections.

**Next 90 minutes: file what you have**

Go through your email, shared drive, and filing cabinet. File every compliance document you find into the appropriate section. Do not stop to read or update documents — just file them. The goal is to consolidate what exists, not to fix gaps.

**Final 15 minutes: document the gaps**

After filing, look at each section. What's missing Create a gap list and add the missing items as tasks with owners and deadlines. These gaps become next month's compliance work.

## The 7-Section Structure

### Section 1: Policies and Procedures

**What belongs here:**
- Privacy Policy (all versions with dates)
- Security Policy (all versions with dates)
- Notice of Privacy Practices — current version and prior versions
- Breach Notification Policy
- Sanction Policy
- Access Control Policy (including workforce security and minimum necessary)
- Device and Media Policy
- Workstation Use Policy
- Contingency Plan (Backup, Disaster Recovery, Emergency Mode Operation)
- Any other clinic-specific HIPAA policies

**What good looks like in this section:**
- Each policy has a title, effective date, version number, and Privacy Officer signature
- When a policy is updated, the new version is added and the old version is marked "superseded" with its retirement date — not deleted
- The NPP in this section matches the NPP currently distributed to patients

**Common gap found here:** Policies exist from five years ago and have never been reviewed or updated. OCR expects periodic review and evidence of it.

**Filename convention:**  
`[SECTION]_[document-name]_[version]_[YYYY-MM-DD].pdf`  
Example: `01_privacy-policy_v3_2025-01-15.pdf`

### Section 2: Workforce Training Records

**What belongs here:**
- Annual training logs for each calendar year (staff name, date, method, completion status, verifier)
- New hire training documentation
- Role-specific training records (clinical staff, billing staff, administrative staff)
- Remedial training records
- Training curriculum summary or reference to LMS records

**What good looks like in this section:**
- A complete log exists for each calendar year, showing all workforce members including contractors and locums
- New hire records show training completed before PHI access was granted
- Records are organized by calendar year with the most recent year on top

**Common gap found here:** Training happened but was never logged in a format that can be produced. The administrator knows training occurred, but there's no artifact.

**Filename convention:**  
`02_training-log_[YYYY]_[completion-date].pdf`  
`02_new-hire-training_[staff-name]_[hire-date].pdf`

### Section 3: Risk Analysis and Risk Management

**What belongs here:**
- Current (most recent) risk analysis — dated, signed by Privacy Officer
- All prior risk analyses (retain for six years)
- Risk management plan for each risk analysis — what actions were taken to address identified risks
- Mid-year risk analysis updates (if triggered by environmental changes)

**What good looks like in this section:**
- Risk analysis is current — updated within the past 12 months or when significant environmental changes occurred
- Risk management plan is attached to the corresponding risk analysis — not a standalone document
- Both documents are signed and dated

**Common gap found here:** Risk analysis exists but risk management plan does not. Or: risk analysis exists from three years ago and was never updated.

**Filename convention:**  
`03_risk-analysis_[YYYY]_[YYYY-MM-DD].pdf`  
`03_risk-management-plan_[YYYY]_[YYYY-MM-DD].pdf`

### Section 4: Business Associate Agreements

**What belongs here:**
- Executed BAA for every current business associate (organized alphabetically by vendor name)
- Vendor inventory list (one-page summary of all vendors with BAA status and expiration dates)
- Terminated vendor BAAs (with notation of termination date — retain for six years from last active date)

**What good looks like in this section:**
- Every current PHI-touching vendor has a signed BAA on file
- BAA expiration dates are visible — either on the BAA itself or in the vendor inventory
- The vendor inventory is current and matches the actual list of vendors in use

**Common gap found here:** BAA folder exists but several current vendors are missing. Or: BAAs were signed but never filed in a central location — they live in email threads.

**Filename convention:**  
`04_baa_[vendor-name]_[execution-date]_[expiry-date].pdf`  
`04_vendor-inventory_[YYYY-MM-DD].pdf`

### Section 5: Incident and Breach Records

**What belongs here:**
- Incident log (all incidents, whether determined to be breaches or non-breach security events)
- For each incident:
  - Intake form
  - 4-factor risk assessment
  - Breach determination memo
  - Notification records (if breach)
  - HHS filing confirmation (if required)
- Annual breach summary submitted to HHS for incidents under 500 individuals

**What good looks like in this section:**
- Every security incident — including near-misses — appears in the log
- Each breach has a complete documentation package: intake → assessment → determination → notification → HHS
- The annual HHS report submission is documented with a copy of the submission and the confirmation

**Common gap found here:** A breach occurred, notifications were sent, but no 4-factor assessment was documented. Or: the HHS annual report for breaches under 500 individuals was not filed.

**Filename convention:**  
`05_incident-log_[YYYY].pdf`  
`05_incident_[YYYY-MM-DD]_[brief-description].pdf` (for individual incident packages)

### Section 6: Access Reviews and Workforce Records

**What belongs here:**
- Quarterly access review logs (current and prior year) — signed by Privacy Officer
- Role-based access matrix (current version)
- Minimum necessary decision log
- Offboarding checklists for all terminated staff (retain for six years after separation)
- New hire onboarding compliance checklists

**What good looks like in this section:**
- Four quarterly access reviews per year, each signed and dated
- Access reviews show what was found and what was remediated — not just a clean bill of health
- Offboarding checklists exist for every staff separation, not just formal full-time employees

**Common gap found here:** Access reviews were never documented — the administrator reviewed access informally but didn't produce a written record.

**Filename convention:**  
`06_access-review_[YYYY]_Q[N]_[YYYY-MM-DD].pdf`  
`06_offboarding_[staff-name]_[separation-date].pdf`

### Section 7: Physical and Technical Safeguard Documentation

**What belongs here:**
- Device and workstation inventory with encryption status and patch dates
- Physical security inspection records (annual minimum)
- Facility access log (keys and badges issued — to whom, when)
- Audit log review records (documentation that EHR access logs are reviewed regularly)
- Network documentation relevant to the Security Rule (if applicable)
- Any third-party security assessments or penetration tests

**What good looks like in this section:**
- Device inventory is current and reflects all clinic-issued devices
- Encryption status is confirmed for each device — not assumed
- Physical security inspections are documented as annual events with findings and follow-up

**Common gap found here:** Encryption has been enabled on clinic devices for years, but there's no documentation of when or how it was verified.

**Filename convention:**  
`07_device-inventory_[YYYY-MM-DD].pdf`  
`07_physical-security-inspection_[YYYY-MM-DD].pdf`

## The 6-Year Retention Matrix

Under §164.530(j), these documents must be retained for six years:

| Document type | Retention period | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Privacy and security policies | 6 years from last effective date | Keep superseded versions too |
| Risk analysis | 6 years from date of completion | All versions |
| Risk management plan | 6 years from date of completion | |
| Training records | 6 years from date of training | Per-staff, per-year |
| BAAs — current vendors | 6 years from date of execution (minimum) | |
| BAAs — terminated vendors | 6 years from last date in effect | |
| Incident records | 6 years from date of incident | All incidents, including non-breach |
| Breach records and notifications | 6 years from date | All breach documentation |
| Access review records | 6 years from date of review | |
| Offboarding records | 6 years from separation date | |

Note: State law may require longer retention for medical records separately from HIPAA compliance documentation. This matrix covers HIPAA compliance documentation retention. Medical record retention is governed by state law.

## Annual Maintenance Checklist

Each January, complete these binder maintenance tasks:

- [ ] File all compliance documents generated in the prior year into the appropriate sections
- [ ] Archive prior-year documents that are no longer current but within the 6-year retention window
- [ ] Identify and destroy documents that have passed their 6-year retention period (with a destruction log)
- [ ] Verify the vendor inventory in Section 4 is current
- [ ] Confirm quarterly access reviews from the prior year are all present in Section 6
- [ ] Confirm the annual HHS breach report for the prior year is filed and documented in Section 5
- [ ] Review Section 1 policies — were any updated Is the current version filed?
- [ ] Confirm Section 3 has a current (within 12 months) risk analysis

## What PHIGuard Changes

PHIGuard is the digital evidence binder. Compliance activities generate records organized automatically into the binder structure: training completions log themselves, access reviews produce signed records, incident reports generate 4-factor assessments and determination memos, vendor reviews attach to vendor records. The 6-year retention window is applied automatically. Annual maintenance becomes a review rather than a reconstruction.
