## How to Use This Template

This template gives your clinic a complete starting-point social media policy. Copy the text below into a Word document, fill in your clinic name and Privacy Officer's name, and review with legal counsel before distributing to staff. The policy should be included in your annual HIPAA workforce training and signed off by each employee. Store signed acknowledgments in your training records for at least six years per 45 CFR §164.530(j).

Social media is the most common source of accidental PHI disclosures in small clinics. Staff post with good intentions — celebrating a recovery, sharing a heartwarming moment — and do not realize that a patient's name, face, or medical context visible in the background constitutes a disclosure under 45 CFR §164.502. This policy closes that gap.

## Social Media Policy Template

**[CLINIC NAME]**
**Social Media and Patient Privacy Policy**
**Effective Date: [DATE]**
**Policy Owner: Privacy Officer — [NAME]**

---

### 1. Purpose and Scope

This policy governs the use of social media by all workforce members of [CLINIC NAME], including employees, contractors, volunteers, students, and any other person acting on behalf of the clinic. It applies to all social media platforms and any online forum, review site, or public communication channel where a workforce member represents or references the clinic.

The purpose of this policy is to protect patient privacy, prevent impermissible disclosures of Protected Health Information (PHI), and ensure [CLINIC NAME] meets its obligations under the HIPAA Privacy Rule at 45 CFR Part 164, Subpart E.

### 2. What Staff May Post

Workforce members may post content related to the clinic only if all of the following are true:

- The content contains no PHI — no patient names, photographs, dates of service, diagnoses, treatment details, financial information, or any combination of information that could identify a patient
- The content has been reviewed and approved by the designated social media coordinator (see Section 6)
- The content is accurate, professional, and consistent with the clinic's brand standards

Permitted content categories include:
- General health education and public health information
- Clinic announcements (hours, new services, staff introductions without patient involvement)
- Non-clinical office culture content (team events, professional achievements of staff)
- Content explicitly authorized under a valid patient written authorization (see Section 4)

### 3. PHI Prohibition

No workforce member may post, share, repost, or otherwise publish PHI in any form on any social media platform. This prohibition applies regardless of intent, context, or the apparent wishes of the patient.

PHI includes, but is not limited to:
- Patient names, photographs, or images in which a patient is identifiable
- Dates of service, appointment dates, or admission and discharge dates
- Geographic information more specific than state
- Patient diagnoses, conditions, medications, or treatment plans
- Financial information related to a patient's account or insurance
- Any other information that could identify a patient individually in connection with their health care

**Positive context is not an exception.** A post celebrating a patient's recovery, congratulating them on a successful procedure, or thanking them for their kind words is still an impermissible disclosure if it contains PHI. Obtain written authorization before posting any patient-related content (see Section 4).

### 4. Patient Photo and Written Authorization Requirements

Before posting any photograph, video, or other image in which a patient is identifiable:

1. Obtain a completed HIPAA-compliant written authorization per 45 CFR §164.508
2. The authorization must specifically describe the content to be posted, the platform(s) on which it will appear, and the purpose
3. The patient must sign and date the authorization before the content is captured or posted
4. A general treatment consent form does not satisfy this requirement
5. Authorization is revocable — if a patient revokes in writing, remove the content promptly

**Before/after photographs** are PHI. Clinical before/after images used for marketing or social media require a separate written authorization from the treatment consent form. The authorization must specifically reference the marketing or social media use.

### 5. Responding to Patient Comments

Patients and members of the public may post comments on the clinic's social media pages, tag the clinic in posts, or share their care experiences publicly. When responding:

- **Acknowledge the comment without confirming or adding PHI.** A response such as "Thank you for reaching out — please contact us directly at [phone number] so we can assist you" is appropriate.
- **Never confirm that a person is or was a patient.** Even confirming a patient relationship is a disclosure under 45 CFR §164.502.
- **Never respond to complaints about care in a public thread.** Move all clinical concerns to a private channel: phone, secure message, or in-person conversation.
- **Do not delete negative reviews** as the sole response. Suppressing a review does not resolve a complaint and can generate additional regulatory and reputational exposure.

### 6. Account Access Controls

The clinic's social media accounts (Facebook, Instagram, X/Twitter, LinkedIn, and any other platform) may only be accessed and posted to by designated staff. Account access is managed as follows:

- The Privacy Officer or their designee maintains an up-to-date list of staff with posting access
- Shared account credentials are stored in the clinic's approved password management system — not in personal email or text messages
- Access is revoked on or before an employee's last day of employment
- Two-factor authentication is enabled on all clinic social media accounts
- No personal devices may be used to maintain permanent login sessions for clinic accounts

Any staff member who discovers unauthorized account access must report it to the Privacy Officer within one business day.

### 7. Enforcement and Sanctions

Violations of this policy are subject to sanctions under [CLINIC NAME]'s workforce discipline policy, up to and including termination of employment or contract. Violations that constitute impermissible disclosures of PHI will be documented, assessed under the clinic's breach risk assessment process, and reported as required under 45 CFR §164.400–414.

Staff who are unsure whether a proposed post complies with this policy must ask the Privacy Officer before posting.

### 8. Annual Review

This policy will be reviewed at least annually by the Privacy Officer and updated to reflect changes in HIPAA guidance, clinic operations, and social media platform practices.
