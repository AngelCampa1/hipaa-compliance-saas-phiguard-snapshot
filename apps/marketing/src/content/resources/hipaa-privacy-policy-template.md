## Who Needs This Template

Small medical clinics are required by the HIPAA Privacy Rule to have written privacy policies and procedures. This is not a recommendation — 45 CFR §164.530(i) requires covered entities to implement policies and procedures necessary to comply with the Privacy Rule and to document them in writing. A clinic operating on informal practices and institutional memory, rather than written policies, is out of compliance.

Practice administrators at clinics with two to fifty staff typically handle privacy policy development alongside billing, credentialing, and operations. This template gives you a starting point that covers the administrative structure required under §164.530 without requiring a compliance attorney to draft from scratch.

## What HIPAA Rule It Satisfies

45 CFR §164.530 is the administrative backbone of the Privacy Rule. It requires covered entities to designate a Privacy Officer, implement workforce training, apply sanctions for violations, provide a process for patients and workforce members to file complaints, and retain documentation for six years.

This template addresses each of those requirements in a policy format your staff can follow and your Privacy Officer can enforce.

## What Is Inside This Template

**Policy header.** Effective date, version number, policy owner (Privacy Officer), scope (all workforce members and contractors with PHI access), and the legal authority section citing 45 CFR §§164.502–164.530.

**Privacy Officer designation.** Name, title, contact information, and defined responsibilities: policy development, workforce training oversight, complaint response, and regulatory correspondence. This section creates the documentation required by §164.530(a).

**Permitted uses and disclosures.** An internal-facing summary of when staff may use and disclose PHI — for treatment, payment, and health care operations — and when they may not without authorization. Includes examples appropriate to small clinic operations.

**Minimum necessary standard.** Procedures requiring staff to limit access to the minimum PHI necessary for the task, consistent with 45 CFR §164.502(b). Includes role-specific guidance for front desk, clinical, and billing staff.

**Workforce training requirements.** Training schedule (initial and annual), documentation requirements, and the training log format. Meets §164.530(b).

**Sanctions policy.** Graduated disciplinary consequences for privacy violations, from written warning through termination, including the obligation to report certain violations externally. Required by §164.530(e). This section is referenced in the employee acknowledgment form — see the [HIPAA employee acknowledgement template](/resources/hipaa-employee-acknowledgement-template).

**Complaint procedures.** How workforce members report concerns, how patients file complaints, how the Privacy Officer investigates, and where patients can file with HHS OCR (www.hhs.gov/ocr/complaints). Required by §164.530(d).

**Documentation and retention.** All policies, procedures, training records, and complaint responses must be retained for six years from creation or last effective date. §164.530(j).

**Amendment procedures.** How the policy is updated, approved, and communicated to workforce.

## How to Fill It Out

**Step 1.** Assign your Privacy Officer. Fill in the name and contact fields in Section 1. If the role is currently vacant or unnamed, assigning it is the first compliance action required before this policy can take effect.

**Step 2.** Tailor the permitted uses and minimum-necessary sections to your specific practice type. A behavioral health practice has different default disclosure rules than a primary care clinic — particularly for psychotherapy notes and sensitive categories of PHI.

**Step 3.** Set your training schedule. Initial training for all current workforce on the effective date; training for new hires before PHI access. Annual refresher on a fixed calendar date.

**Step 4.** Have the Privacy Officer and your clinic administrator review and sign the completed policy before distributing to workforce.

**Step 5.** Distribute to all workforce and obtain signed acknowledgments. Keep acknowledgments in personnel files for six years.

**Step 6.** File the policy in your compliance program binder or document management system. Review annually and update after any regulatory change or significant operational shift.

## Who Owns It, Where to Store It, and When to Update It

**Who fills it out:** The Privacy Officer, with input from the practice administrator. A compliance consultant or healthcare attorney should review before the policy takes effect. The Privacy Officer signs the approved version.

**Where to store it:** The written policy must be retained as a compliance document for six years from the date of creation or the last date it was in effect, per 45 CFR §164.530(j). Keep the current version in the compliance program binder and in the document management system your workforce can access. Superseded versions must also be retained for six years.

**How often it is reviewed:** At minimum, annually. Most compliance programs tie the review to a fixed calendar date — first quarter of each year is common — so it does not drift.

**What triggers a required update:** Under 45 CFR §164.530(i)(2), covered entities must revise policies and procedures as necessary and as promptly as practicable after a change in law, regulation, or practice that materially affects the policy. Specific triggers include: an OCR enforcement action or guidance letter that identifies a required policy element; a change in your clinic's structure (new location, new service line, change in vendor relationships); a change in state health privacy law; or a significant security incident that reveals a gap in the written procedures.
