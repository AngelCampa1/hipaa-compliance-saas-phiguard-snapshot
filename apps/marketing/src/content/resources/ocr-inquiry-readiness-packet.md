## Set Up the Binder Before You Need It

The worst time to organize your HIPAA compliance documentation is after you receive an OCR complaint letter. By then, you're under time pressure, distracted by whatever triggered the complaint, and trying to locate documents scattered across email threads, shared drives, and filing cabinets.

Assemble the evidence binder described in this packet during a calm planning period, not during an active investigation. Once set up, it takes 30-60 minutes per year to maintain. When an OCR inquiry arrives, you open the binder and begin producing documents instead of reconstructing them.

The guidance in this packet is general information about OCR processes and compliance documentation. It is not legal advice. Consult qualified legal counsel before responding to any OCR communication.

## Part 1: The Evidence Binder Structure

Organize your HIPAA compliance documentation in a binder (physical or digital) with these seven sections. Each section corresponds to a category OCR commonly requests.

**Section 1: Policies and Procedures**

Contents:
- Current Privacy Policy (with version history)
- Current Security Policy (with version history)
- Notice of Privacy Practices (NPP) — current version, and prior versions with effective dates
- Breach Notification Policy
- Sanction Policy
- Workforce Security and Access Policy
- Minimum Necessary Policy
- Device and Media Policy
- All other HIPAA-required policies

Filing convention: Each policy should show the document title, effective date, version number, and the Privacy Officer's signature. Prior versions are retained but clearly marked as superseded.

**Section 2: Training Records**

Contents:
- Annual training logs for each calendar year (workforce member name, date, method, completion status, verifier)
- New hire training records (date, method, documentation)
- Refresher and role-specific training records
- Training curriculum used (or reference to LMS records)

This section should demonstrate continuous training, not a single training event from three years ago.

**Section 3: Risk Analysis and Risk Management**

Contents:
- Current risk analysis document (most recent annual analysis or update)
- Prior risk analyses (retain all versions)
- Risk management plan — what remediation steps were taken as a result of each risk analysis
- Documentation of any mid-year risk analysis updates triggered by environmental changes

A risk analysis without a corresponding risk management plan is incomplete. OCR will ask what you did with the findings.

**Section 4: Business Associate Agreements (BAAs)**

Contents:
- Executed BAA for every current business associate
- BAA expiration tracking log
- Vendor inventory list (all vendors with BAA status noted)
- Terminated vendor BAAs (retain for six years after termination)

**Section 5: Incident and Breach Records**

Contents:
- Incident log (date, description, triage outcome, disposition)
- For each breach: intake form, 4-factor risk assessment, determination memo, notification records, HHS filing
- Security event log (minor incidents that were determined to be non-breach)
- Annual breach summary submitted to HHS for breaches affecting under 500 individuals

**Section 6: Access Review and Workforce Records**

Contents:
- Quarterly access review logs (current and prior year)
- Role-based access matrix
- Minimum necessary decision log
- Offboarding checklists for terminated staff (retain for six years)
- New hire onboarding compliance checklists

**Section 7: Physical and Technical Safeguard Documentation**

Contents:
- Device and workstation inventory with encryption status
- Physical security inspection records
- Facility access control documentation (keys/badges issued)
- Audit log review records (documentation that you regularly review EHR access logs)
- Network and system security documentation (relevant for the Security Rule)

## Part 2: 24-Hour Preservation Checklist

When an OCR complaint or investigation notice arrives, the first 24 hours matter. Your preservation actions — or failures to act — become part of the investigation record.

**Immediate (within the first hour):**
- [ ] Do not delete, modify, or overwrite any records related to the complaint subject matter
- [ ] Notify clinic leadership and legal counsel (or begin identifying legal counsel if not retained)
- [ ] Identify the Privacy Officer as the primary response point of contact
- [ ] Preserve all email communications related to the incident or complaint subject — do not delete, auto-archive, or purge
- [ ] Preserve access logs, audit logs, and system logs for the time period relevant to the complaint

**Within 24 hours:**
- [ ] Pull the evidence binder — confirm its completeness
- [ ] Identify any gaps in the binder that will need to be addressed
- [ ] Preserve physical records related to the complaint (do not destroy, even if scheduled for routine destruction)
- [ ] If the complaint involves a specific patient: pull that patient's record and place a hold on its modification or deletion
- [ ] If the complaint involves a specific staff member: pull access logs for that account
- [ ] Notify relevant staff that documentation related to the period of the complaint is subject to a hold

**Do not:**
- Do not attempt to recreate or backdate documents
- Do not contact the complainant directly without legal guidance
- Do not attempt to negotiate or resolve the matter directly with the patient or former employee without counsel
- Do not respond to OCR's initial inquiry letter without counsel review

## Part 3: Response Roles

**Privacy Officer:**
- Primary contact for OCR communications
- Coordinates document gathering from all clinic departments
- Maintains the investigation log (what was requested, when, what was provided)
- Briefs legal counsel on the factual record
- Does not speak to OCR without legal counsel review in most circumstances

**Clinic Administrator / Practice Manager:**
- Supports document retrieval for the Privacy Officer
- Manages staff communication (when asked about the investigation, staff should direct all questions to the Privacy Officer)
- Manages operational continuity during the investigation

**Legal Counsel:**
- Reviews OCR correspondence before any response is submitted
- Advises on whether to cooperate with a specific document request, seek a protective order, or challenge the scope
- Drafts or reviews all written submissions to OCR
- Manages privilege considerations for internal communications about the investigation
- Advises on penalty exposure and settlement considerations

**Providers:**
- Should be briefed that an investigation is underway
- Should not independently contact OCR or the complainant
- Should direct any OCR communications (letters, phone calls) to the Privacy Officer immediately

## Part 4: What OCR Commonly Requests in the First 30 Days

OCR's initial document request covers a subset of the evidence binder. Based on OCR's published audit protocol, common first-round requests include:

1. Your most recent risk analysis and risk management plan
2. Your privacy and security policies and procedures — with effective dates
3. Documentation of workforce training — current and prior year
4. The BAA for the vendor or relationship referenced in the complaint (if complaint-specific)
5. The Notice of Privacy Practices and documentation of how it's provided to patients
6. Documentation of the specific incident referenced in the complaint — incident log, any reports filed

**For breach-triggered investigations:**
- The breach report filed with HHS
- The notification letters sent to affected individuals
- Documentation of the 4-factor risk assessment
- Any business associate notification (if a BA was involved)

**For access-related complaints:**
- Access control policies
- User access logs for the individual named in the complaint
- Role matrix for the role at issue
- Offboarding records if a former employee is involved

## What PHIGuard Changes

The evidence binder described in this packet exists inside PHIGuard as a structured compliance record: policies, training logs, risk analysis documents, BAAs, incident records, access reviews, and offboarding records all in one place with timestamps and version history. When an OCR inquiry arrives, the investigation log starts as a task in PHIGuard. Document production is a filter and export operation, not a scavenger hunt.

The 6-year retention requirement under §164.530(j) is met automatically. PHIGuard retains all compliance records for the required period without manual archiving.
