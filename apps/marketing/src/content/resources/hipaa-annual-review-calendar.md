## Why a Calendar Beats a Checklist

A compliance checklist tells you what needs to happen. A compliance calendar tells you when, who, and what you have to show for it. Most small clinic compliance programs fail not because the practice administrator doesn't know that a risk analysis is required, but because nothing makes "risk analysis" a concrete event on the calendar with a deadline and an owner.

The HIPAA Security Rule is structured around periodic review requirements. Under 45 CFR §164.308, covered entities must periodically evaluate their administrative safeguards, assess security incidents, review workforce access, and train staff. The regulation uses the word "periodically" without specifying months. This calendar translates "periodically" into specific months, quarterly blocks, and named tasks.

## How to Use This Calendar

Copy this calendar into whatever planning system your clinic uses: a shared document, a task management tool, or a physical binder in the administrator's office. The format is a month-by-month table with four columns:

| Month | Task | Owner | Evidence artifact |
|---|---|---|---|

Complete the Owner column before the calendar year begins. If a task has no owner, it will not get done. If a task belongs to a vendor or external party, name the vendor relationship manager accountable for tracking it.

Identify the evidence artifact before the task is due. Knowing that the access review produces a "signed access review log dated [month]" lets the administrator set up the artifact template before the review happens, rather than scrambling during an audit.

## The 12-Month Calendar

### January

**Annual HIPAA Security Rule risk analysis** (or review of existing risk analysis for triggering events in the prior year). Owner: Privacy/Security Officer. Evidence: Signed risk analysis document or risk analysis review memo.

**Annual workforce HIPAA training — schedule and notify.** January is the time to schedule the year's required training, not to complete it. If your program runs annual training in Q1, schedule it now. Owner: Privacy Officer. Evidence: Training calendar invitation record.

**BAA inventory — pull the full list.** January is the right time to reconcile your BAA inventory against your current vendor list. Which BAAs are expiring this year Which vendors were added in the prior year without a BAA review Owner: Privacy Officer. Evidence: Updated BAA inventory log.

### February

**Annual workforce HIPAA training — complete.** If scheduled for Q1, February is the completion target. Deliver privacy and security training to all workforce members including contractors. Document completion. Owner: Privacy Officer. Evidence: Training log with dates, attendees, method, and verifier signatures.

**Sanctions log review.** Review the sanctions log for the prior 12 months. Were all incidents logged Were sanctions applied consistently Was documentation filed Owner: Security Officer. Evidence: Sanctions log review memo.

### March

**Access review — quarterly (Q1).** Review all ePHI system access grants against current workforce roles. Remove excess access. Document changes. Owner: Security Officer. Evidence: Signed access review log, dated March.

**Policy review — Privacy Policies.** Annual review of at least one major policy category. March: privacy policies (NPP, minimum necessary, patient rights). Owner: Privacy Officer. Evidence: Policy review memo and updated policy dates.

### April

**Vendor BAA renewal check.** Identify all BAAs expiring in the next 90 days. Initiate renewal conversations. Owner: Privacy Officer. Evidence: Renewal tracking log entries.

**Physical security review.** Inspect all locations where PHI is stored or accessed: locked file cabinets, server rooms, records storage, fax machines. Confirm access controls are functioning. Owner: Security Officer. Evidence: Physical security inspection form.

### May

**Device and workstation audit.** Confirm all clinic devices are encrypted, have screen locks enabled, and are running current OS and antivirus. Document any unpatched or unsecured devices. Owner: Security Officer. Evidence: Device inventory with encryption and patch status.

**Incident log review — Q1.** Review all security incidents logged in Q1. Were all incidents triaged Were breach determinations made and documented Were HHS notifications filed as required Owner: Security Officer. Evidence: Incident review memo.

### June

**Access review — quarterly (Q2).** Second quarterly access review. Owner: Security Officer. Evidence: Signed access review log, dated June.

**Policy review — Security Policies.** Annual review continued: security policies (workstation use, device management, access control). Owner: Security Officer. Evidence: Policy review memo and updated policy dates.

**Mid-year compliance meeting.** Half-year review with practice leadership: open items, upcoming obligations, any incidents or findings from H1. Owner: Privacy Officer. Evidence: Meeting agenda and notes.

### July

**BAA renewal execution.** Any BAAs expiring in Q3 should be renewed or terminated this month. Owner: Privacy Officer. Evidence: Updated BAA inventory with executed renewal dates.

**Risk analysis review — mid-year triggers.** Has the clinic had a significant change since January New vendor, new system, new physical location, major staffing change If yes, a risk analysis review is indicated. Owner: Security Officer. Evidence: Risk analysis review memo or updated risk analysis.

### August

**Workforce training refresher (if applicable).** Some clinics run an annual training in Q1 and a brief refresher in Q3. If your program uses a refresher model, August is the month. Owner: Privacy Officer. Evidence: Refresher training log.

**Business continuity and backup verification.** Test backup restoration for at least one ePHI system. Confirm contingency plan contacts are current. Owner: Security Officer. Evidence: Backup test result log.

### September

**Access review — quarterly (Q3).** Third quarterly access review. Owner: Security Officer. Evidence: Signed access review log, dated September.

**Policy review — Incident Response and Breach Notification Policies.** Owner: Security Officer. Evidence: Policy review memo.

**Incident log review — Q2/Q3.** Review all incidents from April through September. Owner: Security Officer. Evidence: Incident review memo.

### October

**Annual risk analysis — initiate for next year.** If your program runs risk analysis in Q4 rather than Q1, initiate the process now. Owner: Privacy Officer/Security Officer. Evidence: Risk analysis work plan.

**Vendor review — assess new vendors added in H2.** Did the clinic add new vendors since July Were BAAs executed Are new vendors documented in the inventory Owner: Privacy Officer. Evidence: Updated vendor and BAA inventory.

### November

**Breach notification review.** Any breach notification obligations triggered in the prior 12 months Were all notifications sent on time (60-day rule for individuals; annual report if <500 individuals) Owner: Privacy Officer. Evidence: Breach notification log or "no breach" notation.

**BAA renewal check — Q4.** Any BAAs expiring in Q1 of next year Start renewal conversations now rather than letting them lapse. Owner: Privacy Officer. Evidence: Renewal tracking log.

### December

**Access review — quarterly (Q4).** Final quarterly access review of the year. Owner: Security Officer. Evidence: Signed access review log, dated December.

**Policy review — all remaining policies.** Complete any policy reviews not completed in Q1–Q3. Owner: Privacy Officer. Evidence: Policy review memos.

**Annual compliance meeting — year-end.** Review the full year: training completion, incidents, breaches (if any), vendor status, risk findings, unresolved items. Set priorities for next year. Owner: Privacy Officer. Evidence: Annual compliance meeting agenda and notes.

**Calendar rollover — prepare next year's calendar.** Update owner names, confirm cadence adjustments, set reminders in task management system. Owner: Practice Administrator. Evidence: Next year's calendar with owners confirmed.

## What Annual Reviews Commonly Miss

**HHS breach reporting for incidents under 500 individuals.** The covered entity must submit an annual log of breaches affecting fewer than 500 individuals to HHS by March 1 of the following year. This is separate from individual notification. Many small clinics miss this because the 60-day individual notification was completed and the obligation appeared finished. Add it to January as a pre-task for the March 1 deadline.

**BAA renewals after a vendor is acquired.** If a vendor is acquired by another company, the BAA with the original entity may not automatically carry over to the acquiring entity. The asset-purchase exception under HIPAA is narrow. When a vendor notifies you of an acquisition, treat it as a BAA review trigger.

**Software updates as risk analysis triggers.** A significant update to the EHR — especially an update that changes how data is stored, transmitted, or accessed — is an environmental change that may trigger a risk analysis review. Annual-only risk analysis misses mid-year changes.

**Contractor and locum training lapse.** Annual training records show all W-2 staff completed training. The locum who covered for a month never received training, was never added to the log, and the gap surfaces during an investigation.
