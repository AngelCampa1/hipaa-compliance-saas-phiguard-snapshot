## Who Needs This Guide

Practice administrators at small medical clinics spend more time reviewing vendor-drafted BAAs than they spend drafting their own. Every EHR vendor, billing service, IT managed service provider, and cloud storage platform presents their own agreement. Knowing what to require — and what to reject — protects your clinic before a vendor relationship begins.

This resource is the clinic-side complement to the [PHIGuard BAA template](/resources/baa-template), which gives you a full contract to present to vendors when they do not have their own. Use both: present your template when the vendor has nothing, and use this guide when the vendor hands you theirs.

## What HIPAA Rule It Satisfies

45 CFR §164.504(e) requires covered entities to have a written BAA with every business associate before the associate creates, receives, maintains, or transmits PHI on the clinic's behalf. The rule specifies minimum contract provisions. A BAA missing any of them provides no compliance protection regardless of what the vendor verbally represented.

The Security Rule adds parallel requirements. 45 CFR §164.308(b)(1)–(4) requires covered entities to obtain satisfactory assurances in writing that the business associate will implement appropriate administrative, physical, and technical safeguards for any ePHI it creates, receives, maintains, or transmits on the covered entity's behalf. 45 CFR §164.314(a)(1)–(2) specifies the minimum content those written assurances must contain, including the requirement that the BA implement and document Security Rule safeguards and report Security Incidents to the covered entity. Together, §164.504(e) and §§164.308(b)/164.314(a) define what a fully compliant BAA must cover.

OCR enforces these rules against covered entities, not just business associates. If your vendor was hacked and you cannot produce a valid executed BAA covering both the Privacy Rule and Security Rule obligations, you are the entity facing investigation.

## What Is Inside This Resource

**Part 1 — Required provisions checklist.** A line-by-line review guide for every element required in a fully compliant BAA. This includes the Privacy Rule required provisions of §164.504(e)(2) and the Security Rule required provisions of §§164.308(b)(1)–(4) and 164.314(a)(1)–(2). Bring this checklist to your desk when a vendor sends their agreement.

The required provisions are:

- Permitted uses and disclosures limited to those necessary to perform services (§164.504(e)(2)(i))
- Prohibition on uses or disclosures not permitted by the agreement (§164.504(e)(2)(i))
- Obligation to implement appropriate safeguards — for ePHI, the BAA must require the BA to comply with the applicable requirements of the Security Rule (§§164.308(b)(1), 164.314(a)(2)(i))
- Reporting obligations: breaches of unsecured PHI under §164.410, and Security Incidents, without unreasonable delay (§§164.504(e)(2)(ii)(C), 164.314(a)(2)(i)(C))
- Subcontractor flow-down: the BA must obtain BAA-equivalent assurances from subcontractors that handle ePHI (§§164.504(e)(2)(ii)(D), 164.308(b)(3)–(4))
- Individual rights access: amendments, accounting of disclosures, access to a Designated Record Set (§164.504(e)(2)(ii)(E)–(G))
- HHS inspection access (§164.504(e)(2)(ii)(H))
- Return or destruction of PHI on termination (§164.504(e)(2)(ii)(J))

**Part 2 — Red-flag clause guide.** Provisions that shift compliance risk inappropriately onto your clinic:

- Breach notification windows that rely on the full 60-day regulatory maximum. Under 45 CFR §164.410, a business associate must notify the covered entity of a breach without unreasonable delay and no later than 60 calendar days after discovery. That is the ceiling, not the target. Push for 5–10 business days for a small-clinic relationship where breach response can be fast.
- "Best efforts" language replacing specific safeguard obligations. Best efforts is not a standard. Require compliance with 45 CFR §§164.308, 164.310, and 164.312 by name, as mandated by §164.314(a)(2)(i).
- Indemnification limited to direct damages only, excluding regulatory fines. Civil monetary penalties are your exposure — make sure the vendor's indemnification covers them.
- No data return or destruction clause on termination. Any BAA that is silent on return or destruction means the vendor may retain your patients' data indefinitely after you leave.
- Subcontractor list "available on request" with no obligation to notify you of changes. Require affirmative notice within 30 days of any subcontractor change that touches PHI.

**Part 3 — BAA tracker template.** A spreadsheet structure for maintaining your executed agreement inventory, including: vendor name, services provided, PHI categories, execution date, last review date, expiration or next review trigger, and agreement status.

## How to Use This Guide

**When a vendor sends their BAA:**

1. Open the required-provisions checklist and mark each provision as present, absent, or deficient.
2. Flag any red-flag clauses and prepare written questions before signing.
3. Negotiate in writing — email is fine. Document what the vendor agreed to and whether they modified their standard terms.
4. Store the final executed agreement in a centralized location, not in your email inbox.
5. Add a row to your BAA tracker with the execution date and a review reminder.

**On annual review:**

Pull your tracker and review every active relationship. Have any services changed Has the vendor added new subcontractors Is the BAA still linked to an active services agreement If the vendor was acquired, you may need to execute a new agreement with the successor entity.
