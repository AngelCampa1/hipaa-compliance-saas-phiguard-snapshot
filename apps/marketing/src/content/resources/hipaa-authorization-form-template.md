## Who Needs This Template

Medical clinics need a HIPAA authorization form any time they intend to use or disclose PHI in a way that falls outside the standard treatment, payment, and health care operations exceptions. The most common situations for small clinics are: sharing PHI with a patient's employer, insurer, or attorney at the patient's direction; disclosing psychotherapy notes (which have their own category under §164.508(a)(2)); participating in any clinical research involving patient data; and any marketing communication that constitutes a use of PHI.

If your clinic uses a single release form for every records request, this template adds the additional elements required specifically for these restricted disclosure categories.

## What HIPAA Rule It Satisfies

45 CFR §164.508 establishes when a covered entity must obtain a patient's written authorization before using or disclosing PHI. The rule requires authorization for:

- Any disclosure of psychotherapy notes, with narrow exceptions (§164.508(a)(2))
- Most marketing communications using PHI (§164.508(a)(3))
- Any sale of PHI (§164.508(a)(4))
- Research disclosures where a waiver has not been granted by an IRB or Privacy Board

For general patient records releases, the [HIPAA release form template](/resources/hipaa-release-form-template) covers the standard authorization elements. This template adds the category-specific provisions required for the restricted disclosure types above.

## What Is Inside This Template

**Disclosure category selection.** The form opens with checkboxes identifying the category of use or disclosure: psychotherapy notes, marketing, sale of PHI, research, or other. Each category triggers additional required language.

**Eight core authorization elements (§164.508(b)(1)).** The template includes all required elements:

1. Description of the information to be used or disclosed, identified specifically
2. Name or specific identification of the person or class of persons authorized to make the disclosure
3. Name or specific identification of the person or class of persons to whom the disclosure may be made
4. Description of each purpose of the requested use or disclosure
5. Expiration date or event
6. Patient signature and date (or personal representative signature with authority documentation)
7. Right to revoke statement, with any exceptions applicable to the category
8. Ability to condition treatment, enrollment, or eligibility on the authorization (and whether the clinic is doing so)

**Category-specific addenda.**

For psychotherapy notes: A statement that this disclosure is from the psychotherapy notes of the treating provider, and that the authorization may be revoked at any time. Important: under §164.508(a)(2), psychotherapy notes require a separate authorization even from the treating provider themselves — the treating clinician cannot use or disclose psychotherapy notes for most purposes (including sharing with other treating providers or for payment) without a specific written authorization. This is stricter than the treatment/payment/operations exception that applies to other PHI. The addendum in this template includes the required psychotherapy-specific language and a reminder to keep psychotherapy note authorizations in a separate filing location from general records release authorizations.

For marketing: A disclosure of whether the covered entity is receiving financial remuneration from a third party whose products or services are described in the marketing communication.

For sale of PHI: A statement that the disclosure will result in remuneration to the covered entity.

For research: An IRB waiver reference field, or a full §164.508 authorization with research-specific purpose language.

**Revocation form.** Detachable revocation document that the patient can submit in writing at any time.

**Authorization tracking log.** A structured record of executed authorizations with columns for patient identifier (no PHI in the log name), category, execution date, expiration date, and revocation date if applicable.

## How to Fill It Out

**Step 1.** Identify the disclosure category and select the appropriate form version. Do not use the general release form for psychotherapy notes or marketing disclosures.

**Step 2.** Complete the disclosure description with specificity. "All mental health records" is less defensible than "individual psychotherapy session notes from [date range] with [treating provider]."

**Step 3.** Confirm the expiration date or event. For research authorizations, the expiration is often "end of the research study." Document the study name and IRB number.

**Step 4.** Review the conditioning statement. If your clinic will not condition treatment on the authorization, check "No" in that field and ensure staff know not to make treatment contingent.

**Step 5.** Have the patient sign in the presence of a staff member where possible. Retain the original; provide a copy to the patient on request.

**Step 6.** Log the authorization in the tracking register. Set a calendar reminder for the expiration date.

## Who Fills It Out, Where to Store It, and When to Update It

**Who fills it out:** Clinical or medical records staff, in collaboration with the patient or their authorized representative. For psychotherapy note authorizations and research authorizations, the treating clinician or research coordinator should confirm the specific description of records before the patient signs.

**Where to store it:** Retain the original signed authorization in the designated record set for six years from the date of creation or the date it was last in effect, per 45 CFR §164.530(j). Psychotherapy note authorizations should be stored in a separate, restricted-access location from general records. Log each executed authorization in the authorization tracking register.

**How often it is reviewed:** Review the form template annually and any time a new disclosure category (e.g., research protocol, new marketing use) requires customization. The category-specific addenda should also be reviewed when OCR publishes updated guidance on psychotherapy notes, research, or marketing authorizations.

**What triggers a required update:** Any change to 45 CFR §164.508, a state law amendment affecting the required authorization content, a new research protocol requiring IRB-specific language, or a court finding invalidating a specific form element requires an immediate update to the relevant version of the template. Authorizations already executed under a prior compliant form remain valid.
