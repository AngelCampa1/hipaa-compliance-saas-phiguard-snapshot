## What OCR Looks For — and What Most Small Clinics Are Missing

The Office for Civil Rights audits covered entities using a protocol that maps directly to the HIPAA Security Rule's implementation specifications. When OCR investigates a breach or conducts an audit, the first question is whether the covered entity has a documented, active compliance program that addresses each safeguard category. The investigation then drills into specific gaps.

The gap analysis scorecard in this PDF gives your clinic a structured way to conduct that investigation on yourself — before OCR does. It covers every implementation specification in the Security Rule, uses a weighted scoring system that distinguishes between required and addressable specifications, and produces a prioritized output that tells you not just what is missing but in what order to address it.

Small clinics typically face the same set of gaps: no formal risk analysis, no documented security incident procedures, workforce access permissions that have never been formally reviewed, and backup systems that have never been tested for restoration. The scorecard makes these patterns visible so they can be addressed systematically.

This is a downloadable PDF that your Privacy Officer or practice administrator can complete in approximately two hours. It does not require a compliance consultant. It does require honest answers.

## What's in the PDF

### Part 1 — Administrative Safeguards (45 CFR § 164.308)

The administrative safeguards section covers all eight implementation specifications. For each specification, the scorecard presents 3–5 yes/no questions. Required specifications are marked R; addressable specifications are marked A with a note on the standard for compliance.

**1. Security Management Process — § 164.308(a)(1)**

The security management process encompasses two required specifications: risk analysis (§ 164.308(a)(1)(ii)(A)) and risk management (§ 164.308(a)(1)(ii)(B)).

Scorecard questions:
- Has the organization completed a documented risk analysis within the past 12 months or following any material change (R)
- Does the risk register document identified risks with likelihood and impact scoring (R)
- Are risk management actions assigned to owners with target dates (R)
- Have high-priority remediations from the most recent risk analysis been addressed (R)

**2. Assigned Security Responsibility — § 164.308(a)(2)**

A single individual must be designated responsible for developing and implementing security policies and procedures.

Scorecard questions:
- Is there a named Security Officer with documented responsibility (R)
- Has this designation been communicated to all workforce members (A)

**3. Workforce Security — § 164.308(a)(3)**

Workforce security covers authorization and supervision (A), workforce clearance procedures (A), and termination procedures (A).

Scorecard questions:
- Are access levels tied to job functions with documented authorization criteria (A)
- Are access permissions reviewed at hire and when role changes occur (A)
- Does the termination procedure include same-day or next-business-day access revocation (A)

**4. Information Access Management — § 164.308(a)(4)**

This specification covers access authorization (A), access establishment and modification (A), and the specific requirement for health care clearinghouses to isolate functions (R where applicable).

Scorecard questions:
- Is access to ePHI restricted to workforce members with a documented need (A)
- Are access permissions reviewed on at least an annual basis (A)
- Is there a documented process for granting, modifying, and revoking access (A)

**5. Security Awareness and Training — § 164.308(a)(5)**

Training covers security reminders (A), protection from malicious software (A), log-in monitoring (A), and password management (A).

Scorecard questions:
- Have all workforce members completed HIPAA security training in the past 12 months (A)
- Does training include protection from malicious software and phishing recognition (A)
- Is training completion documented and retained per 45 CFR § 164.530(j) (A)

**6. Security Incident Procedures — § 164.308(a)(6)**

A required implementation specification: the organization must identify and respond to security incidents and mitigate harmful effects.

Scorecard questions:
- Is there a documented security incident response plan (R)
- Do all workforce members know how to report a suspected incident (R)
- Are incidents logged with investigation notes and outcome documentation (R)

**7. Contingency Plan — § 164.308(a)(7)**

Contingency planning covers data backup (R), disaster recovery (R), emergency mode operation (R), testing and revision procedures (A), and applications and data criticality analysis (A).

Scorecard questions:
- Is there a documented data backup plan with a tested backup (R)
- Is there a disaster recovery plan that addresses ePHI restoration (R)
- Has the disaster recovery plan been tested in the past 12 months (A)
- Is there an emergency mode operation plan for continuing operations during a security incident (R)

**8. Evaluation — § 164.308(a)(8)**

Organizations must perform periodic technical and non-technical evaluations of their security program in response to environmental or operational changes.

Scorecard questions:
- Has a formal security program evaluation been conducted in the past 12 months (R)
- Have evaluations been conducted after any significant system change, workforce change, or incident (R)

### Part 2 — Physical Safeguards (45 CFR § 164.310)

Physical safeguards cover facility access controls, workstation use, workstation security, and device and media controls. The scorecard presents yes/no questions for each, weighted by the risk profile of a small ambulatory practice.

### Part 3 — Technical Safeguards (45 CFR § 164.312)

Technical safeguards cover access control (unique user identification, emergency access, automatic logoff, encryption), audit controls, integrity, person or entity authentication, and transmission security. These specifications generate the highest number of critical findings in small practices because they often require technical configuration that was set at installation and never reviewed.

### Scoring and Output

Each yes answer contributes points toward the section total. Required implementation specifications carry higher weights than addressable specifications. The scorecard produces:

- A section score for each of the three safeguard categories (administrative, physical, technical)
- An overall program score
- A color-coded risk level for each section and overall
- A ranked remediation list sorted by risk weight of the gap multiplied by the inverse of estimated remediation effort (high-risk, low-effort items first)

## Regulatory Basis

The HIPAA Security Rule administrative safeguards at 45 CFR § 164.308 are organized into required and addressable implementation specifications. Required specifications must be implemented as written. Addressable specifications must be implemented if reasonable and appropriate, and if not implemented, the organization must document why not and what alternative measure was implemented. This distinction is built into the scorecard's weighting.

NIST SP 800-66 Rev. 2 ("Implementing the HIPAA Security Rule: A Cybersecurity Resource Guide"), published January 2023, provides the authoritative methodology for Security Rule implementation and is the primary reference for the scorecard's question design.
