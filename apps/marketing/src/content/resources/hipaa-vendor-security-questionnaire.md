## Why Signing a BAA Is Not the Same as Vendor Due Diligence

The HIPAA business associate framework requires a signed BAA before a vendor handles your PHI. But a BAA is a contract, not an assurance. A vendor can sign a BAA and have a security program that falls well short of what the Security Rule requires. The BAA creates a legal obligation and allocates liability; it does not guarantee that the vendor's systems are secure or that they can actually fulfill the obligations they are agreeing to.

Effective vendor due diligence starts before the BAA is signed. It uses a structured questionnaire to gather objective information about the vendor's security program, the certifications they hold, the terms they are willing to accept, and the specific ways they handle customer data. The information gathered in the questionnaire informs what the BAA needs to say and surfaces risks that can be addressed through contractual terms, operational controls, or a decision not to proceed.

45 CFR § 164.308(b) requires covered entities to have a written contract with each business associate that provides satisfactory assurances that the business associate will appropriately safeguard PHI. A questionnaire-driven due diligence process is how a covered entity establishes those assurances before signing.

This questionnaire is a downloadable PDF with 30 questions organized into seven categories. It is designed to be sent directly to vendor compliance or security teams. It includes guidance on interpreting responses, a scoring rubric for comparing vendors, and notes on when specific answers are disqualifying.

## What's in the PDF

### Category 1 — BAA Terms (8 questions)

**Q1.** Do you offer a HIPAA Business Associate Agreement, and is it available on the pricing tier we would be using If a BAA is only available at a higher tier, what pricing tier is required?

**Q2.** Who at your organization signs the BAA Is it executed by a company officer or legal representative with authority to bind the company?

**Q3.** Does your standard BAA accept incoming BAAs from covered entities, or do you require covered entities to use your template If you require your template, are the terms negotiable?

**Q4.** What uses and disclosures of PHI does your BAA permit on your own behalf (HIPAA permits business associates to use PHI for their own management, legal, and operations purposes — the BAA should specify what those uses are.)

**Q5.** What is your contractual commitment for breach notification to the covered entity following discovery of a breach Is this commitment shorter than 60 days to allow the covered entity to meet its own notification deadline?

**Q6.** Does your BAA grant the covered entity audit rights — the right to verify your security practices and compliance program?

**Q7.** What are the data return and destruction requirements at BAA termination What is your process for certifying that PHI has been returned or destroyed?

**Q8.** If you are breached and PHI is disclosed, does your BAA address indemnification for the covered entity's notification costs and regulatory fines?

### Category 2 — Security Certifications (5 questions)

**Q9.** Do you hold a SOC 2 Type II certification If yes: does it cover the specific product we would be using, what period does the most recent report cover, and can we receive a copy under NDA?

**Q10.** Do you hold ISO 27001 certification If yes, which scope statement does it cover?

**Q11.** Do you hold HITRUST CSF certification If yes, what version, which components, and when does the certification expire?

**Q12.** Have you had a third-party penetration test conducted within the past 12 months If yes, can you share the executive summary?

**Q13.** Do you conduct annual third-party security risk assessments What framework do you use?

### Category 3 — Subprocessors (4 questions)

**Q14.** Do you maintain and publish a list of third-party subprocessors who may access or process customer data If yes, where is that list published?

**Q15.** Are your subprocessors contractually bound by data security and HIPAA-equivalent obligations Are those obligations equivalent to the obligations you accept in a BAA?

**Q16.** Do you notify customers before adding new subprocessors What is the advance notice period, and what is the opt-out mechanism?

**Q17.** Do any subprocessors operate in or route data through jurisdictions outside the United States?

### Category 4 — Data Handling (5 questions)

**Q18.** Is all PHI encrypted at rest using AES-256 or equivalent Who manages the encryption keys — your organization, the customer, or a shared key management service?

**Q19.** Is all PHI encrypted in transit using TLS 1.2 or higher Are older TLS versions or unencrypted connections supported (and can they be disabled)?

**Q20.** Where is PHI stored geographically Can you confirm all storage and processing occurs on U.S. servers?

**Q21.** Do you use customer data to train, fine-tune, or improve machine learning models Is this commitment contractually binding in your BAA or data processing agreement?

**Q22.** What is your data retention policy for customer data following termination of the contract What is the deletion timeline, and how is deletion certified?

### Category 5 — Access Controls (3 questions)

**Q23.** What categories of your employees have access to customer PHI in production environments Is access logged and auditable?

**Q24.** Do you require multi-factor authentication for employee access to production systems containing customer PHI?

**Q25.** Do you conduct background checks on employees who may have access to customer PHI?

### Category 6 — Incident Response (3 questions)

**Q26.** Do you have a documented incident response plan When was it last tested?

**Q27.** In the event of a breach affecting our PHI, who would contact us, in what timeframe, and through what channel?

**Q28.** How many security incidents have you experienced in the past 12 months How many affected customer data (A refusal to answer this question is itself significant.)

### Category 7 — Termination and Data Deletion (2 questions)

**Q29.** Following contract termination, within what period will you return or destroy all PHI What format will returned data be in?

**Q30.** Can you provide a written certification of destruction when PHI is deleted What does the certification include?

### Scoring Rubric

Each category is scored on a 0–4 scale. The scoring rubric assigns points based on the quality and specificity of vendor responses. A category score of 0 or 1 in any category is a flag for BAA negotiation or additional scrutiny. Specific disqualifying responses are identified: any "yes" to using customer data for model training (Q21), any "no" to BAA availability (Q1), and any inability to confirm U.S.-only data residency (Q20) are absolute disqualifiers.

### BAA Negotiation Notes

The final section of the PDF provides guidance on using questionnaire responses in BAA negotiation: which gaps can be addressed through contractual terms, which gaps require operational controls, and when the questionnaire outcome should lead to declining the vendor relationship.

## Regulatory Basis

The business associate framework is at 45 CFR § 164.308(b)(1): covered entities must "obtain satisfactory assurances" from business associates. The Privacy Rule parallel is at 45 CFR § 164.502(e) and 164.504(e). Under the 2013 Omnibus Rule, business associates are directly liable for HIPAA violations and must extend equivalent obligations to subcontractors.

NIST SP 800-66 Rev. 2 recommends vendor due diligence as part of the business associate contracting process and identifies the security elements that should be evaluated before a BAA is executed.
