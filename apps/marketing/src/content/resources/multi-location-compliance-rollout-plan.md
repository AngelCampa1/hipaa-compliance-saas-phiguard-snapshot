## Multi-Location Compliance Is Not Multiple Single-Location Programs

A medical group with three locations needs one organizational compliance program that accounts for the specific circumstances at each location: different staff, different systems, different physical security configurations, potentially different state law requirements.

The most common failure pattern in multi-location rollouts: the central compliance program is designed and documented, and each site is expected to "implement it." Three months later, implementation varies widely because the rollout plan never specified what each site was responsible for, who owned it locally, or what the evidence artifact looked like.

This plan resolves that by separating what is shared (organizational policies, training content, software platform, risk analysis framework) from what must happen locally (access reviews at each site's systems, physical security at each site's facility, local staff training completion, site-specific BAA verification for location-specific vendors).

## Part 1: Site Assessment Matrix

Before beginning the rollout, assess each site's current compliance posture. This assessment identifies the gaps at each location and informs the site-specific rollout priorities.

Complete one column per site. Score each dimension as: **0** = not in place, **1** = partially in place, **2** = fully in place.

Use the total scores to sequence rollout priority. Sites with lower scores need more intensive rollout support.

## Part 2: Shared vs. Site-Specific Framework

### Shared (Centralized) Compliance Activities

These activities are designed and managed at the organizational level. Each site benefits from the central work but does not independently create or maintain these:

**Organizational policies:** The Privacy Policy, Security Policy, NPP, Breach Notification Policy, Sanction Policy, and all other required policies are created and version-controlled at the organizational level. All sites operate under the same policies.

**Training content:** Training curriculum, modules, and assessment materials are developed once at the organizational level. Completion tracking is site-specific (each site logs who completed training), but the content is consistent.

**Risk analysis framework:** The annual risk analysis covers the full organizational scope including all sites. Site-specific risks (a particular location's physical security situation, a specific system used only at one site) are captured within the organizational risk analysis.

**Software and platform:** The compliance management platform is configured at the organizational level. Site-level tasks and records are organized within the same platform.

**BAA management:** Organizational BAAs with vendors used across all sites are executed and managed centrally. Site-specific vendor BAAs (if any site uses a vendor not used organization-wide) are tracked in the central inventory.

### Site-Specific Compliance Activities

These activities must happen at each physical location, because each location has its own staff, systems, and facilities:

**Local training completion:** Each site must log training completion for its own workforce. A training log from Site 1 doesn't cover Site 2 staff.

**Physical security:** Each site has its own physical facility, access controls, records storage, and workstation configurations. Physical security inspections must be conducted at each site.

**Access review:** If each site has its own EHR instance or if user accounts are site-specific, access reviews must be conducted at each site.

**Local staff incident reporting:** Staff at each site must know who to report an incident to, and that contact must be someone accessible to them — not just a central compliance officer they've never met.

**Local vendor relationships:** If a site uses a vendor not used by other sites (a specific lab, a local specialty service, a site-specific software tool), that vendor's BAA must be assessed and tracked.

## Part 3: Local Compliance Owner Designation

Each site requires a named local compliance contact — the person accountable for site-specific compliance tasks and the first point of contact for staff at that site.

The local compliance owner does not replace the organizational Privacy Officer. They report to the Privacy Officer, execute site-specific tasks, and escalate incidents and compliance questions to the central program.

**Local compliance owner designation:**

| Site | Local compliance owner | Role | Contact information | Reports to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Site 1 | | | | Privacy Officer |
| Site 2 | | | | Privacy Officer |
| Site 3 | | | | Privacy Officer |

**Local compliance owner responsibilities:**
- Confirm training completion for their site's workforce
- Conduct or support quarterly access reviews for site-specific systems
- Complete physical security inspections at their facility
- Be the first contact for staff incident reports at their site
- Escalate incidents to the Privacy Officer immediately
- Attend quarterly compliance meetings

**What the local compliance owner is not:**
- The decision-maker for breach determinations (that's the Privacy Officer with legal counsel)
- Responsible for negotiating or executing BAAs
- The author of organizational policies
- A replacement for the Privacy Officer in an OCR investigation

## Part 5: Evidence Rollup Structure

The organizational evidence binder must reflect all sites. Structure the binder so site-specific documentation is findable by site, while shared organizational documentation is at the top level.

**Recommended folder structure:**

```
/HIPAA Compliance Binder
  /01 Policies [organizational — applies to all sites]
  /02 Training Records
    /02a Training — Site 1
    /02b Training — Site 2
    /02c Training — Site 3
  /03 Risk Analysis [organizational — covers all sites]
  /04 Business Associate Agreements
    /04a Organizational BAAs
    /04b Site 1 BAAs (site-specific vendors only)
    /04c Site 2 BAAs
  /05 Incident Records [all sites, chronological]
  /06 Access Reviews
    /06a Access Review — Site 1 (by quarter)
    /06b Access Review — Site 2
    /06c Access Review — Site 3
  /07 Physical and Technical Safeguards
    /07a Physical Security — Site 1
    /07b Physical Security — Site 2
    /07c Physical Security — Site 3
```

## Where Multi-Location Rollouts Stall

Three failure points appear consistently in multi-location compliance rollouts:

**1. No local owner.** The central Privacy Officer cannot be present at every site daily. Without a designated local owner at each site, site-specific tasks don't get done. Someone at each site must own the site-specific compliance responsibilities.

**2. Inconsistent training completion.** Organizational training is designed and approved. Site 1 completes it for all 15 staff members. Site 2 completes it for 11 of 12. Site 3 never gets around to it because a key trainer was out sick during the training week. Six months later, Site 3 is unfinished. Tracking completion by site with named owners and deadlines catches this before the rollout officially closes.

**3. Shared evidence that doesn't distinguish sites.** An incident log combining all three sites into a single chronological file makes it difficult to answer site-specific questions from an OCR investigator. Organize evidence by site from the beginning.
