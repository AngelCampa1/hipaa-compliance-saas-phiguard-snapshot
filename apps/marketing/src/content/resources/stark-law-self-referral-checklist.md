## How to Use This Checklist

This checklist is a self-assessment tool. Work through each question with your practice administrator and note every "yes" or "unsure" answer. Those answers identify arrangements that need legal review — they do not by themselves indicate a violation. Many legitimate arrangements fall within Stark exceptions, but those exceptions have specific requirements that must be satisfied in full and documented.

This checklist does not provide legal advice. Stark Law analysis is fact-specific and technically complex. Any arrangement that may implicate Stark should be reviewed by health care counsel familiar with federal self-referral law before you rely on any exception.

---

## Part 1 — Ownership and Investment Interest Assessment

For each question, mark Yes / No / Unsure. Every "Yes" or "Unsure" requires legal review.

**1. Physician ownership in outside entities**

- [ ] Does any physician in your practice hold an ownership or investment interest — equity, stock, partnership interest, or profit-sharing right — in any entity that provides designated health services (DHS) to which your clinic refers patients?
- [ ] Does any immediate family member of a physician in your practice hold such an interest in a DHS entity?
- [ ] Does any physician in your practice receive returns on investment (dividends, distributions, capital gains) from a DHS entity based on referral volume or value?

**2. In-clinic ancillary services**

- [ ] Does your practice offer any of the following in-office services to which referring physicians' patients are directed: clinical laboratory, imaging (X-ray, MRI, ultrasound, CT), physical or occupational therapy, or durable medical equipment?
- [ ] Do referring physicians in the practice have an ownership interest in the practice entity that owns or operates those ancillary services?

**3. Hospital or surgery center ownership**

- [ ] Does any physician in your practice hold an ownership or investment interest in a hospital, ambulatory surgery center (ASC), or other facility to which the physician refers patients for designated health services?

---

## Part 2 — Compensation Arrangement Assessment

**4. Direct compensation from DHS entities**

- [ ] Does any physician in your practice receive compensation — salary, fees, medical directorships, speaking honoraria, or any other payment — from an entity that provides DHS to which that physician refers?
- [ ] Is any physician's compensation from your practice itself structured in a way that takes referral volume into account — i.e., the physician earns more when they refer more patients to in-practice ancillary services?

**5. Indirect compensation**

- [ ] Does any physician receive non-cash benefits — free office space, equipment, staff, meals, travel, or other items of value — from a DHS entity?
- [ ] Does any physician receive compensation from a third party (e.g., a management services organization, a staffing company) where the ultimate source of funds is a DHS entity to which that physician refers?

**6. Recruitment arrangements**

- [ ] Has your practice recruited or is it recruiting a physician under a recruitment arrangement funded or subsidized by a hospital or health system If so, does that physician refer patients to that hospital?

---

## Part 3 — Co-Location and Specialty Arrangements

**7. Shared space and services**

- [ ] Does your clinic share space, staff, or equipment with any other provider or entity to which your physicians refer patients for DHS?
- [ ] Are any lease or service agreements between your clinic and a DHS entity structured at below-market rates, or do they include variable rent tied to volume or revenue?

**8. Specialty arrangements**

- [ ] Does your clinic have any formal or informal arrangement with a specialist or specialist group under which referrals flow in exchange for anything of value?
- [ ] Does your clinic hold any interest in a home health agency, infusion company, or DME supplier to which patients are referred?

## Part 4 — Exception Reference Guide

If you answered "Yes" to any question above, the arrangement may still be permissible under a Stark exception. Exceptions are narrow and their requirements must be fully satisfied — partial compliance is not compliance. The following exceptions are most commonly relevant to small clinics.

| Exception | Description | Key Requirements |
|-----------|-------------|------------------|
| **In-Office Ancillary Services** (42 CFR §411.355(b)) | Physicians may refer to ancillary services furnished in the same building as the practice or in a centralized building used by the group | Services must be supervised by a physician in the group; billed by the group or a physician in the group; location requirements must be met |
| **Group Practice** (42 CFR §411.352) | Allows physician owners in a qualifying group practice to receive profit distributions | Group must meet the regulatory definition; at least 75% of patient care services must be provided by group physicians; overhead sharing and unified business requirements apply |
| **Employment** (42 CFR §411.357(c)) | Physicians employed by a DHS entity may receive compensation without violating Stark | Compensation must be at fair market value; not determined by volume or value of referrals; commercially reasonable; documented in a written agreement |
| **Personal Services** (42 CFR §411.357(d)) | Contractual arrangements for legitimate services (e.g., medical directorships) | Must be in writing; compensation at fair market value; aggregate services do not exceed what is commercially reasonable; not tied to referral volume |
| **Bona Fide Employment — Recruitment** (42 CFR §411.357(e)) | Hospital-funded recruitment arrangements for physicians joining a practice | Written agreement with hospital required; physician must relocate primary practice; compensation must not be tied to referrals to the hospital |
| **Fair Market Value** (42 CFR §411.357(l)) | Compensation arrangements at fair market value for identifiable services | Must be in writing; fair market value documented; not determined by referral volume; commercially reasonable |

## Action Steps

For each "Yes" or "Unsure" answer in Parts 1–3:

1. Document the arrangement in writing — what it involves, who the parties are, when it started, and what compensation flows
2. Identify which exception(s) might apply
3. Bring documentation and this checklist to health care legal counsel for a formal review before relying on any exception
4. If the arrangement cannot be structured to meet an exception, discuss corrective action with counsel
