## How to Use This Template

Copy the policy text below into a Word document, fill in your clinic name and Privacy Officer's contact information, and complete the Approved Tools Registry with the AI tools your clinic currently uses or plans to use. Before distributing to staff, confirm BAA status for every tool in the registry — contact the vendor directly if a signed BAA is not already on file.

AI tools change fast. Consumer products update their terms, enterprise versions shift their data retention policies, and new tools appear constantly. The annual review cycle built into Section 7 of this policy is not optional — it is how your clinic stays current. Staff need clear guidance now; the AI landscape your clinic navigates next year will look different.

## AI Use Policy Template

**[CLINIC NAME]**
**Artificial Intelligence Use Policy**
**Effective Date: [DATE]**
**Policy Owner: Privacy Officer — [NAME]**

---

### 1. Purpose and Scope

This policy governs the use of artificial intelligence (AI) tools — including large language models, AI writing assistants, AI-powered transcription tools, AI coding tools, and similar products — by all workforce members of [CLINIC NAME]. It applies to any AI tool used for any work-related task, regardless of whether that task directly involves patient care.

The purpose is to protect patient PHI, ensure [CLINIC NAME] meets its obligations under the HIPAA Security Rule (45 CFR §§164.308, 164.312) and Privacy Rule (45 CFR §164.502), and establish a clear framework for responsible AI use in a clinical environment.

### 2. Approved AI Tools

Only AI tools that appear on the [CLINIC NAME] Approved Tools Registry (Section 8 of this policy) may be used for work-related tasks. A tool enters the Approved Tools Registry only after:

1. The Privacy Officer or IT lead has reviewed the tool's data handling practices and terms of service
2. A signed HIPAA Business Associate Agreement (BAA) is obtained if the tool will process, store, or transmit PHI
3. The tool's permitted and prohibited uses are documented in the registry

Staff may not use any AI tool not on the registry for work-related tasks. This applies to free consumer tools, personal subscriptions, browser extensions, and mobile applications.

### 3. Prohibited Uses

The following are prohibited for all workforce members:

- Entering PHI into any AI tool that does not have a signed BAA and does not appear on the Approved Tools Registry
- Using any consumer AI product — including ChatGPT (personal accounts), Google Gemini (personal accounts), Microsoft Copilot (personal accounts), Claude.ai (personal accounts), or comparable tools — for any task involving patient information, patient records, billing data, or clinical documentation
- Uploading patient documents, records, screenshots, or files to any AI tool unless the tool is on the Approved Tools Registry and the upload is within the tool's documented permitted uses
- Using an approved AI tool in a manner outside its listed permitted uses in the registry

### 4. What Staff May Input Into AI Tools

**Without a BAA:** AI tools without a BAA may receive only de-identified content — content that meets the de-identification standard at 45 CFR §164.514(b) or (c). De-identification is not simply removing a patient's name. It requires removing all 18 categories of identifiers listed in the Safe Harbor method, or expert determination. When in doubt, treat content as PHI.

General administrative tasks — drafting policy language, formatting templates with no patient data, summarizing publicly available clinical guidelines, writing staff communication drafts with no patient references — may use non-BAA tools if the content contains no PHI.

**With a BAA:** Tools on the Approved Tools Registry with a signed BAA may receive PHI within their documented permitted uses. Staff must stay within those permitted uses and not expand them without Privacy Officer approval.

### 5. BAA Requirements

Before any AI tool is approved to receive PHI:

1. The Privacy Officer must confirm the vendor offers a BAA
2. A signed BAA must be executed and filed in the clinic's vendor agreement records
3. The BAA effective date must be recorded in the Approved Tools Registry
4. The BAA must be reviewed at each annual policy review

If a vendor does not offer a BAA, that tool may not process PHI regardless of other features or price. This is a hard requirement with no exceptions.

### 6. Incident Reporting

Any workforce member who suspects that PHI was entered into an unapproved AI tool, or entered into an approved tool outside its permitted uses, must:

1. Stop using the tool immediately
2. Report the suspected incident to the Privacy Officer within one business day
3. Preserve any records of what was entered, when, and into which tool
4. Cooperate with the Privacy Officer's breach risk assessment under 45 CFR §164.402

The Privacy Officer will assess whether the incident constitutes a reportable breach and act accordingly. Workforce members who report incidents in good faith will not face retaliation.

### 7. Annual Review

This policy and the Approved Tools Registry will be reviewed at least annually by the Privacy Officer. The review will confirm:

- All tools in the registry are still in use and still have current signed BAAs
- New tools in use by staff are assessed and either added to the registry or prohibited
- Policy terms are updated to reflect changes in HHS guidance on AI and HIPAA
