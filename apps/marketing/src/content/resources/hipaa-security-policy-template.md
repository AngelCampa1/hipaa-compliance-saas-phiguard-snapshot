## Who Needs This Template

Any covered entity that creates, receives, maintains, or transmits ePHI — electronic protected health information — must comply with the HIPAA Security Rule. For a medical clinic, that means your EHR, billing system, patient portal, email containing PHI, and any cloud storage holding patient records. If your clinic has any of those, you need a written security policy.

Many small clinics have an informal understanding of security practices — "we use strong passwords and have antivirus" — but no written policy that can be produced during an OCR investigation, a vendor audit, or a cyber incident response. This template turns that informal understanding into a documented compliance program.

## What HIPAA Rule It Satisfies

The HIPAA Security Rule, at 45 CFR §§164.302–164.318, requires covered entities to implement administrative, physical, and technical safeguards to protect ePHI confidentiality, integrity, and availability. 45 CFR §164.316 requires those safeguards to be documented in written policies and procedures and retained for six years.

NIST Special Publication 800-66r2 provides detailed implementation guidance for the Security Rule and is the standard reference for small and medium healthcare organizations assessing their security programs.

## What Is Inside This Template

**Administrative Safeguards (§164.308).** The largest section, covering:

- Security Officer designation and responsibilities
- Risk analysis and risk management procedures (required specification)
- Sanction policy for security violations
- Information system activity review — log monitoring and access reviews
- Workforce security: authorization, access provisioning, and termination procedures
- Security awareness and training program structure
- Security incident response procedures — detection, containment, documentation, and notification
- Contingency plan reference (links to the [HIPAA contingency plan template](/resources/hipaa-contingency-plan-template))
- Business associate management — BAA requirements and vendor security review

**Physical Safeguards (§164.310).** Covering:

- Facility access controls — lock procedures, visitor logs, facility security plans
- Workstation use policy — screen locks, positioning, authorized software
- Workstation and device security
- Device and media controls — disposal procedures, hardware inventory, media sanitization

**Technical Safeguards (§164.312).** Covering:

- Access control: unique user IDs, emergency access procedures, automatic logoff, encryption and decryption
- Audit controls: system activity logging and review schedules
- Integrity controls: ePHI authentication and error correction
- Transmission security: encryption in transit for all ePHI

**Policies and Procedures (§164.316).** Version control, review schedule, distribution, and the documentation retention requirement.

## How to Fill It Out

**Step 1.** Assign your Security Officer. This is required under §164.308(a)(2). In a small clinic it is often the same person as the Privacy Officer or the practice administrator. The name must appear in the written policy.

**Step 2.** Complete a risk analysis before finalizing the policy. Under §164.308(a)(1), a risk analysis is a required specification. The security policy references and is informed by the risk analysis — you cannot write a meaningful security policy without knowing what your actual ePHI flows and vulnerabilities are.

**Step 3.** Work through the addressable specifications section by section. For each addressable specification you are not implementing as written, document the alternative measure you are using and the rationale.

**Step 4.** Adapt the device inventory section to your actual environment. List every device category that accesses or stores ePHI — workstations, tablets, smartphones, scanners — and confirm each is covered by the applicable physical and technical controls.

**Step 5.** Have the Security Officer review and sign. Distribute to all workforce members who have ePHI access and obtain signed acknowledgments.

## Who Owns It, Where to Store It, and When to Update It

**Who fills it out:** The Security Officer, who is responsible under §164.308(a)(2) for implementing and maintaining the security policies. The practice administrator and any IT support personnel should contribute to device inventory, vendor, and access control sections.

**Where to store it:** Retain the written security policy for six years from the date created or last in effect, per 45 CFR §164.316(b)(2)(i). The current version should be accessible to all workforce members with ePHI access, and superseded versions must be archived rather than deleted.

**How often it is reviewed:** At minimum, annually, under §164.316(b)(2)(iii). Also review and update whenever a significant operational or environmental change occurs.

**What triggers a required update:** Any change to ePHI systems triggers a mandatory review of the relevant policy sections. Specific triggers include: adopting a new EHR or cloud platform; a workforce termination that reveals an access control gap; a security incident or near-miss; a change in the vendor roster covered by the BAA section; a NIST SP 800-66 revision; or any OCR guidance update affecting the Security Rule standards. Document each review in the version control log, including what was reviewed, who reviewed it, and what changed.
