## Short answer

Confluence has a HIPAA path, but it is not automatic. Atlassian's current materials say BAAs are available for Standard, Premium, and Enterprise plans for Confluence, Jira, and Jira Service Management. Free and trial plans are not eligible.

That means the old "Premium or Enterprise only" answer is stale. The better question is whether Confluence should be the operating home for clinic compliance work. A signed BAA helps with vendor contracting, but it does not turn a wiki into a policy acknowledgement workflow, training tracker, incident record, vendor BAA registry, or remediation system.

PHIGuard is the stronger alternative when the clinic needs the compliance work to move: owner, due date, evidence, review history, and follow-up.

## Is Confluence HIPAA compliant?

Confluence can support HIPAA-governed use when the clinic completes the required Atlassian steps. Based on Atlassian's current support documentation, that means:

| Requirement | What the clinic needs to verify |
|---|---|
| Eligible plan | Standard, Premium, or Enterprise; not Free or trial |
| BAA | A signed Business Associate Agreement with Atlassian before PHI goes into the product |
| HIPAA tagging | The relevant Atlassian apps are tagged or enabled for HIPAA handling |
| AI settings | AI is deactivated for the Atlassian apps in the organization when HIPAA is enabled |
| Third-party apps | Marketplace apps, integrations, connectors, and add-ons are separately evaluated for BAA and PHI exposure |
| Restricted fields | Staff understand where PHI must not be entered, including names, keys, configuration labels, or other fields Atlassian excludes |
| Internal process | The clinic documents permissions, review cadence, retention, incident escalation, and staff training |

If any of those pieces are missing, the clinic should not treat Confluence as ready for PHI.

## Confluence vs PHIGuard

| Criterion | Confluence | PHIGuard |
|---|---|---|
| Primary design center | Wiki pages, documentation, knowledge base, and team collaboration | HIPAA compliance operations for clinics |
| BAA path | Available on eligible Atlassian Standard, Premium, and Enterprise plans; not Free or trial | Built around BAA-covered clinic workflows |
| HIPAA setup burden | Customer must sign BAA, tag apps, disable AI, configure fields, and manage third-party app exposure | Compliance workflows are already organized around vendors, incidents, training, policies, and risk work |
| Policy library | Strong for writing and organizing documents | Useful for connecting policies to acknowledgement, review, and follow-up tasks |
| Acknowledgements | Requires custom process, app, or manual tracking | Fits recurring attestations and evidence follow-up |
| Incident response | Possible as pages or templates, but easy to separate from tasks and audit history | Incident records, tasks, evidence, and review history stay connected |
| Vendor BAA tracking | Possible with tables and pages | Native fit for vendor records, BAA status, and review dates |

Confluence is better as a knowledge base. PHIGuard is better as the system of record for the compliance work that should not disappear inside a page tree.

## Where Confluence can stay useful

Keep Confluence for general internal documentation, engineering notes, meeting notes, non-PHI SOPs, product decisions, and team knowledge bases. If the organization already uses Atlassian and has the correct BAA and HIPAA configuration, Confluence may also hold some policy documentation.

The clinic still needs boundaries. A page title, space name, attachment, comment, whiteboard, embedded app, or AI feature can create exposure if staff treat Confluence like an unrestricted dumping ground for patient-specific detail.

## Where Confluence becomes awkward for clinics

Small clinics rarely fail because they lack a place to type policies. They fail because the policy was never reviewed, the new employee never acknowledged the current version, the vendor BAA was never renewed, or the incident decision never became assigned follow-up.

Confluence can store the text of a HIPAA policy. It does not automatically answer:

- Who still needs to acknowledge this version?
- Which policy changed after the risk analysis?
- Which staff member owns the annual review?
- Which incident caused this procedure to change?
- Which vendor has PHI access under this policy?
- What evidence would we produce during an OCR inquiry?

Those are workflow questions, not page-layout questions.

## What changes with PHIGuard

PHIGuard keeps policy work connected to the compliance cycle around it:

- policy review tasks with owners and due dates
- acknowledgements tied to the current policy version
- training follow-up when a policy changes
- vendor BAA records linked to operational responsibilities
- incident response tasks when a policy gap causes or reveals a problem
- audit history around assignments, completion, and evidence

That makes the compliance record easier to explain. Instead of searching a wiki for the latest page and then rebuilding the workflow from memory, the clinic can see the policy work and the follow-up record together.

## Recommendation

Do not reject Confluence because it lacks a HIPAA path. Atlassian's current materials say there is a BAA path for eligible Standard, Premium, and Enterprise plans.

Reject Confluence as the main compliance operating system if the clinic needs policy acknowledgements, incident records, vendor BAAs, risk remediation, training evidence, and audit-ready follow-up. Use Confluence for documentation where it fits. Use PHIGuard for the HIPAA work that needs owners, proof, and review history.
