Smartsheet is a powerful spreadsheet-style work management platform. Many operations teams use it for project plans, intake forms, timelines, approvals, and cross-functional tracking. Clinics may consider it for credentialing trackers, compliance checklists, staff onboarding, access reviews, or incident response follow-up.

The key point is that Smartsheet is not a simple "no" for HIPAA. Smartsheet publishes HIPAA guidance, a HIPAA compliance page, and a Business Associate Agreement path for eligible services. Its own materials emphasize plan eligibility, product scope, configuration, and customer responsibility. That makes the buying decision more precise: Smartsheet can be part of a HIPAA environment when the right contract, eligible services, and controls are in place, but it is still a general work platform.

PHIGuard is narrower. It is built for small clinics that need HIPAA compliance tasks, evidence, audit history, and BAA-backed workflows without designing a spreadsheet-based compliance system from scratch.

## The BAA Is Necessary, Not Sufficient

HHS guidance makes the BAA requirement clear: when a vendor creates, receives, maintains, or transmits PHI on behalf of a covered entity, the covered entity needs appropriate written assurances. Smartsheet's BAA materials describe a shared-responsibility model and note that PHI should be used only within PHI Eligible Services.

That matters because a BAA does not make every sheet, add-on, integration, AI feature, export, notification, or workflow automatically safe for PHI. A clinic still has to validate which services are covered, how the workspace is configured, who can access the data, how logs are retained, and how staff will avoid putting patient identifiers in the wrong place.

For an enterprise compliance team, that may be manageable. For a small clinic, it can become another system to administer. Someone has to define sheets, lock columns, manage users, create templates, train staff, collect evidence, review exceptions, and prove completion later.

## What Changes With PHIGuard

PHIGuard starts from the healthcare compliance workflows a clinic already needs to run:

- HIPAA training assignments and completion evidence
- Annual risk analysis tasks and remediation follow-up
- Vendor BAA tracking and documentation
- Policy review and staff acknowledgment
- Incident response tasks and retained evidence
- Access reviews for workforce and vendor accounts

Those workflows are opinionated on purpose. PHIGuard attaches evidence to the task that required it, preserves history around compliance actions, and prices the product per clinic instead of making every new staff member a pricing event.

Smartsheet gives a team a broad platform. PHIGuard gives a clinic a compliance operating system.

## Smartsheet vs PHIGuard for HIPAA Compliance Work

| | Smartsheet | PHIGuard |
|---|---|---|
| Best fit | Broad work management and spreadsheet-style tracking | HIPAA compliance operations for clinics |
| BAA/HIPAA posture | Published BAA path and PHI Eligible Services requiring validation | BAA-backed PHIGuard compliance workflows |
| Setup burden | Customer configures sheets, permissions, templates, evidence, and cadence | Compliance workflows are built in |
| Evidence tracking | Custom sheet or attachment process | Native evidence fields |
| Audit history | Depends on plan, feature, retention, and configuration | Built around compliance tasks |
| Pricing model | Per-user/platform model with enterprise path for many advanced needs | Per-clinic plans |
| Best clinic use | Non-PHI operations or heavily managed enterprise workflows | Training, risk, vendor, policy, incident, and audit work |

The comparison is not whether Smartsheet has enterprise controls. It does. The practical question is whether a small clinic wants to run HIPAA compliance inside a general platform that requires configuration discipline, or inside a product designed specifically for that compliance cycle.

## Who Should Use PHIGuard Instead of Smartsheet

Use PHIGuard when the work is part of the HIPAA compliance record. That includes training, policy review, risk analysis, incident response, access review, vendor BAA follow-up, and any patient-adjacent task that needs controlled handling.

Use Smartsheet for general operational projects that do not contain PHI: facilities work, equipment rollouts, budget tracking, marketing calendars, or cross-department timelines. Clinics with mature enterprise administration may still use Smartsheet for some HIPAA-eligible workflows after a formal BAA and configuration review. Small clinics usually benefit from separating general operations from compliance operations.
