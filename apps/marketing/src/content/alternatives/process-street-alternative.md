Process Street is a well-known process management platform for SOPs, checklists, approvals, forms, workflow automation, and recurring operational work. Its current pricing page says HIPAA compliance and a BAA are available upon request for Enterprise plans. Its security and help materials describe controls such as SOC 2 Type II, encryption, vulnerability management, access controls, monitoring, SSO, audit/activity history, and other enterprise security practices.

That matters. Process Street should not be described as a tool with no HIPAA path. The more accurate comparison is this: Process Street can be an enterprise SOP platform with HIPAA support available by request, while PHIGuard is a clinic compliance workflow built for HIPAA tasks from the start.

For a small clinic, that difference still matters.

## The Enterprise SOP Problem

Process Street is strongest when a team wants to document repeatable processes and then run them as structured checklists. That can be valuable for:

- Administrative SOPs
- IT and facilities checklists
- HR onboarding and recurring reviews
- Procurement and vendor onboarding
- Quality management workflows
- Enterprise process standardization
- Healthcare and HIPAA template adaptation

In a clinic, SOP software becomes more complicated as soon as patient-related details enter the workflow. A checklist task might mention a patient. A form response might include an identifier. A comment might describe an incident. An attachment might include a screenshot from an EHR. An automation might send content into another service.

HHS business associate guidance focuses on whether a vendor creates, receives, maintains, or transmits PHI on behalf of a covered entity. If Process Street will touch PHI, the clinic must verify the BAA, plan scope, covered features, subprocessors, notification behavior, retention rules, export behavior, and integration boundaries.

Process Street also publishes healthcare and HIPAA materials. The question is not whether templates exist. The question is whether the clinic wants to govern HIPAA work inside a configurable enterprise SOP platform or inside a narrower compliance workflow built around clinic evidence.

## What Clinics Must Verify

Because Process Street positions HIPAA and BAA support as Enterprise features available upon request, a clinic should get written answers before putting PHI into the platform:

- Which plan and contract include the BAA
- Which services, forms, attachments, comments, automations, integrations, and AI features are covered
- Whether staff can prevent PHI from appearing in emails, Slack messages, webhooks, or external integrations
- How activity history and audit reports work for checklist runs, approvals, comments, files, and edits
- How retention, deletion, breach notification, and subprocessors are handled
- Whether the clinic needs a specific implementation pattern to stay within the BAA scope

Those are not objections to Process Street. They are the normal vendor-management questions a covered entity has to answer.

## What Changes With PHIGuard

PHIGuard starts with the healthcare compliance record, not a blank SOP template. The workflows are built around the recurring obligations a clinic needs to assign, evidence, and review:

- HIPAA training assignment and completion evidence
- Risk analysis tasks and remediation follow-up
- Vendor BAA tracking and renewal review
- Policy review and staff acknowledgment
- Incident response tasks and retained evidence
- Access review tasks for workforce and vendor accounts

The practical difference is setup burden. In Process Street, a clinic can build a sophisticated checklist system, but the clinic still owns the program design. In PHIGuard, the workflow is already shaped around HIPAA compliance operations.

## Process Street vs PHIGuard for Clinics

| | Process Street | PHIGuard |
|---|---|---|
| Best fit | SOPs, checklists, forms, approvals, enterprise process automation | HIPAA compliance operations |
| HIPAA/BAA path | Enterprise HIPAA and BAA available upon request; verify scope | BAA-backed PHIGuard compliance workflows |
| Primary setup | Build or import process templates | Use clinic compliance workflows |
| PHI handling | Must be governed across forms, comments, files, notifications, automations, and integrations | Designed around PHI-aware compliance operations |
| Evidence tracking | Checklist runs and user-created process evidence | Native evidence fields tied to compliance tasks |
| Audit history | Activity and workflow history exist, but HIPAA-specific immutable audit fit must be verified | Built around compliance task history |
| Clinic templates | Healthcare and HIPAA templates exist, but must be adapted and governed by the clinic | Built in |

Process Street is broader and can support complex process teams. PHIGuard is narrower and focused on the clinic compliance record.

## Who Should Use PHIGuard Instead

Choose PHIGuard if your clinic is tracking HIPAA training, policy acknowledgment, vendor BAAs, risk analysis, incident response, access reviews, or remediation tasks. Those workflows need evidence, due dates, owners, and audit history.

Use Process Street for non-PHI SOPs if it fits your operations. If you plan to use it for patient-related workflows, confirm the Enterprise BAA scope and implementation requirements before the first PHI-containing checklist is created.
