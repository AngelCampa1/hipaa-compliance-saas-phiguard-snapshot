Hive is a flexible project management platform for teams that need tasks, views, collaboration, proofing, time tracking, and add-ons. It can be a reasonable choice for general operations. A clinic evaluating Hive for HIPAA-sensitive work has a narrower question: will this workflow create, receive, maintain, or transmit PHI on behalf of the clinic

If the answer is yes, feature checklists are secondary. The clinic needs a signed Business Associate Agreement, clear scope for the services covered by that agreement, access controls, audit history, evidence retention, and staff workflows that reduce accidental PHI exposure. PHIGuard is built around that operating model. Hive is built around general project execution.

## The BAA Question Comes First

HHS explains that a covered entity must obtain satisfactory written assurances from a business associate before the vendor handles PHI on its behalf. In practice, that means a clinic cannot treat a project management tool as HIPAA-ready just because the tool has password protection, permissions, or enterprise security controls.

Hive's public pricing page describes Free, Starter, Teams, and Enterprise options, plus add-ons such as proofing, timesheets, team resourcing, dashboards, automations, external users, Buzz AI, and SSO/security controls. The public pricing page is useful for comparing operational cost, but a clinic still needs direct written confirmation of BAA availability, covered features, excluded services, AI restrictions, notification handling, export scope, and audit-log retention before putting patient-related work into Hive.

That is the practical risk for small clinics. A task such as "call Maria about lab result" can become PHI. So can an incident note, access review comment, training exception, or vendor follow-up note that identifies a patient or care situation. The tool may begin as a project board and slowly become part of the PHI workflow.

## What Changes With PHIGuard

PHIGuard is not trying to be a broad work management suite. It is built for small healthcare teams that need to run a compliance program without turning a spreadsheet or project board into an improvised evidence system.

PHIGuard gives clinics:

- BAA-backed compliance workflows for covered-entity operations
- Audit history attached to compliance work, not just workspace activity
- Evidence fields for training, risk analysis, policy review, vendor review, and incident response
- PHI-aware task handling that avoids spreading patient context through generic notifications and exports
- Role-based access that maps to clinic responsibilities such as admin, clinical, billing, and front desk work
- Per-clinic pricing so adding staff does not automatically multiply the compliance software bill

The difference is not "Hive plus HIPAA language." It is a narrower workflow built around the record a clinic may need to show an owner, auditor, regulator, payer, or malpractice carrier.

Hive may be a good place for marketing launches, facilities projects, and non-clinical operational work. PHIGuard should own the HIPAA work where patient context, staff accountability, vendor documentation, and evidence retention matter.

## Who Should Use PHIGuard Instead of Hive

Choose PHIGuard if your clinic needs to track annual HIPAA training, risk analysis, vendor BAAs, policy review, incident response, or patient-adjacent compliance tasks. Those workflows need more than a task card and a due date. They need ownership, evidence, history, and a clear PHI boundary.

Choose Hive if your team needs a general productivity platform and the work does not include PHI. Many clinics can use both: Hive for general operations and PHIGuard for HIPAA compliance operations. Keeping those scopes separate is often the cleaner control.

## Evaluation Notes for Clinic Buyers

Hive can be a reasonable operational tool when the workflow is clearly non-clinical, but patient-adjacent work changes the buying criteria. A clinic needs a signed BAA when PHI is involved, a documented scope for which features are covered, and a way to prove who accessed or changed compliance records. The source set for this page is Hive: Security in Hive; Hive: Workspace Activity; HHS: Business Associates. Treat those pages as starting points, then confirm plan names, BAA eligibility, and support commitments in the current contract before relying on the tool for PHI.

When comparing Hive with PHIGuard, separate general productivity from HIPAA operations. Hive is a flexible project management platform. PHIGuard is built for small clinics that need BAA-backed compliance tasks, audit history, evidence, and per-clinic pricing. Hive is a general project management platform. Clinics should verify BAA availability, plan scope, security configuration, and permitted PHI use directly with Hive before putting patient-related work in Hive. PHIGuard is most relevant when the work involves incident logs, vendor BAAs, policy acknowledgements, training evidence, recurring risk-analysis tasks, and review history that needs to be defensible later.

Keep Hive for workflows where it is strongest: planning, collaboration, sales operations, internal tracking, or non-PHI documentation. Move the compliance record to a system designed for clinic accountability when the task names a patient, describes care, tracks an incident, stores a BAA, or requires a dated record of staff action. That split lets teams avoid overbuying enterprise software while keeping HIPAA evidence in one controlled place.
