ProofHub is a project management and team collaboration platform. Its pricing page emphasizes flat-rate plans with unlimited users, while its product materials describe tasks, discussions, files, notes, proofing, time tracking, reports, roles, approvals, and activity history. Its security page describes operational and infrastructure safeguards for general business collaboration.

That makes ProofHub a serious option for teams that want predictable project-management pricing. It does not make ProofHub a purpose-built HIPAA compliance workflow for medical clinics.

PHIGuard is the better ProofHub alternative when the work needs a healthcare compliance record, not just a project board.

## The Appeal of Flat Pricing

ProofHub's flat-rate model is genuinely useful. Many project management tools charge per user, so every new employee, contractor, or admin can increase monthly cost. A clinic with rotating staff or multiple administrative users may prefer a predictable team price.

That is a real advantage for general operations. It can work well for:

- Facilities projects
- Marketing calendars
- Website or campaign work
- Vendor projects that do not include patient data
- Administrative tasks that never mention patients
- Internal planning outside the clinical compliance record

The problem is that HIPAA risk does not disappear because the pricing model is convenient.

## The BAA and Activity-Log Question

HHS business associate guidance focuses on whether a vendor creates, receives, maintains, or transmits PHI on behalf of a covered entity. If a clinic uses ProofHub for work that includes patient information, incident notes, care follow-up, insurance issues, screenshots, documents, or other PHI-adjacent details, the clinic must verify the vendor relationship in writing.

This review found ProofHub pricing, security, and activity-log documentation, but did not find a public official HIPAA BAA path. That is not the same as saying ProofHub will never sign one. It means a clinic should not assume PHI use is permitted from public marketing pages alone.

ProofHub also documents activity logs, which can be useful for general project oversight. For HIPAA workflows, a clinic should verify whether those logs are complete for tasks, comments, files, proofs, permissions, status changes, exports, integrations, and administrative actions. It should also verify immutability, retention, exportability, administrator access, and contract scope.

## What Changes With PHIGuard

PHIGuard is built around compliance tasks clinics need to prove:

- HIPAA training assignment and completion evidence
- Annual risk analysis and remediation tracking
- Vendor BAA tracking and renewal review
- Policy review and staff acknowledgment
- Incident response tasks and retained evidence
- Access review tasks for workforce and vendor accounts

Those workflows are narrower than ProofHub's project management surface. That is intentional. A clinic compliance record should not depend on staff deciding whether the official evidence lives in a task comment, a proofing note, a file attachment, a discussion thread, or a project report.

PHIGuard keeps the evidence attached to the compliance task that required it.

Both matter. Only one answers the clinic's privacy obligation.

## Who Should Use PHIGuard Instead

Choose PHIGuard if the work includes HIPAA training, risk analysis, policy review, vendor BAA follow-up, access review, incident response, remediation tracking, or anything patient-adjacent. Those workflows need a clear owner, due date, evidence, status, review history, and vendor scope.

Keep ProofHub for non-PHI general operations if it fits your project management style. Before using it with PHI, get written answers on BAA availability, covered services, data handling, subprocessors, retention, notifications, integrations, exports, and audit history.
