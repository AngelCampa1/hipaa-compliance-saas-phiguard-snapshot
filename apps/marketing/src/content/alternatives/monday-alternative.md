## Short answer

monday.com can support HIPAA-regulated workflows on Enterprise when the account activates the BAA and the required settings. Its current support page also describes email-notification redaction, mobile-app HIPAA support, access-review guidance, audit-log review, and third-party app cautions.

That is stronger than a simple "not HIPAA compliant" answer. The clinic still has to ask whether it wants to run PHI-adjacent work in a general work-management platform.

## What monday.com provides for HIPAA

monday.com's HIPAA documentation describes an Enterprise account path. It includes administrative activation of HIPAA compliance, review and acceptance of the BAA, and optional redaction of content in email notifications about updates.

The same documentation points admins toward stronger authentication, regular access reviews, audit-log monitoring, and careful evaluation of third-party apps and integrations. That caveat matters. Integrations can move PHI outside the covered workflow if the clinic does not evaluate them separately.

| Integrations | Useful but must be evaluated for HIPAA separately | Built for core HIPAA operations without relying on marketplace workflows |

| Pricing posture | Enterprise sales and per-seat expansion | Clinic-oriented packaging |

## Where monday.com can stay useful

Keep monday.com for general operations, marketing calendars, facilities work, non-PHI project tracking, or administrative work that does not create patient-linked records.

It may also fit larger healthcare organizations that already run monday.com Enterprise, have dedicated admins, and can maintain strict rules for boards, columns, notifications, automations, integrations, guests, and exports.

## Where monday.com becomes risky for clinics

The risk is not that monday.com lacks features. The risk is that a flexible workspace lets staff invent their own PHI workflows.

A patient name appears in an update. A board notification reaches email. A file is attached to a broad workspace. An integration sends form data to a third-party app. A vendor review lives in one board while the signed BAA is in another folder. None of those failures are solved by the BAA alone.

## What changes with PHIGuard

PHIGuard is built around the recurring compliance work small clinics actually need to prove:

- vendor BAA tracking
- incident records
- risk remediation
- annual training follow-up
- policy review
- evidence retention
- audit-ready task history

## Recommendation

monday.com Enterprise can be part of a HIPAA program when the contract, BAA, settings, and workflows are handled carefully.

PHIGuard is the better alternative when the clinic wants the HIPAA workflow itself - vendors, incidents, risk, training, policies, and evidence - to be structured before staff start entering patient-adjacent work.

## Evaluation Notes for Clinic Buyers

Monday.com can be a reasonable operational tool when the workflow is clearly non-clinical, but patient-adjacent work changes the buying criteria. A clinic needs a signed BAA when PHI is involved, a documented scope for which features are covered, and a way to prove who accessed or changed compliance records. The source set for this page is HHS: Business Associates; HHS: Summary of the HIPAA Security Rule. Treat those pages as starting points, then confirm plan names, BAA eligibility, and support commitments in the current contract before relying on the tool for PHI.

When comparing Monday.com with PHIGuard, separate general productivity from HIPAA operations. monday.com has an Enterprise HIPAA path with a BAA and admin controls, but small clinics still need to decide whether a generic work platform is the right system for PHI-adjacent operations. monday.com documents a HIPAA path for Enterprise accounts that includes BAA activation and related settings. A clinic still has to configure the account, control integrations, train staff, and keep PHI out of unsafe workflows. PHIGuard is most relevant when the work involves incident logs, vendor BAAs, policy acknowledgements, training evidence, recurring risk-analysis tasks, and review history that needs to be defensible later.

Keep Monday.com for workflows where it is strongest: planning, collaboration, sales operations, internal tracking, or non-PHI documentation. Move the compliance record to a system designed for clinic accountability when the task names a patient, describes care, tracks an incident, stores a BAA, or requires a dated record of staff action. That split lets teams avoid overbuying enterprise software while keeping HIPAA evidence in one controlled place.
