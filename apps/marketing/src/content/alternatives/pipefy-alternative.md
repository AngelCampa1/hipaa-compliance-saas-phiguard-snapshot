Pipefy is a business process automation platform. Its public materials focus on process management, request intake, forms, approvals, workflow automation, integrations, analytics, and enterprise operations. Its security materials describe controls such as encryption, access management, monitoring, incident response, vulnerability management, and compliance program information.

Those are meaningful capabilities for a general operations team. They do not automatically make Pipefy the right system of record for a small clinic's HIPAA compliance work.

PHIGuard is the better Pipefy alternative when the work is not just "route this request" but "prove this HIPAA obligation was assigned, completed, reviewed, and retained."

One important nuance: Pipefy does publish audit-log documentation for pipes and cards. The gap is not "no activity history." The gap is that a clinic still has to verify whether Pipefy's contract, DPA, BAA status, log retention, exports, integrations, and notification behavior fit the exact PHI workflow. Pipefy's public DPA also makes sector-specific compliance suitability a customer responsibility, which is exactly why a healthcare review cannot stop at general security controls.

## Where Pipefy Fits

Pipefy can be useful for structured business processes:

- Procurement approvals and vendor onboarding
- IT service requests and facilities tickets
- Finance, HR, and back-office operations
- Customer operations outside the clinical record
- Custom workflows that an operations team wants to design from scratch

That flexibility is the point of a broad workflow platform. Teams can decide which fields, statuses, owners, automations, and reports they need.

For clinics, that same flexibility creates extra compliance work. A patient name can land in an intake form. A task title can reveal appointment context. An automation can send a detail to an email or integration. A comment can become the unofficial place where staff document an incident. Once a workflow may create, receive, maintain, or transmit PHI, the clinic has to evaluate BAA scope and operational controls before using the tool that way.

## The BAA and Workflow Question

HHS business associate guidance says a covered entity needs appropriate written assurances when a vendor handles PHI on its behalf. That review is broader than checking whether a vendor has security controls.

For Pipefy, clinics should verify directly:

- Whether a BAA is available for the exact plan and services the clinic will use
- Whether forms, automations, notifications, attachments, integrations, and exports are covered
- How audit history works for task changes, status changes, comments, files, and automations, including retention and export limits
- Whether staff can restrict PHI from email notifications and third-party integrations
- How retention, deletion, subprocessors, and incident notification are handled

If the clinic cannot answer those questions in writing, Pipefy should be treated as a non-PHI operations tool.

## What Changes With PHIGuard

PHIGuard starts from the clinic compliance workflow instead of a blank process canvas. The product is built around repeatable HIPAA obligations that small clinics need to assign and evidence:

- HIPAA training assignment and completion records
- Annual risk analysis tasks and remediation owners
- Vendor BAA tracking and renewal review
- Policy review and staff acknowledgment
- Incident response tasks with retained evidence
- Access review tasks for workforce and vendor accounts

The important difference is evidence structure. In a generic workflow tool, the clinic must decide what the official record is. Is it the task? A comment? A form response? An attachment? A report? In PHIGuard, the compliance task is the record, and evidence is attached to the work that required it.

## Pipefy vs PHIGuard for Clinics

| | Pipefy | PHIGuard |
|---|---|---|
| Best fit | General process automation | HIPAA compliance operations |
| Primary workflow | Custom forms, requests, approvals, automations | Clinic compliance tasks and evidence |
| HIPAA/BAA path | Must be verified directly for PHI use | BAA-backed PHIGuard compliance workflows |
| PHI handling | Depends on configuration, forms, notifications, and integrations | Designed around PHI-aware compliance operations |
| Evidence tracking | User-created workflow and reports | Native evidence fields tied to compliance work |
| Audit history | Pipe/card audit logs are documented, but HIPAA-specific immutable audit fit, retention, and export scope must be verified | Built around compliance task history |

Pipefy is broader. PHIGuard is narrower. For a clinic compliance program, narrower is often easier to govern.

## Who Should Use PHIGuard Instead

Choose PHIGuard if your team is tracking HIPAA training, risk remediation, incident response, vendor BAA follow-up, policy acknowledgment, access review, or any patient-adjacent compliance task. Those records need ownership, due dates, evidence, status, and history.

Keep Pipefy for non-PHI operational workflows if it fits your broader business process needs. The boundary should be explicit: general operations in the general process tool, HIPAA compliance work in PHIGuard.
