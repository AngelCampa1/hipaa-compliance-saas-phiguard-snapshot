Todoist is a polished task manager for individuals and teams. Its pricing page describes personal projects, team workspaces, comments, assigned tasks, team activity, project templates, roles, permissions, reminders, calendar views, integrations, activity history, and SOC 2 Type II status. Its security page describes TLS, encryption at rest, AWS hosting, backups, admin roles, and sharing controls.

Those are real strengths for ordinary productivity. They do not make Todoist a purpose-built HIPAA compliance workflow for clinics.

PHIGuard is the better Todoist alternative when the task is patient-adjacent compliance evidence, not a personal reminder.

## Where Todoist Fits

Todoist is useful for:

- Personal reminders
- Non-PHI administrative tasks
- Lightweight team project lists
- Calendar-driven personal productivity
- Shared checklists that do not include patient information

The problem is scope drift. A clinic administrator may start with "renew vendor contract" and later add a patient name, incident detail, insurance issue, referral note, appointment context, or screenshot. A personal task manager can quietly become an unofficial compliance record.

Under HHS business associate guidance, if a vendor creates, receives, maintains, or transmits PHI on behalf of a covered entity, the clinic needs appropriate written assurances. Todoist security controls and SOC 2 status help with vendor review, but they do not answer the HIPAA BAA question by themselves.

## The BAA and Activity-History Question

Todoist's help center says Doist has not yet pursued HIPAA certification. Todoist also publishes reporting and activity-history features for task changes. This review did not find a public official Todoist HIPAA BAA path. That should be treated as a vendor-review trigger, not as permission to store PHI.

Clinics should verify directly before using Todoist for patient-related work:

- Whether a BAA is available for the exact plan
- Whether task names, comments, attachments, reminders, notifications, team activity, integrations, and backups are covered
- Whether activity history is complete, retained, exportable, and suitable for the clinic's evidence needs
- Whether external sharing, guests, integrations, email forwarding, and calendar sync can expose PHI
- How deletion, retention, subprocessors, and incident notification are handled

Without written confirmation, keep Todoist limited to non-PHI reminders and general productivity.

## What Changes With PHIGuard

PHIGuard is built around recurring clinic compliance tasks:

- HIPAA training assignment and completion evidence
- Risk analysis and remediation tracking
- Vendor BAA tracking and renewal review
- Policy review and staff acknowledgment
- Incident response tasks with retained evidence
- Access review tasks for workforce and vendor accounts

PHIGuard keeps the compliance record attached to the work. A clinic does not need to reconstruct evidence from a personal task list, a comment thread, a calendar reminder, or an exported activity report.

Choose PHIGuard if the task involves HIPAA training, risk analysis, vendor BAA follow-up, policy acknowledgment, incident response, access review, remediation, or patient-adjacent operations. Keep Todoist for non-PHI reminders and productivity if it fits your personal workflow.
