## Short Answer

Trello is a good general-purpose board. It is the wrong place for patient-adjacent clinic operations unless the clinic has a current written BAA path for Trello and a workflow that keeps PHI out of unsafe fields, notifications, automations, attachments, and integrations.

Atlassian's current HIPAA support page says Atlassian can sign BAAs for Standard, Premium, and Enterprise plans for Jira, Jira Service Management, and Confluence. It does not list Trello in that public BAA-eligible product statement. That distinction matters because Trello belongs to Atlassian, but not every Atlassian product is covered the same way.

| Pricing posture | Per-user Trello plans, including Free, Standard, Premium, and Enterprise | Clinic-oriented pricing for compliance operations |

## Where Trello Breaks Down for Clinics

Trello's strength is speed. A staff member can create a card in seconds. That is also the problem in a regulated clinic.

A card title like "Call Maria Gomez about lab results" can identify a patient and disclose care context. A checklist item can include appointment details. A comment can include a billing dispute. A screenshot attachment can include an EHR record. An email notification can forward card content into an inbox outside the intended workflow. A Power-Up can move data into another vendor system that has not been reviewed.

Those are not edge cases. They are normal ways staff use task boards when they are trying to get work done.

| Asana Enterprise | General project work where the clinic can qualify for Asana's HIPAA path | Enterprise buying and configuration discipline are required |

| Trello | Non-PHI boards, lightweight operations, office tasks, and marketing work | Atlassian's public HIPAA product list reviewed here does not include Trello |

The main question is not which board looks closest to Trello. The main question is where patient-adjacent evidence can safely live.

## What Changes With PHIGuard

PHIGuard narrows the workspace to the HIPAA work clinics actually need to prove:

- vendor BAA tracking
- incident response records
- risk remediation tasks
- annual and event-driven training follow-up
- policy review and acknowledgement
- access review evidence
- audit-ready task history

That narrower scope matters. A generic board asks the clinic to invent controls after the fact. PHIGuard gives the clinic a dedicated place for the compliance record before staff start entering patient-adjacent tasks.

## Best-Fit Recommendation

Keep Trello for non-PHI operations if your team likes it: office moves, supply ordering, website work, event planning, and internal projects with no patient identifiers.

Use PHIGuard for any workflow where the task itself could become compliance evidence or contain patient-adjacent context:

- "review possible disclosure"
- "update BAA for billing vendor"
- "follow up on access termination"
- "document annual training completion"
- "assign corrective action after wrong-recipient message"
- "review policy acknowledgement for new hire"

The more a task needs to survive an audit, the less it belongs in a generic board.

## Buyer Checklist

Before using any Trello alternative for clinic work, ask:

- Will the vendor sign a BAA for the exact product and plan?
- Are PHI fields separated from titles, comments, email notifications, and logs?
- Can the clinic export evidence by incident, vendor, staff member, or policy?
- Are edits, deletions, assignments, and status changes preserved?
- Can managers limit access by clinic role?
- Are guests, automations, Power-Ups, and integrations covered by the same review?
- Can the clinic show who owned each compliance task and when it closed?
- Does pricing scale per user, per clinic, or through an enterprise contract?
