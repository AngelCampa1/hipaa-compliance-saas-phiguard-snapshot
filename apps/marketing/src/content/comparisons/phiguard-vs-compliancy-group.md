## Short answer

PHIGuard and Compliancy Group both address healthcare compliance buyers, but they take different routes. Compliancy Group is a broader compliance solution with plan tiers, coaching, training, policy resources, risk assessments, vendor management, hotline reporting, and healthcare compliance breadth. PHIGuard is a clinic operating layer for the HIPAA work that has to stay assigned and provable week after week.

Choose Compliancy Group when the clinic wants a packaged compliance program with coaching and broader plan options. Choose PHIGuard when the clinic wants one accountable record for tasks, incidents, training evidence, vendor BAAs, policies, access reviews, and risk follow-up.

## Category fit

Compliancy Group's public pricing page is useful because it makes the buying model visible. It presents tiers such as Foundation, Growth, Advanced, and Elite, with user/location limits, employee-based pricing, training, policy manuals, risk assessments, vendor and BAA management, hotline reporting, and broader compliance libraries depending on tier. Incident-management depth should be verified against the current tier and add-on details. That is a serious compliance-platform model.

PHIGuard is more focused. It is not trying to be the broadest healthcare compliance marketplace. It is trying to keep the daily work of HIPAA from falling apart inside a small clinic. That means the product centers on owners, due dates, evidence, incidents, training, vendor reviews, policies, and a durable audit trail.

## Feature comparison

| Evaluation point | PHIGuard | Compliancy Group |
|---|---|---|
| Primary fit | Small clinic HIPAA operations and evidence | Broader healthcare compliance platform |
| Training | Built into the operating workflow | Included in plan tiers and training libraries |
| Policy management | Policy acknowledgement tied to work | Policy manuals, templates, and attestation tooling |
| Vendor BAA review | Recurring workflow and evidence | Vendor and BAA management in broader tiers |
| Incident handling | Core compliance workflow | Hotline/reporting and incident features by plan or add-on |
| Risk analysis | Follow-up tasks and evidence | Risk assessments and broader assessment library by plan |
| Pricing posture | Clinic-level pricing with BAA on every public plan | Base plan plus employee, location, and tier variables |
| Best buyer | Practice administrator who needs execution | Compliance buyer who values coaching and program breadth |

This is a fit comparison. Compliancy Group has real compliance breadth. PHIGuard wins when the core issue is operational accountability inside the clinic.

## Pricing and procurement

Compliancy Group publishes starting prices and tier details, but the final buying model can include billing term, employee count, locations, administrator limits, and plan scope. The Growth and higher tiers add broader libraries and vendor-management capabilities, while Advanced and Elite move toward more customizable or enterprise-grade compliance needs.

That model can be right for organizations that want layered compliance coverage. The tradeoff for a small clinic is procurement complexity and ongoing plan-fit management. If a clinic only needs HIPAA work to stay assigned, reviewed, and documented, it should be careful about buying more platform than it can operate.

PHIGuard's public pricing is built around clinic-level plans. BAA details are available during plan review, and the baseline includes recurring HIPAA task management, incident tracking, and an immutable audit trail. The clinic should compare total operating cost, not only list price: how many staff members need access, how often evidence is reconstructed manually, and how much follow-up happens outside the system.

## Where Compliancy Group fits

Choose Compliancy Group when the organization wants guided support, broader healthcare compliance coverage, plan-based training libraries, policy tooling, hotline reporting, and the structure of a larger compliance package. It can also fit buyers who want coaching as part of the program rather than a mostly self-service operating workflow.

The clinic should verify current plan details. Ask which tier includes vendor and BAA management, whether incident routing is included or needs a higher tier, how employee fees are calculated, how evidence exports work, and how coaching is delivered.

## Where PHIGuard fits

Choose PHIGuard when the clinic's compliance problem is daily execution. Someone has to follow up after the risk analysis. Someone has to review vendor BAAs. Someone has to document incident triage. Someone has to prove training and policy acknowledgement. Someone has to review access after staff changes.

PHIGuard is built around those recurring jobs. The evidence lives with the work, which is the practical advantage during audits, management reviews, incident response, and staff turnover.

## What to verify before buying

Before choosing either product, document these answers:

1. Does the clinic need coaching and broad compliance content, or a daily HIPAA operating record?
2. Which plan includes vendor BAA management, risk assessment, policy management, and incident workflows?
3. How do employee counts, locations, administrators, billing terms, and add-ons affect price?
4. Who owns remediation tasks after the risk assessment?
5. How are incidents triaged, routed, documented, and exported?
6. Can frontline clinic staff participate without creating side spreadsheets or inbox trails?
7. What evidence can be exported for an audit, investigation, or leadership review?

## Bottom line

Compliancy Group is a credible choice when the buyer wants a broader, guided healthcare compliance platform and values coaching, layered plans, and compliance-program breadth.

PHIGuard is the better fit when the clinic wants HIPAA work to operate cleanly every week. If the deciding factor is not the number of compliance resources but whether the work stays assigned and provable, PHIGuard is the sharper tool.
