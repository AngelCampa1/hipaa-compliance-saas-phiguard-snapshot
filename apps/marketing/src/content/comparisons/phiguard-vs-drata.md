## Short answer

Drata and PHIGuard both touch HIPAA, but they are built for different buyers. Drata is a compliance automation and GRC platform for teams that need framework evidence, control monitoring, risk management, integrations, and audit readiness. PHIGuard is a HIPAA operating record for small clinics.

Choose Drata if the organization needs broader compliance automation across frameworks. Choose PHIGuard if the clinic needs recurring HIPAA work to stay assigned, reviewed, and documented.

## Category fit

Drata's HIPAA materials describe mapping controls to evidence, tracking PHI risk, clarifying control gaps, and preparing for assessments. Its plan page describes compliance automation, pre-mapped frameworks, integrations, risk management, third-party risk management, Trust Center features, and add-ons.

PHIGuard is intentionally narrower. It does not try to replace a GRC platform for a technology company. It gives a clinic one place for risk follow-up, incidents, vendor BAAs, policies, training evidence, access reviews, and audit history.

## Feature comparison

| Evaluation point | PHIGuard | Drata |
|---|---|---|
| Primary fit | Small clinic HIPAA operations and evidence | Compliance automation and GRC evidence |
| Framework scope | Focused on HIPAA clinic workflows | SOC 2, ISO 27001, HIPAA, GDPR, and other framework work |
| Evidence model | Evidence tied to operational HIPAA work | Continuous evidence and control monitoring |
| Integrations | Clinic workflow and compliance context | Cloud, HR, identity, security, and compliance integrations |
| Training evidence | Built into HIPAA operating workflow | Drata has HIPAA/security training materials and evidence workflows |
| Vendor BAAs | Built into PHIGuard compliance workflow | Verify third-party risk and BAA workflow fit |
| Incident handling | Clinic compliance incident workflow | Verify HIPAA breach-response workflow and evidence model |
| Pricing posture | Clinic-level public pricing | Plan structure with personalized pricing |

## Pricing and procurement

Drata publishes plan structure but not a simple clinic price list. Buyers should verify framework count, FTE assumptions, integrations, add-ons, user access review, third-party risk management, Trust Center scope, and contract terms.

PHIGuard publishes clinic-level pricing and makes BAA details available during plan review. That can be easier for small clinics because the buying question is operational scope, not framework portfolio complexity.

## Where Drata fits

Drata fits organizations that have a security or compliance owner and need to prove controls across systems. A digital health company, health-tech vendor, SaaS business associate, or larger organization preparing for customer due diligence may need that model. In those environments, HIPAA often sits next to SOC 2, ISO 27001, GDPR, vendor risk, access reviews, cloud configuration checks, and auditor collaboration.

That is not a criticism of Drata. It is the reason the category works. Compliance automation is valuable when the organization has many systems producing evidence and needs reusable control proof across frameworks. Drata's own HIPAA materials also avoid the false promise that software automatically makes an organization compliant, which is the right posture for a serious buyer.

## Where PHIGuard fits

PHIGuard fits when the buyer is a clinic administrator, not a security team. The everyday HIPAA questions are different: did the new hire finish training, who acknowledged the policy, what happened after the suspicious email, which vendor BAA is still pending, and who owns the risk-analysis remediation item?

Those are not primarily cloud-control questions. They are operating questions. PHIGuard keeps the owner, due date, status, decision, and evidence attached to the work, so the clinic is not rebuilding the story from email, spreadsheets, and meeting notes.

## Does Drata replace a clinic HIPAA program?

No software replaces the clinic's HIPAA obligations. Drata can help a technology-centric organization operationalize HIPAA control evidence, and PHIGuard can help a clinic keep HIPAA work assigned and documented. In both cases, the covered entity or business associate still has to maintain policies, safeguards, training, risk analysis, vendor contracts, and incident response.

For a traditional small clinic, the practical issue is usually not whether an AWS control was continuously monitored. It is whether the staff-facing HIPAA work stayed complete and defensible. That is why PHIGuard and Drata should not be treated as interchangeable.

## What to verify before buying

Before choosing either tool, document these answers:

1. Is the organization buying GRC automation or clinic HIPAA operations?
2. Which frameworks are in scope?
3. Which integrations and evidence sources are required?
4. Who owns risk remediation after issues are found?
5. How are incidents documented and exported?
6. Where are training, policies, vendor BAAs, and access reviews kept?
7. Does pricing match a clinic team or a security/compliance team?
8. Which evidence must be ready for OCR review, customer due diligence, internal audit, or management review?

## Bottom line

Drata is a strong option for technology companies and security teams that need compliance automation, framework evidence, integrations, and audit readiness. It should stay on the shortlist for that category.

PHIGuard is the sharper fit for small clinics that need HIPAA work to happen every week. For a covered entity with clinic staff, the core problem is often follow-through, not framework automation.
