## Short answer

PHIGuard and Secureframe both address HIPAA, but they solve different problems. Secureframe is a compliance automation platform for organizations that need evidence collection, continuous monitoring, risk management, policies, employee workflows, third-party risk, Trust Center work, and audit-readiness across frameworks. PHIGuard is a small-clinic HIPAA operating record.

Choose Secureframe if the buyer needs a broader compliance automation platform. Choose PHIGuard if the buyer is a clinic administrator who needs HIPAA work to stay assigned, documented, and reviewable week after week.

## Category fit

Secureframe's HIPAA page describes automating and streamlining HIPAA compliance, setting up policies, training employees, managing vendors and business associates, and continuously monitoring a technology stack. Its pricing page shows quote-based packages with features such as infrastructure monitoring, evidence collection, personnel management, risk management, policy management, 300+ integrations, automated evidence collection, continuous control monitoring, access to audit partners, third-party risk management, and user access review options.

That is a serious compliance automation model. It is most useful when many systems produce control evidence and someone owns security, compliance, vendor review, and framework reporting.

PHIGuard starts from a narrower operational problem. A small clinic may not have a cloud-control evidence program. It still has to document risk analysis follow-up, staff training, policy acknowledgement, incident response, vendor BAA status, access reviews, and recurring compliance tasks. PHIGuard keeps that work in one clinic-facing operating record.

## Feature comparison

| Evaluation point | PHIGuard | Secureframe |
|---|---|---|
| Primary fit | Small clinic HIPAA operations | Compliance automation and framework evidence |
| Framework scope | Focused HIPAA clinic workflows | HIPAA plus broader compliance automation and add-on frameworks |
| Evidence model | Evidence tied to clinic work, tasks, incidents, and decisions | Automated evidence collection, controls, tests, and monitoring |
| Integrations | Clinic workflow and compliance context | Large integration catalog and infrastructure monitoring |

| Vendor BAAs | Available in PHIGuard program workflows | Third-party risk and BAA evidence should be verified for the use case |
| Pricing posture | Clinic-level public pricing | Quote-based packages and feature tiers |
| Best buyer | Practice administrator or clinic owner | Security, compliance, IT, or business associate team |

## Pricing and procurement

Secureframe does not present a simple small-clinic price list. Its package page directs buyers to get a quote and separates features by package. A clinic should verify the selected framework, integrations, number of workspaces, third-party risk features, user access review features, support model, implementation work, and contract term.

PHIGuard publishes clinic-level pricing and includes a Business Associate Agreement on every public plan. That makes the buying model easier for small clinics that are not trying to negotiate a broader GRC platform.

## Where Secureframe fits

Secureframe fits organizations that need structured compliance automation. A health-tech vendor, SaaS business associate, digital health company, or larger organization may need HIPAA evidence alongside SOC 2, ISO 27001, vendor diligence, access reviews, Trust Center workflows, and automated monitoring.

In that situation, Secureframe's breadth is the point. The buyer likely has systems that can be connected, controls that can be tested, and customers or auditors asking for reusable evidence.

## Where PHIGuard fits

PHIGuard fits when the HIPAA problem is operational follow-through inside a clinic. The administrator needs to know whether training is complete, which vendor BAA is missing, who owns a risk-analysis finding, what happened after an incident, and where the audit history lives.

Those questions are not mainly infrastructure questions. They are staff and workflow questions. PHIGuard keeps the owner, due date, action, note, evidence, and audit history attached to the work so the clinic does not have to reconstruct the story from email, spreadsheets, and meetings.

## What to verify before buying

Before choosing either product, answer these questions:

1. Is the organization buying compliance automation or clinic HIPAA operations?
2. Which frameworks are in scope besides HIPAA?
3. Which systems must produce evidence automatically?
4. Who maintains policies, training, incidents, vendor BAAs, and risk follow-up?
5. How does the product handle BAA terms for PHI-related use?
6. What evidence can be exported for OCR inquiry, management review, customer due diligence, or internal audit?
7. Does the pricing model fit a small clinic or a security/compliance team?

## Bottom line

Secureframe is credible when the buyer needs compliance automation, integrations, framework evidence, risk management, policy workflows, and audit-readiness at a broader company level. It should stay on the shortlist for that category.

PHIGuard is the sharper choice when the buyer is a small clinic trying to run HIPAA every week. For that use case, focused workflow is not a limitation. It is the reason the work gets done and the proof stays together.
