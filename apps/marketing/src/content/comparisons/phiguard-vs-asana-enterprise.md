## Short answer

PHIGuard and Asana Enterprise+ solve different problems. Asana is a broad work-management platform that can support HIPAA-covered work only under Asana's HIPAA path. PHIGuard is a narrower HIPAA operations system for small clinics that need owners, due dates, incidents, vendor BAA evidence, training records, policy acknowledgement, and risk follow-up without designing that system from scratch.

If a clinic already runs on Asana and has an administrator who can maintain HIPAA settings, Asana Enterprise+ may be the right controlled collaboration environment. If the clinic's primary goal is to run HIPAA week after week, PHIGuard is more direct.

## BAA and plan path

Asana's HIPAA datasheet says customers must execute Asana's BAA if they are subject to HIPAA and intend to store PHI in their Asana domain. It also says HIPAA compliance must be enabled in an Asana instance and is available only with an Enterprise+ plan for the full organization.

That matters for small clinics. Buyers often search for "Asana Enterprise HIPAA" because Enterprise is the familiar plan label, but Asana's current HIPAA materials point to Enterprise+ for HIPAA activation. A clinic should not compare PHIGuard against a casual Asana workspace or a lower-tier plan. The real comparison is PHIGuard against an Enterprise+ Asana environment where a super admin has executed the BAA and HIPAA Use Requirements, waited for activation, reviewed app authorizations, and trained staff on PHI boundaries.

PHIGuard's buying motion is narrower. It is meant for clinics that need HIPAA operations rather than a general collaboration suite. The BAA, workflow expectations, and compliance use case are the point of the product, not an enterprise add-on to a broader project platform.

## Where Asana is strong

Asana is strong when the clinic needs broad work management across departments. It can coordinate projects, portfolios, teams, tasks, goals, and cross-functional work. In a HIPAA-enabled Enterprise+ domain, Asana also changes important defaults: private projects and teams, safer notification behavior, MFA, guest/API restrictions, and disabled or governed integrations.

For a healthcare organization with an IT owner, an Asana admin, and a clear PHI policy, those controls can be enough. The clinic still needs to define which projects can contain PHI, which fields are safe, which integrations are allowed, how long audit logs are retained, and who reviews changes.

## Where Asana becomes heavy for small clinics

Small clinics often do not need another blank workflow builder. They need a system that already knows the recurring HIPAA jobs:

- review vendor BAAs
- assign risk-analysis remediation
- document incident triage
- record training completion
- track policy acknowledgement
- preserve access-review evidence
- keep owner, due date, and status visible

Asana can be configured to track those items, but configuration is the burden. A task title can expose PHI. A comment can include patient context. An attachment can become a medical-record copy. A notification can reveal sensitive information. A third-party integration can create another business-associate relationship. That is a lot for a small practice to govern.

## Where PHIGuard is different

PHIGuard starts with the compliance-operating model instead of generic project management. It is designed around the evidence a clinic will need later: who owned the task, what changed, what decision was made, which vendor was reviewed, what incident was triaged, and what evidence supports the follow-up.

That narrower scope is useful. A clinic does not have to decide whether to make a "vendor BAA review" a project, a task, a custom field, a form, a comment thread, or a spreadsheet export. The workflow is part of the system.

## What to verify before buying

Choose Asana Enterprise+ when:

- the clinic already uses Asana as a major operating system
- there is a named admin for HIPAA settings
- Enterprise+ procurement is acceptable
- integrations and AI features can be reviewed and controlled
- the team needs broad project management beyond HIPAA

Choose PHIGuard when:

- the main need is HIPAA compliance follow-through
- staff need fewer places to put PHI
- vendor BAAs, incidents, training, policies, and risk work should live together
- the clinic wants compliance evidence without building an Asana operating model
- the buyer is a practice administrator, not an enterprise collaboration team

## Bottom line

Asana Enterprise+ can support HIPAA-covered work for organizations prepared to buy, configure, govern, and monitor it correctly. It is a credible option when Asana is already central to the clinic's work.

PHIGuard is the better fit when the clinic is not trying to standardize on a broad PM suite and simply needs HIPAA work to stay assigned, reviewed, documented, and audit-ready. For small clinics, less configurability can be the advantage.
