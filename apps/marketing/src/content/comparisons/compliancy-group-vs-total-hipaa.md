## Short answer

Compliancy Group and Total HIPAA both help clinics build a HIPAA compliance program, but they emphasize different buying paths. Compliancy Group is a broader compliance platform with tiered plans, training, policy, risk, vendor, hotline, and support features. Total HIPAA publishes compliance packages with training, documentation, risk assessments, BAAs, incident and breach materials, and consultation.

Choose Compliancy Group when the clinic wants a platform plan with layered compliance coverage. Choose Total HIPAA when the clinic wants a more package-and-consultation-oriented HIPAA program. Choose PHIGuard when the missing piece is daily HIPAA follow-through and evidence.

## Category fit

Compliancy Group's public pricing page frames the product as a healthcare compliance platform with plan tiers. Depending on tier, public materials include employee training, policy manuals, attestation, risk assessment, vendor and BAA management, hotline reporting, resource libraries, and broader compliance content.

Total HIPAA's public materials emphasize HIPAA compliance services and software for covered entities, business associates, health plans, agents, and organizations handling health information. Its pricing page describes compliance packages, training, documentation, risk assessments, BAAs, incident and breach notification materials, and consultation-oriented help.

## Feature comparison

| Evaluation point | Compliancy Group | Total HIPAA |
|---|---|---|
| Primary fit | Layered healthcare compliance platform | HIPAA compliance packages, training, documentation, and consultation |
| Pricing posture | Tiered plans with employee and plan variables | Published package/pricing information with consultation-oriented scope |
| Training | Training libraries and employee modules | Online HIPAA training positioned as a core offering |
| Policy work | Policy manuals, templates, and attestation tooling | Compliance documentation and policy/procedure package materials |
| Risk analysis | Risk assessments and broader assessment library by plan | Risk assessments included in package positioning |
| Vendor BAAs | Vendor and BAA management in broader plans | BAA materials and package language; verify workflow depth |
| Incident materials | Hotline/reporting and incident features by plan or add-on | Incident and breach notification materials in package language |
| Best buyer | Buyer who wants platform breadth and ongoing plan structure | Buyer who wants HIPAA package scope and consultation |

## Pricing and procurement

Both vendors now publish pricing or package information, so buyers should avoid stale assumptions that everything requires a quote. Compliancy Group's plan structure depends on tier, employee count, locations, administrators, and feature needs. Total HIPAA's package structure depends on the buyer type, compliance scope, consultation needs, and selected services.

The right procurement question is not only "which starting price is lower?" It is "what annual package actually covers this clinic's work?" Ask about training seats, covered locations, policy updates, risk assessment cadence, BAA support, incident materials, consultation hours, renewal terms, and evidence exports.

## Training and documentation

Total HIPAA is especially relevant when training, documentation, and guided package scope are central to the purchase. Its public materials emphasize online training, customized HIPAA compliance plans, documentation, BAAs, incident and breach notification rules, dedicated support/review, and packages for covered entities and business associates.

Compliancy Group is especially relevant when the clinic wants a broader platform model with employee portals, training libraries, policy management, risk assessments, vendor management, hotline reporting, and compliance resources under a tiered plan.

## The follow-through question

HIPAA administrative safeguards include risk analysis and risk management expectations. That means a clinic has to move beyond training completion and policy folders into tracked action: which risks were found, what decisions were made, who owns remediation, what evidence supports the work, and how incidents or vendor reviews are documented.

Compliancy Group and Total HIPAA can both support compliance-program documentation. The clinic should still verify where day-to-day follow-through happens and whether the evidence is preserved with owners, dates, and history.

## Where PHIGuard fits

PHIGuard fits when a clinic wants the compliance program and operating record connected. It is built for recurring HIPAA tasks, incidents, vendor BAA reviews, training evidence, policies, risk follow-up, and audit history in one workflow.

That does not make Compliancy Group or Total HIPAA bad options. It clarifies the buying question. If the clinic needs a package, choose a package. If it needs the work to stay assigned and provable, choose an operating layer.

## What to verify before buying

Before choosing either vendor, document these answers:

1. Which plan or package covers the clinic's exact covered-entity or business-associate use case?
2. How do employees, locations, consultation, training seats, and add-ons affect annual cost?
3. Does the vendor sign the right BAA, and what services are covered?
4. How are risk-analysis findings converted into assigned remediation work?
5. Where are incident notes and breach-analysis materials stored and exported?
6. How are vendor BAAs tracked, renewed, and linked to systems that receive PHI?
7. Where will daily HIPAA follow-through live after training and documentation are complete?

## Bottom line

Choose Compliancy Group when the clinic wants a broader tiered compliance platform. Choose Total HIPAA when the clinic wants HIPAA packages, training, documentation, risk assessments, and consultation.

Choose PHIGuard when the clinic needs the operational proof layer. HIPAA work has to be more than a training record or policy folder; it has to remain assigned, reviewed, and defensible during normal clinic operations.
