## Short answer

PHIGuard and Total HIPAA are both credible options for small organizations, but they represent different operating models. Total HIPAA is service-led: expert guidance, customized documentation, training, risk assessment support, and audit or breach support are central to the offer. PHIGuard is software-first: the clinic owns the recurring HIPAA work inside one operating record.

Choose Total HIPAA if the clinic wants a compliance partner to guide and review the program. Choose PHIGuard if the clinic wants staff to run HIPAA tasks, incidents, policies, training evidence, vendor BAAs, and risk follow-up internally with clear accountability.

## Category fit

Total HIPAA's public site says it provides HIPAA compliance services and software for covered entities, health plans, health insurance agents, business associates, medical providers, and dental providers. Its homepage emphasizes expert guidance, customized policies and documents, a software platform, training, risk assessments, and unlimited audit and breach support.

Its pricing page describes HIPAA DIY, HIPAA Prime, and HIPAA Concierge. HIPAA Prime is positioned with training, an online risk assessment, a customized compliance plan, expert review, customer support, automatic plan updates, and unlimited audit and breach support. HIPAA Concierge is positioned for larger or more complex organizations with a senior-level project manager, vendor management, and tailored monitoring.

PHIGuard is different. It is not a consultant-led subscription. It gives a clinic one workspace for assigned compliance tasks, incidents, vendor BAA status, policy and training evidence, risk follow-up, and append-only audit history.

## Pricing and procurement

Total HIPAA publishes package information. At the time of verification, its pricing page lists HIPAA Prime starting at $199 per month with a $999 onboarding fee and annual billing language, and HIPAA Concierge starting at $485 per month. Buyers should verify current pricing, onboarding fees, included users or locations, support scope, document customization, risk assessment review, breach support terms, and renewal assumptions.

PHIGuard publishes clinic-level pricing and includes a Business Associate Agreement on every public plan. The buying question is how much operating structure the clinic needs, not how much outside service support it wants.

## Where Total HIPAA fits

Total HIPAA fits clinics and healthcare-adjacent organizations that want a human support layer. If the buyer wants experts to review the risk assessment, customize documents, answer implementation questions, keep the compliance plan updated, and stand beside the organization during an audit or breach response, Total HIPAA's model is aligned with that need.

That is a valid reason to choose Total HIPAA. Some clinics do not want to build internal compliance confidence first. They want guided service, documentation help, and support.

## Where PHIGuard fits

PHIGuard fits when the clinic wants to run the work itself and keep the proof where the work happens. A clinic administrator needs to know who completed training, which policy was acknowledged, what happened after an incident, which vendor BAA is missing, and whether a risk-analysis item was closed.

Those questions do not stop after onboarding. They recur every week. PHIGuard is built around recurring ownership and audit history so the clinic can keep moving without turning every compliance question into a service request.

## What to verify before buying

Before choosing either product, document:

1. Whether the clinic wants internal ownership, outside guidance, or both.
2. Which training, policy, risk assessment, vendor BAA, and incident workflows must be maintained after setup.
3. Whether audit and breach support is required as a service commitment.
4. What current package pricing includes and excludes.
5. How many locations, entities, users, or business lines are in scope.
6. How evidence exports for OCR inquiry, management review, legal review, or internal audit.
7. Who owns weekly follow-through after the initial compliance plan is created.

## Bottom line

Total HIPAA is a strong fit when the buyer values service, expert guidance, customized documentation, risk assessment support, and audit or breach help. That model can reduce uncertainty for clinics that want a compliance partner.

PHIGuard is the sharper fit when the clinic wants a software-first operating record. For recurring HIPAA work, the advantage is simple: the owner, status, evidence, and audit history stay attached to the task instead of drifting across documents, emails, and outside support threads.
