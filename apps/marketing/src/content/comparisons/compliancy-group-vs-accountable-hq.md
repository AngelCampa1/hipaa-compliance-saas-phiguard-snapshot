## Short answer

Compliancy Group and Accountable HQ are both serious HIPAA compliance software options, but they appeal to different buyers. Compliancy Group is stronger when the clinic wants a guided healthcare compliance package with layered plans, training libraries, policy tools, risk assessment, vendor management, hotline reporting, and support. Accountable HQ is stronger when the clinic wants transparent HIPAA software tiers with included employee counts and a more software-led compliance workflow.

For a small clinic, the best choice depends on whether the problem is compliance-program guidance or daily follow-through. If the clinic already knows what needs to happen but keeps losing ownership and evidence, PHIGuard should be evaluated as the operating layer.

## Category fit

Compliancy Group's public pricing page shows plans such as Foundation, Growth, Advanced, and Elite. The plan structure includes variables like users, locations, employee-based pricing, training libraries, policy manuals, risk assessment, vendor and BAA management, hotline reporting, and broader healthcare compliance resources. This is a compliance package model.

Accountable's public pricing page shows HIPAA compliance software tiers such as Basic HIPAA, Plus, and Pro. It publishes included employee counts, extra-seat pricing, annual totals, a trial path, and a comparison against consultant and law-firm cost ranges. This is a transparent software-plan model.

## Feature comparison

| Pricing posture | Tier, employee, location, and add-on variables | Published plan pricing with included employees and extra seats |

| Incident handling | Hotline/reporting and incident features by plan or add-on | Incident response listed in plan materials |
| Best buyer | Buyer who wants program breadth and support | Buyer who wants transparent HIPAA software tiering |

## Pricing and procurement

Both vendors now publish enough pricing structure that old "must request quote" comparisons are too stale. Compliancy Group publishes starting prices and employee-based pricing by tier. Accountable publishes plan prices, annual billing totals, included employees, and extra-seat costs.

The clinic should compare total annual cost under its real usage: number of employees, locations, administrators, plan tier, vendor-management needs, incident workflow requirements, support expectations, and any add-ons. A low starting price is not the same as the right annual total.

## Coaching, support, and self-service

Compliancy Group's value proposition is broader program guidance and healthcare compliance breadth. That can be useful when a clinic wants help interpreting requirements, building policies, training staff, and maintaining a compliance calendar across multiple obligations.

Accountable's value proposition is a clearer software-led HIPAA workflow with public tiering. It may fit teams that want to work through compliance steps inside the product and use support when needed, especially when published pricing makes procurement easier. Buyers should distinguish dedicated compliance support from a branded coaching model, then decide which kind of guidance they actually need.

## Where PHIGuard fits

PHIGuard should enter the comparison when the clinic's problem is operational evidence. HIPAA work often breaks after the assessment: remediation tasks do not get assigned, vendor reviews drift, incidents stay in inboxes, access reviews are forgotten, and policy acknowledgements become hard to prove.

PHIGuard is built around that recurring operating record. It gives the clinic one place for task ownership, incident notes, vendor BAA reviews, training evidence, policy acknowledgement, risk follow-up, and audit history.

## What to verify before buying

Before choosing either vendor, document these answers:

1. What is the annual cost for the clinic's actual employee count, locations, and support needs?
2. Which plan includes vendor BAA management, incident workflows, risk assessment, and training depth?
3. Does the BAA cover the clinic's intended PHI workflows?
4. Who owns follow-up after the risk analysis?
5. Can the clinic export incident, vendor, training, policy, and task evidence without reconstruction?
6. How are access reviews and staff changes documented?
7. Where will day-to-day HIPAA work live if the platform is primarily a compliance program layer?

## Bottom line

Choose Compliancy Group when the clinic values a broader guided compliance package and plan-based healthcare compliance coverage. Choose Accountable HQ when published HIPAA software pricing, transparent included users, and a software-led workflow are more important.

Choose PHIGuard when daily follow-through is the deciding factor. The best compliance platform for a clinic is the one that still has usable evidence after six months of normal work.
