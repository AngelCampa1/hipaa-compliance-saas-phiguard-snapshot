## Short answer

Thoropass and PHIGuard both support HIPAA compliance work, but they are built around different buying motions. Thoropass combines compliance software, automation, expert guidance, and third-party assessment. PHIGuard gives small clinics one operating record for recurring HIPAA work.

Choose Thoropass if the organization wants a broader compliance partner and an assessment or attestation path. Choose PHIGuard if the clinic wants its own staff-facing system for tasks, incidents, training evidence, vendor BAAs, policies, and risk follow-up.

## Category fit

Thoropass publicly positions its HIPAA offering around rapid, continuous compliance, automation, expert guidance, native integrations, policy templates, clear action items, guided workflows, risk analysis, document management, employee training tracking, and third-party attestation. Its HIPAA page also describes sharing a trusted compliance report with customers and prospects.

That is valuable for companies that need to prove compliance to external buyers, partners, or auditors. It is especially relevant when HIPAA sits beside broader security and trust work.

PHIGuard is narrower. It is not trying to be a third-party attestation partner or a broad audit firm. It is built for small clinics that need the HIPAA program to become day-to-day work: assigned owners, due dates, decisions, evidence, incidents, vendor BAA records, and an append-only audit history.

## Pricing and buying process

Thoropass does not present a simple small-clinic price list on the HIPAA page. Buyers should verify current pricing, included frameworks, assessment scope, expert support, integrations, training, policy templates, risk workflows, report deliverables, renewal costs, and whether the implementation model fits a clinic team.

PHIGuard publishes clinic-level pricing and includes a Business Associate Agreement on every public plan. That makes the comparison more direct for clinics that want predictable internal execution rather than a broader guided compliance engagement.

## Where Thoropass fits

Thoropass fits when a company needs more than a workflow tool. A digital health vendor, software business associate, or healthcare-adjacent company may need a guided roadmap, risk analysis, document management, employee training tracking, integrations, and an external assessment to satisfy prospects or partners.

In that context, the attestation layer matters. If customers are asking for a report that can be shared externally, PHIGuard is not trying to replace that motion.

## Where PHIGuard fits

PHIGuard fits when the buyer is trying to make HIPAA work happen inside the clinic. The key questions are operational: who completed training, who owns the overdue risk item, which vendor BAA is missing, what happened during an incident, and where is the record of follow-up?

Those questions need a simple place where clinic staff can complete work and where the administrator can review the history. PHIGuard keeps the work and the proof together instead of treating evidence as a separate project after the fact.

## What to verify before buying

Before choosing either product, document:

1. Whether the clinic needs internal HIPAA operations, third-party attestation, or both.
2. Whether customers or partners expect a report from an outside assessor.
3. Which integrations are required and who will maintain them.
4. How training, policies, incidents, vendor BAAs, and risk follow-up connect.
5. What the pricing includes beyond the initial compliance push.
6. How evidence exports for OCR inquiry, internal audit, buyer diligence, or management review.
7. Whether the clinic has staff to maintain a broader compliance platform after onboarding.

## Bottom line

Thoropass is credible when the buyer wants compliance automation plus expert guidance and an attestation path. It can be the better answer for health-tech companies, business associates, and organizations selling into healthcare.

PHIGuard is the better answer when the buyer is a small clinic trying to run HIPAA every week. It keeps the recurring work, ownership, evidence, and audit history in one place so the clinic can prove what happened without rebuilding the story later.
